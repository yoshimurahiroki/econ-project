| Question | Required observations | Representation and defining quantity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How is a population distributed? | Individual values, frequencies or saved interval counts | Histogram height is count, proportion or density as declared. Density equals proportion divided by interval width, so area represents population share. An empirical cumulative curve sums observed frequencies at their values. |

### Example: draw an already calculated group share

Fictional inputs: district North has 40 participants among 100 eligible households; South has 30 among 50 in 2024. The producing calculation saves 40.0 and 60.0 percent. Draw these saved values on a common 0–100 percent axis. Label both bars with their percentage and give both denominators in the caption. South's 30 participants form 60 percent of its 50 eligible households; North's 40 participants form 40 percent of its 100. The existing display producer supplies the output path and format.

```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Draw the percentages saved by the producing analysis.
districts = ["North", "South"]
participation_pct = [40.0, 60.0]
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5.0, 3.2), layout="constrained")
bars = ax.bar(districts, participation_pct, width=0.6)
ax.bar_label(bars, fmt="%.0f%%", padding=3, fontsize=11)
ax.set_ylabel("Eligible households participating (%)", fontsize=11)
ax.set_ylim(0, 100)
ax.set_yticks([0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100])
ax.tick_params(labelsize=11)
ax.set_title("Participation by district, 2024", fontsize=12)
```

Expected output: the two bars start at zero and end at 40 and 60 percent. The caption identifies the 100-household and 50-household denominators, the 2024 period and the fictional inputs.
