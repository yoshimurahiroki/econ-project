# Knowledge retrieval procedures

Select the section required by the current lookup through [econ-knowledge](../SKILL.md). Read that section and its declared prerequisites completely.

## Domains and search

The [retrieval program](../scripts/knowledge.py) provides `domains`, bounded `search`, and `read` commands. Select the relevant domain from `domains`, compare the candidate's use, inputs and outputs with the request, and read the selected IDs. `search --domain DOMAIN --query TEXT --limit 8` returns at most eight ranked candidates in that domain. When independent search questions share the domain and limit, repeat `--query TEXT` in one invocation. Each list in `searches` keeps its own ranking and matches; the index-read report is shared. Omitting `--domain` searches the available domain indexes under one shared limit. Use that cross-domain search when the question spans mechanisms or the relevant field is itself being selected; refine the question's economic objects through the returned domain and unit descriptions. A merger question can require demand, ownership, equilibrium, consumer welfare and business valuation. Search those objects together or through the corresponding focused queries, then select the complete chain the requested result uses. Exact IDs take priority, followed by matches in the indexed title, domain, topic, purpose, inputs and outputs through normalized words, bilingual aliases and Japanese character fragments. The lead makes the economic selection from the candidates' meanings and the adopted assumptions.

Search weights words by their specificity across the selected indexes, normalizes English plurals, and counts each matched concept once. An exact model contract ID prioritizes its indexed member units; use `plan MC-ID` to obtain the contract's complete model and equation closure. Inspect `matched_concepts` and their definition owners to distinguish meanings of a shared term. `concepts --query TEXT` accepts a full question and returns the matching labels with each distinct concept. Compare the definition and economic object before selecting a meaning. If a domain-restricted search returns no applicable candidate, repeat the same question without `--domain` and inspect the returned definition owners before classifying a knowledge gap. Numerical methods can be owned by the substantive field that uses them; Bayesian posterior simulation is under econometrics.

## Concepts and classifications

Both `search` and `concepts` accept registered JEL codes in the query. `I32` selects poverty measurement and analysis, `C2` selects mapped codes under that group, and `JEL Q` selects the mapped environmental, resource and agricultural fields. Exact knowledge IDs rank first, followed by JEL matches and the query's economic concepts. Inspect `matched_jel_codes` together with the unit's purpose and model conditions; a classification match identifies a field, while the body establishes its substantive coverage.

Concept `mappings` retain versioned STW correspondences from the local concept to the external URI. `exactMatch` records equivalent concepts, `closeMatch` a close correspondence, `broadMatch` a broader external concept, `narrowMatch` a narrower external concept, and `relatedMatch` an associative link. Establish each mapping from the source's labels, scope and the local definition. Keep distinct meanings of matching, CV, BIC and Hotelling under their own definition IDs. The local bilingual labels supply retrieval terms; the typed unit relations supply theoretical and computational prerequisites.

The mappings reference ZBW — Leibniz Information Centre for Economics, [STW Thesaurus for Economics](https://www.zbw.eu/stw/version/latest/download/about.en.html), under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Each mapping records its source version; the local Japanese labels and mapping judgments are authored here. The [STW–JEL crosswalk](https://www.zbw.eu/stw/version/latest/mapping/jel/about.en.html) is published under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and records its target JEL edition as `20210225` (2021-02-25). Interpret its category links against that edition. Obtain current JEL codes and English labels from the [AEA classification tree](https://www.aeaweb.org/econlit/classificationTree.xml). For example, the crosswalk labels `P16` as “Political Economy”; the current AEA tree labels it “Capitalist Institutions • Welfare State”. Use each unit's JEL codes to locate its economic field and its full body to establish model coverage. Preserve local definitions when an external label groups distinct methods or objects, such as resampling and cross-validation, or a consumer price index and a constant-utility cost-of-living index.

## Read selected units

Run `read ID... --limit 16000` to retrieve those IDs and their topologically ordered required prerequisites in bounded body pages. Follow `next_offset` with `--offset VALUE --expected-sha256 HASH`, using the first page's `content_sha256` and the same IDs and limit; a changed stream rejects the continuation and starts a new read at zero. Offsets count Unicode characters in the complete body stream. Read through the final page before applying the selected bodies. Omitting `--limit` retains the complete structured JSON interface for existing programs and source-catalog export. Task-bound pages add `--restore` so page retrieval preserves the saved cache. Related and alternative IDs support selection and enter the read only when the operation needs them. Verify each returned body's definition, assumptions and implementation conditions against the requested use. Additional technical dependencies discovered during the work trigger the corresponding additional ID read.

For a version 2 collection, use `concepts --query TEXT` to inspect bilingual names, aliases, broader and narrower concepts, definitions and JEL mappings. Use `relations ID...` to inspect typed incoming and outgoing relations; `--type TYPE` restricts the relation and repeated `--context KEY=VALUE` supplies an adopted applicability selector. Use `plan ID...` with the same optional context to identify understanding prerequisites, applicable implementation dependencies, required assumption definitions, unresolved conditions and model contracts. A satisfied `requires_assumption` relation adds its concept's definition unit and that unit's prerequisites; its `definition` locator identifies the complete body and snapshot availability. For example, `plan STRUCT-06 --read` includes NUM-04 for its required equilibrium-selection assumption. The plan also returns `inapplicable_relations` with the authored conditions that conflict with the supplied context. A selector reports whether the supplied value matches a registered condition; establish the condition from the actual model and evidence before relying on it. Use related and alternative relations to compare candidates under their stated target, identifying observations and conditions.

For an online experiment, select CAUSAL-02. `experiment_inference=posterior` adds its Bayesian decision prerequisites; `experiment_inference=frequentist_sequential` adds the testing definitions. `assignment_policy=adaptive` adds INFO-02, and `decision_horizon=dynamic` adds its Bellman prerequisites. Supply only selectors established by the adopted experiment. The bodies distinguish assignment effects, sampling/stopping inference and exploration value before the existing design and data owners choose an implementation.

Use `plan ID... --context KEY=VALUE` to resolve the required IDs, then pass its `required_ids` to bounded `read` when the planned bodies exceed the tool budget. `plan ... --read` retains the combined full-output interface for a small closure or a programmatic consumer. It returns the plan and each resolved required unit once, in dependency order, including its full body and version. Unresolved conditions remain in the plan and do not add dependencies. An application cycle or required body outside the snapshot returns the plan with `read_blocked`, no bodies and a nonzero exit. Resolve the cycle or expand the snapshot before reading. The full structured read's report counts distinct knowledge files and bytes read in this invocation. Standard output also contains metadata and JSON formatting; body bytes are the UTF-8 lengths of the returned unit texts. A selected snapshot permits bodies within its declared closure; obtain an expanded snapshot through the existing exporter when the needed body lies outside it. Use the graph and contract views to locate the complete bodies, interfaces and conditions that support the specialist's economic judgment.

The source and deployed script resolve their own reference directory. A flat snapshot uses `--base knowledge/references` before the subcommand. A snapshot selected with the existing exporter's `--knowledge-id ID` contains that selection and its prerequisite closure; use a matching expanded snapshot when the operation requires another ID. In that snapshot, `domains` lists only fields represented by the selected closure. Direct file readers use `selection.json` to restrict the canonical domain listing and indexes before selecting a body.

For direct reads, use this reference directory's `domains.json`, the selected domain index and the required ID files under `units/`. The domain index supplies each ID's prerequisites and path. Read only that closure. Keep the manifest and runtime marker available for identity; their administrative text need not enter the model context. The linked `../scripts/knowledge.py` resolves this same directory in repositories and installed plugins.

## Model contracts and execution

The plan returns contracts linked to any required unit, including a unit that shares a contract whose definition belongs elsewhere. Contract and equation locators report which bodies are readable in the current snapshot. Read the contract's definition and required equation bodies before adopting its model; expand the snapshot when their locators fall outside the selection. Related contracts do not add their units to the understanding-prerequisite closure.

`plan` also accepts model contract IDs. For example, `plan MC-hark-consumption-saving --context calculation_target=market_equilibrium` starts from that contract's units and its selection, assumptions, identification, equations, numerical diagnostics and example locators, then follows the applicable prerequisite relations. The market-equilibrium condition returns the related household-distribution and market-clearing unit; select it with `plan MC-hark-consumption-saving HET-02 --context calculation_target=market_equilibrium --read`. `calculation_target=household_policy` leaves that condition in `inapplicable_relations`. Compare the actual actors, information, choice timing, feasible actions and target before supplying either value. A contract ID locates its model; its name supplies no economic adoption decision.

Contracts expose `availability`, derived from their existing implementations and example locators: `theory_reference` identifies mathematical bodies, `numerical_example` a worked numerical calculation, `internal_runner` a registered econ-data binding with its public command, `internal_function` a registered callable, and `external_official_api` a pinned official module. A contract can expose several uses. The selection and numerical-diagnostic locators point to the owning chapter text and carry its body hash. Static availability describes those interfaces; installation, optimization, simulation and saved results use their actual execution records. A pinned external source can carry both its package `version` and 40-character `revision`, or the revision and its `source_url` when the selected interface is distributed at that commit.

Pass a selected contract as `model_contract_id` to the existing `research_causal.py select` command. The catalog's `selection_binding` connects that contract to the existing structural-model branch; the selector applies its target, economic-condition and numerical-method restrictions and verifies the selected runner or official function against the contract's implementation. The result includes the complete contract, knowledge plan, checks and saved-result provenance. `selection_status="theory_only"` supplies equations; `"implementation_only"` supplies its authored callable or command without a selector binding. Both return `status="implementation_required"` from selection and execute no calculation. A bound selection returns the existing owner's execution specification when its input schema is supported. Preserve the original adopted specification and adopt any reported changes before execution.

Contract inputs, outputs, assumptions and equation locators define the mathematical calculation. A contract with an empty `implementation` list supplies that mathematical interface; pass its full equations to econ-data for the requested derivation or calculation. Its related link identifies the model to read. Execution and validation follow the actual implementation and returned result.

Contract implementation descriptors identify the existing econ-data owner and, when registered, its resource ID. An `execution_binding` identifies a registered engine or pinned external module; an `executable_example_provenance` identifies source-only example code. A `canonical_source` path, source URL and SHA-256 address the exact source provenance. Retrieve source-only examples through that source URL and resolve registered bindings through the econ-data resource closure. Execute a selected binding through its documented dependencies. Retain the actual inputs, fitted specification, outputs and requested verification in the producing research record.

Use a method comparison on its stated economic problem, population and quantity. Check the competing methods' identifying observations, maintained restrictions, inference and computational requirements before selecting one. The body's investigation path identifies the unresolved object, specialist search terms, original material or official interface needed, the observation that changes the adopted method, and the resulting calculation. Pass that concrete source question to econ-literature under DECIDE; econ-design adopts the resulting model or estimand and econ-data executes its selected engine. Preserve the existing result owner and resume its requested outcome after the lookup.

## External domain sources

Use the host's available skill catalog or knowledge tools to discover the source for the pending question. On a host exposing `skills.list` and `skills.read`, list the applicable authority, select the returned package and resource, and read the selected resource through its returned cursor until complete. Use the actual tool schema and source locators. For a filesystem skill, use its declared path and existing file reader. Read the needed resource and its required prerequisites; source discovery does not require reading every skill body.

Retain the acquired source through `knowledge.py acquire --task TASK --root PROJECT --source FILE`. The input JSON contains `provider`, `knowledge_id`, `version`, `title`, `domain`, `applicability` as a nonempty array, and `retrieval` with the actual `tool` and `arguments`. Supply the exact acquired `body` and `locator`. For a complete single-page `skills.read` return, put its returned object in `retrieval.result`; acquisition takes `contents` and `resource` directly from that result. For a paginated resource, join its original pages into `body` in returned order and retain their cursor arguments in `retrieval`; a final page alone does not supply the preceding text. Use a source's actual authored version or the exact content identity exposed by its provider. Optional `prerequisites` identifies needed local or previously acquired external IDs; optional `model_contract_ids` joins existing local model contracts. `inputs` and `outputs` describe its research use. Keep adopted project definitions and choices with their existing specification and decision owners.

Acquisition saves the original source once as an immutable existing task output and updates a small `knowledge:external:PROVIDER:ID` pointer. Identical acquisition reuses those outputs. Its return includes a current `reference` and an immutable `saved_reference`, both in the existing argument reference format. The current reference lets `project-impact --references` identify a changed acquisition; the saved reference locates the exact original used by an earlier judgment. Retain the actual prerequisite outputs before the selected source. Source updates request the reference's existing typed action; they do not adopt a changed scientific specification or execute its calculations.

Use `search --task TASK --root PROJECT --query TEXT` to rank existing external-source metadata with local knowledge in the same bounded result. Use `plan ID... --task TASK --root PROJECT --read` for their selected prerequisite bodies and existing model contracts. An absent acquired prerequisite appears as an unreadable locator; obtain that source through the host before the dependent read. Full external bodies remain in the existing task outputs, outside the distributed ERS collection.

Use `read ID... --task TASK --root PROJECT --restore` to restore selected bodies after resumption. `--as-sources` returns those exact texts, hashes, locations, versions and references in `argument.source_catalog` form for the existing argument/source catalog. Bind the returned `references` for every required external source to the argument or adopted decision that uses them, including prerequisite sources, and retain immutable references with their source correspondence. Bind required local IDs through the existing `knowledge` references. This makes a changed prerequisite visible even when its consumer's text stays the same. Reuse the existing source catalog when it already contains that version. Locate earlier immutable versions through the task's recorded output IDs. External source text provides evidence and domain definitions; the original request and adopted authority govern its use.

## Versioned reuse and continuation

Retrieve for the pending question, method or result interpretation, using known IDs directly. Carry its complete selected bodies, model contracts and true prerequisites to that decision. A stage transition alone requires no new search: reuse matching bodies still in context, refresh changed dependencies, and restore originals after actual context loss.

`read ID... --task TASK --root PROJECT` uses the [existing task state](../../../harness/state.md) and its packaged state helper. Activate `requested_local_state_helper_operation` for task-bound acquisition, searches, plans or reads; the state helper and its rules then enter the reading set. Operations without `--task` do not use that helper. Caching requires a running task and saves local bodies as existing object outputs named `knowledge:ID`, with body, catalog, manifest and retrieval-specification versions. A matching saved body is checked and omitted from the repeated result; the return identifies its saved output. A changed version supplies the current body. Reuse a body already available at that version. `--restore` and `--as-sources` read saved bodies without changing the task and also work after task completion.

Restore the original request, adopted choices and every applicable mandatory rule through the existing governing context before continuing. Preserve valid calculations and results. A missing file, stale generation, invalid prerequisite or cyclic dependency requires repair through its canonical owner before the affected use.

## Canonical content and distribution

Source editing uses the authored catalog and chapters with the generated indexes, bodies and manifest. A runtime export contains the canonical manifest, the applicable small domain indexes and complete selected ID bodies, plus `runtime.json` bound to that source generation. The exporter reads the saved manifest and selected prerequisite closure. Runtime reads verify the marker, index and unit hashes and the declared selection; source reads verify the consumed catalog and chapters. Preserve this explicit layout identity when transferring a collection.

The authored catalog and chapter files are the content source. After editing a chapter or its per-ID metadata, run the retrieval program's `build` command once to update the affected generated bodies, indexes and version manifest. Propagate the saved generation through the existing export and synchronization owners. Generated unit bodies project relative file links to canonical source URLs; authored chapters keep their local source links and execution retains its registered owner.

The knowledge collection is packaged once under this reference Skill. The other five skills refer to this owner and retain their execution instructions and package interfaces. Knowledge content supplies economics; the runtime supplies authority, state, verification and completion.
