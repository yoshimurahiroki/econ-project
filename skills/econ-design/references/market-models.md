# Executable market models

The market runner accepts JSON containing `model`, a complete `baseline`, a complete `scenario` (or null when the selected engine owns its counterfactual), `unit`, and `sources`. Sources identify the input with a description, path, or URL. Objects reject unknown fields. Run `python skills/econ-data/scripts/research_markets.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json`. The caller provides an existing output parent. Outputs contain model, engine, adopted inputs, options, finite named statistics with units, scenario results, and the specification hash. Existing research DAG commands can invoke this CLI with an argument array.

## Differentiated demand and supply

For consumer i and product j in market t let utility be `delta_jt + mu_ijt + epsilon_ijt`, outside utility be epsilon_0, and `delta = X1 beta + xi`. Heterogeneity is `mu = X2 Sigma nu` with integration nodes and weights. Independent extreme-value errors give conditional logit probabilities; integrating them gives shares. Nested logit adds within-group correlation rho. Recover delta with `delta <- delta + log(s_observed) - log(s_model)` until simulated and observed shares agree. Excluded instruments Z satisfy `E[Z'xi]=0`; for endogenous characteristics, include distinct excluded instruments with variation that shifts those characteristics and is independent of xi. Supply instruments satisfy the analogous cost-error restriction. GMM minimizes the quadratic form of sample moments using the supplied weighting and nonlinear optimization settings. Identification of each endogenous characteristic, heterogeneity parameter and nesting correlation requires independent moment variation; choose instruments and integration using the research design.

For single-product prices, own and cross derivatives form D, with `D_jk = ds_j/dp_k`. The elasticity is `D_jk p_k / s_j`; diversion from j to k is `-D_kj / D_jj`. Multi-product firm f chooses prices to maximize `sum_{j in f} (p_j-c_j)s_j`; the ownership-adjusted derivative matrix gives the first-order markup vector and marginal cost. With price-affine quasilinear utility `v_ij - alpha_i p_j`, each agent's `alpha_i > 0` is common across products. Divide each agent's expected log sum by that agent's alpha, then integrate. Multiply shares, per-consumer profit and consumer surplus by market population when reporting aggregate amounts. With fixed production costs, producer surplus is profit plus fixed costs; social surplus is consumer surplus plus producer surplus plus government revenue. An ownership change changes the first-order conditions. Changes to marginal costs, product attributes, utility, product availability or policy alter the equilibrium and welfare; retain product market size and common currency units in the input interpretation.

`model: blp` accepts a baseline object with exactly `products`, `linear`, `nonlinear`, `supply`, `integration`, `solve`, `counterfactual`, `optimal_instruments`, and `add_exogenous`. Product records require market_ids, product_ids, firm_ids, positive prices and shares, positive outside share in each market, and excluded demand_instruments fields. Ownership is binary and is determined by firm_ids in both the baseline and counterfactual. The runner rejects ownership and numbered ownership columns in products or entry_products before estimation; these PyBLP fields take precedence over firm_ids. Use the direct PyBLP ownership-matrix interface for an adopted partial-ownership model. Add nesting_ids for nesting and supply_instruments fields for endogenous supply. Set `add_exogenous` to false when a characteristic is endogenous, and provide all exogenous regressors, the intercept and excluded instruments explicitly in the demand_instruments columns; their projected design must identify the parameters. Formulation strings are PyBLP formulas. `nonlinear`, `supply`, `integration`, and `counterfactual` accept null when absent; integration has method, size and options. `solve` uses PyBLP Problem.solve arguments; optimization and iteration each have method and options. The runner uses `Problem`, `Formulation`, `Integration`, `Optimization`, `Iteration` and `ProblemResults` methods for estimated coefficients, shares, costs, elasticities, diversion, markups, consumer surplus, and optional approximate optimal instruments. A counterfactual contains `keep_product_ids`, `firm_ids`, `costs` and `utility_shifts`, with one value per retained observation in original order. `Simulation.replace_endogenous` solves new multiproduct Bertrand prices; the runner checks convergence, profit gradients, and profit Hessians. Its profits are per potential consumer, and market-level conversion is determined by the supplied population. Retention implements product exit and ownership changes. Optional `entry_products` provides new product records in existing markets and `entry_xi` supplies one structural utility shock per entrant; arrays for costs, firms and shifts cover retained observations followed by entrants. Specify the entrant characteristics, utility shock and cost from the adopted identification and simulation design. Specify availability and the resulting market population consistently.

Set counterfactual `costs` to `null` for an ownership or exit scenario that holds each retained incumbent's fitted marginal cost constant. The runner selects those costs from the same `ProblemResults.compute_costs()` result in the retained product order, then solves the new equilibrium once through `Simulation.replace_endogenous`. Explicit arrays specify an adopted change in marginal costs. An entry scenario supplies costs for every retained product and entrant. Saved `cost_source` identifies the selected cost assumption, and the returned prices, shares, profits and welfare all use that scenario's equilibrium. These operations follow the [official simulation interface](https://pyblp.readthedocs.io/en/stable/_api/pyblp.Simulation.replace_endogenous.html).

Before calculating surplus, `blp` evaluates `dX1/dp beta + dX2/dp Sigma nu` at every agent in each baseline and counterfactual market. The combined derivative must be independent of prices, finite, strictly negative, and common across that agent's products. The symbolic price-affinity check combines estimated coefficients before substituting product attributes, including entrants; zero contributions and exact cancellations therefore retain their actual meaning. Product equality uses relative tolerance `1e-10` and absolute tolerance zero. The strict sign check rejects zero and every positive derivative. A violation raises `ValueError` before surplus is computed. Product-specific money utility or nonlinear price utility requires an adopted compensating-variation calculation through econ-design. Each scenario computes surplus once for the reported surplus and welfare total.

The counterfactual reuses the fitted `problem.agents` market IDs, integration nodes and weights. It selects the rows of remaining markets in their original order and preserves every node column, including columns whose fitted sigma is zero. `Simulation` receives these arrays as `agent_data` with no new integration draw. With no nonlinear formulation it receives no agent data. This keeps an ownership, cost or choice-set change on the same consumer distribution even when the integration seed is absent or an entire market exits.

An optional `market_sizes` object maps every baseline market's string ID to its positive potential-consumer count. The runner multiplies each market's saved per-consumer surplus by that count, retains the market totals, and sums them into `aggregate_welfare`. Its baseline and counterfactual use the same population. A market whose inside products all exit retains that population at the normalized outside option. Named statistics save the total consumer, producer and social surplus in each scenario and their changes. The `welfare_totals` display carries the population weights and monetary unit into the common gallery and Writing context.

Set `report_agent_surplus:true` to retain the official `compute_consumer_surpluses(keep_all=True)` values. `agent_welfare` and the `consumer_types` display pair each value with its market, integration-type index, integration weight and taste deviation `Sigma nu`. The same nodes identify each type before and after the intervention. Interpret these rows as the model's numerical integration types, and aggregate them with their saved weights. A demographic or empirically defined group requires its adopted observation and weighting map. The monetary conversion continues to use the checked price-affine utility and each agent's common negative price derivative.

`model: equity` delegates its baseline directly to `equity.fit`; the `scenario` is null and the adopted equity counterfactual belongs in that specification. The same owner supports estimated demand, PyBLP simulations, and `model: cournot`. For the latter, inverse demand is `P=a-bQ`, firm cost is `c_i q_i + d_i q_i^2/2`, and a firm's best response is `clip((a-c_i-b Q_-i)/(2b+d_i), 0, capacity_i)`. Supply-side instruments identify inverse demand when observed quantity is endogenous. The shared equity solver estimates inverse demand by 2SLS or accepts supplied coefficients and checks every capacity-constrained best response. Under linear demand the consumer surplus is `b Q^2/2`; profit equals revenue less variable cost, and their sum gives welfare when production has no external cost.

## Auction models

All private-value cases have n risk-neutral symmetric bidders with independent uniform values on [0,1], observed n and format, a public reserve r, simultaneous sealed bids, independent tie breaking and zero value for no allocation. In a second-price auction each active bidder bids value. In a first-price auction a type v >= r bids `v - (v^n-r^n)/(n v^(n-1))`; lower types do not participate. Uniform zero-reserve bid recovery is `v=n b/(n-1)` in the first-price case and `v=b` in the second. This is structural inversion using the adopted bid distribution, whose support and bidder count are identifying inputs. Expected revenue integrates the equilibrium payment over the largest order statistic; bidder surplus is expected realized value less revenue. The runner optimizes reserve over [0,1] and calculates sale probability, revenue, expected total and bidder surplus. `baseline` and `scenario` each require `n`, `format` (first or second), `information` (private or common), `reserve`, and `bids`; recovery observations use zero reserve.

The common-value model has independent uniform signals X_1,...,X_n, common realized value `V=sum X_i/n`, and each bidder observes only its own signal before bidding. At own x and highest rival signal t, `E[V|x,max rival=t]=x/n+t/2`; this incorporates the information conveyed by winning. With zero reserve the second-price equilibrium bid is `(n+2)x/(2n)` and the first-price bid is `(n-1)(n+2)x/(2n^2)`. The runner numerically maximizes expected profit over the rival-signal threshold at x=0.6 and reports the distance from the equilibrium threshold. Common-value recovery data and nonzero reserves require their own identifying distribution and are outside this specified model.

## Search, bargaining and platforms

Search has n independent uniform [0,1] offers, search cost c per inspection, a known outside option and free recall. The consumer inspects sequentially until the best observed value reaches `z=1-sqrt(2c)` or no offers remain. This satisfies `E[(X-z)+]=c`. For outside below z, expected inspections are `sum_{k=0}^{n-1} z^k`; the integrated distribution of the final maximum gives expected gross and net surplus. `offers`, `search_cost`, `outside_option` are the required inputs. The scenario changes the search environment and recomputes the rule and value.

In bargaining, the buyer values trade v, the seller bears cost c and a per-transaction tax t, outside payoffs are d_b and d_s, and buyer bargaining weight is w. A deal occurs when `S=v-c-t-d_b-d_s>0`. Maximizing `(u_b-d_b)^w (u_s-d_s)^(1-w)` gives buyer gain wS and seller gain (1-w)S. Price is v minus buyer payoff. The output includes tax revenue, markup, allocation and the Nash first-order residual. Required fields are `buyer_value`, `seller_cost`, `buyer_outside`, `seller_outside`, `buyer_weight`, `tax`.

For the two-sided platform, side participation q solves inverse demands `p_A=a_A+g_A q_B-q_A` and `p_B=a_B+g_B q_A-q_B`. The profit-maximizing monopolist chooses both participation levels with unit costs c_A,c_B. Its FOCs are `2q_A-(g_A+g_B)q_B=a_A-c_A` and the symmetric equation for B. Nonnegative cross effects, sum below two and positive participation give an interior unique maximum. Each side's consumer surplus is q²/2; social welfare is the sum of both consumer surpluses and platform profit. Required arrays `intercepts`, `cross_effects`, `costs` each have two entries. Policy or externality changes enter the scenario arrays.

## Matching

The matching market consists of equal-size sets with strict complete preferences and one-to-one assignments. Proposers apply sequentially to their next choice; each receiver holds its preferred offer and rejects the others until no proposer is free. No blocking pair remains. Welfare is the sum of provided cardinal partner values; these values strictly follow each supplied preference ordering. Specify arrays `proposers`, `receivers` and maps `proposer_preferences`, `receiver_preferences`, `proposer_values`, `receiver_values`. A changed institution or preference profile is represented by a full scenario. Outcomes report each match, total cardinal welfare, proposals and the number of blocking pairs.

## Estimated and fixed parameters

Saved parameter tables identify `estimated` and `fixed` coefficients from the official `ProblemResults` parameter bounds. Equal lower and upper bounds identify a fixed parameter, including zero restrictions in random-coefficient matrices. Concentrated linear coefficients remain estimated. Each row retains the official value, standard error and economic unit; table rendering reads that saved status and performs no fit.

## Estimating auction value distributions

`model: auction_fit` accepts complete records from zero-reserve, independent-private-value auctions with n symmetric risk-neutral bidders and values drawn independently from `Uniform(0,U)`, where U is unknown. Each model object contains `n`, `format` (`first` or `second`), `records`, and `counterfactual_reserve`. Each record has a unique string `auction_id`, a list of exactly n objects with `bidder_id` and `amount`, a `winner_id`, and a `payment`. The winner is the unique highest bidder. Payment equals the highest bid under first price and the second-highest bid under second price. The runner validates these relations before estimation.

For first price, the equilibrium bid is `(n-1)v/n`, so each observed bid yields `v=nb/(n-1)`; second-price bidding gives `v=b`. The likelihood of the recovered independent values under Uniform(0,U) is `U^(-N)` for U at least the largest value, so the maximum-likelihood estimate is their sample maximum. `scipy.stats.uniform.fit` fixes the location at zero and estimates U; the output also reports the unbiased order-statistic correction `(N+1)U_hat/N`, all recovered bidder values, recorded payments and their mean. Using U_hat, the runner evaluates expected revenue and welfare at the supplied reserve and calculates the optimal reserve and its revenue. The same complete-record input contract supports second-price auctions. These formulas use all bids; estimation from winning bids alone requires the winning-order-statistic likelihood.
