# Stochastic RBC, one-asset HANK and native Dolo models

## Stochastic RBC model and estimation

`skills/econ-data/scripts/research_dsge.py` solves a stochastic representative-household economy. The household maximizes `E₀ Σ βᵗ log(cₜ)` and chooses consumption and next-period capital subject to `cₜ+kₜ₊₁=exp(zₜ)kₜ^α+(1−δ)kₜ`. Productivity follows `zₜ₊₁=ρzₜ+εₜ₊₁`, with independent Gaussian innovations of variance `σ²`. Competitive production has a fixed unit labor endowment. The stochastic Euler equation is

`1/cₜ = β Eₜ{[α exp(zₜ₊₁) kₜ₊₁^(α−1)+1−δ]/cₜ₊₁}`.

The deterministic steady state is `k*=[α/(1/β−1+δ)]^[1/(1−α)]`, `y*=k*^α`, and `c*=y*−δk*`. Preference parameter β is an input calibration. The solver rejects invalid parameters and nonpositive consumption.

Let `xₜ=log(kₜ/k*)`. The local policy is `xₜ₊₁=a xₜ+b zₜ`, with log consumption deviation `A xₜ+B zₜ`. Resource linearization gives `A=[αy*+(1−δ)k*−k*a]/c*` and `B=(y*−k*b)/c*`. For `η=βα(k*)^(α−1)`, conditional Euler linearization gives `A=[A−η(α−1)]a` and `B(1−ρ)=[A−η(α−1)]b−ηρ`. `scipy.optimize.brentq` solves the first equation on `(0,1)` for the stable root; the second is solved analytically for `b`. The output records all four coefficients, the stable root, the original linearized Euler and resource coefficients and the steady-state Euler residual. These residuals must be below `1e−9`.

The exact nonlinear conditional Euler residual is evaluated with 25-node Gauss–Hermite integration. Starting from `(x,z)=(0.03,0.04)`, the calculation jointly scales the state and the estimated innovation standard deviation by 1, 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8. At each node it applies the local log-capital policy and uses the exact resource equation for current and next consumption. The residual is second order in the scale near zero; the residuals, quadrature choice and state are saved. Actual simulation also uses the local capital policy and exact budget equation, reporting period-by-period resource residuals. It is a first-order policy for the stated stochastic economy, not an exact global policy.

`statsmodels.tsa.arima.model.ARIMA(..., order=(1,0,0), trend='n', enforce_stationarity=True).fit()` estimates persistence and innovation variance by Gaussian maximum likelihood. It reports parameter names, estimates, standard errors, full covariance, log likelihood, convergence and numerical warnings. Identification assumes a zero-mean stationary AR(1), independent Gaussian innovations, and nonzero lagged productivity variation. The specification identifies the CSV path, SHA-256, period column, productivity column, observation type and source. The reader verifies the file hash, requires consecutive ordered integer period IDs and finite equally spaced log-productivity observations, and records the period bounds and row count. Relative paths resolve against the specification directory. The separate simulation seed is recorded.

A one-period technology innovation creates the conditional mean log-productivity path `zₜ=ρᵗ shock`; `conditional_irf` reports the model's capital, output, consumption and investment changes along that path. With nonlinear level transformations, these are allocations evaluated at the conditional mean shock path. The stochastic paths instead draw Gaussian innovations and return each innovation and full allocation. `forecast_moments` reports sample means and variances of capital, output, consumption and investment, plus sample and model-implied moments of log productivity. The latter have conditional mean zero and variance `σ² Σⱼ₌₀ᵗ⁻¹ρ²ʲ` from the specified initial state `z₀=0`.

Welfare sums exact `βᵗ log(cₜ)` over the reported path under the local policy. Its terminal value is the first-order approximation `V*+vₖ x_T+v_z z_T`, where `V*=log(c*)/(1−β)`, `vₖ=A/(1−βa)` and `v_z=(B+βvₖb)/(1−βρ)`. The result reports baseline, conditional-mean-path and mean simulated welfare under this approximation. Positive consumption is required throughout each computed path.

The JSON specification contains `parameters.alpha`, `beta`, `delta`; `observations.type`, `source`, `path`, `sha256`, `period_column`, `value_column`; `horizon`, `simulation_paths`, `seed` and `technology_shock`. Model levels are goods per period, capital is goods, and z is log productivity. Run `python skills/econ-data/scripts/research_dsge.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json`. The result is written atomically through the existing output helper. The computation uses NumPy, SciPy and statsmodels 0.14.6, declared in `requirements-macro.txt`. The companion OLG model in `macro-models.md` addresses heterogeneous households, heterogeneous firms, the financial capacity margin and markup policy.

## One-asset HANK equilibrium and transitions

The same runner uses `model="hank_one_asset"` for `MC-hank-one-asset`. It composes the official [HANK blocks](https://github.com/shade-econ/sequence-jacobian/blob/eb4ac4c3157357a4cacbea4b5a7a281f5b48cab9/src/sequence_jacobian/examples/hank.py) with the official [household EGM block](https://github.com/shade-econ/sequence-jacobian/blob/eb4ac4c3157357a4cacbea4b5a7a281f5b48cab9/src/sequence_jacobian/hetblocks/hh_labor.py). Package version is 1.0.0 at revision `eb4ac4c3157357a4cacbea4b5a7a281f5b48cab9`; the source revision fixes the native option propagation. The runner constructs these blocks without calling `hank.dag()` and therefore passes the adopted calibration rather than executing the factory's fixed example.

A unit mass of infinitely lived households chooses consumption `c`, next assets `a'` and hours `n`. Efficiency `e` follows the adopted Rouwenhorst process; assets have lower bound zero. The budget is `c+a'=(1+r)a+wen+T(e)`. Utility is `c^(1−1/eis)/(1−1/eis)−vphi*n^(1+1/frisch)/(1+1/frisch)`, with log consumption at `eis=1`. The official EGM solves household policies and propagates their joint asset/income distribution. Firm labor demand is `L=Y/Z`; dividends are `Y−wL−mu/(mu−1)/(2*kappa)*log(1+pi)^2*Y`. Government debt is `B`, taxes are `rB`, and dividends net of taxes are distributed in proportion to efficiency. The monetary rule is `r_t=(1+rstar_(t−1)+phi*pi_(t−1))/(1+pi_t)−1`. Thus a period-zero `rstar` shock enters the nominal rule with its declared lag.

The pricing equation is `kappa*(w/Z−1/mu)+(Y_next/Y)*log(1+pi_next)/(1+r_next)−log(1+pi)=0`. Asset, labor and goods residuals are `A−B`, `NE−L`, and `Y−C−mu/(mu−1)/(2*kappa)*log(1+pi)^2*Y`. The stationary model replaces the pricing equation with `w=Z/mu` and calibrates `beta,vphi` to the adopted asset and effective-labor targets. The transition model jointly solves `w,Y,pi` against `asset_mkt,goods_mkt,nkpc_res`; the labor residual is saved and checked separately.

Supply `calibration_source`, the full `parameters` object and `steady_state`. Parameters are the native `rstar,eis,frisch,B,mu,rho_s,sigma_s,kappa,phi,Y,Z,pi,nS,amax,nA`; the public calibration also supplies `r`. `steady_state.unknowns` contains initial values or native brackets for `beta,vphi`; `steady_state.targets` contains `asset_mkt,NE`. `steady_state.solver` defaults to `broyden_custom`; `steady_state.options` uses the native root and household convergence option names. The result saves supplied values, solved primitives, effective options and source hashes. These calibrations perform no estimation of observed data.

| `operation` | Additional inputs | Official calculation and saved quantities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `solve_steady_state` | Calibration and stationary targets | Calls `Block.solve_steady_state`, then evaluates the transition model's stationary equations. Saves the native `SteadyStateDict.toplevel` and `internals`, including household grids, policies, marginal values and distribution. |
| `solve_jacobian` | Integer `horizon>=2`; `inputs` selecting `rstar` and/or `Z` | Calls `Block.solve_jacobian` and saves output-by-shock matrices with output-period rows and shock-period columns. Saves the native partial Jacobians for subsequent official impulse calculations. |
| `solve_impulse_linear` | `horizon`; `shocks` containing finite `rstar` and/or `Z` arrays of that length | Calls `Block.solve_impulse_linear` using the same steady state and partial Jacobians. Saves first-order deviations, implied levels and original linearized market/pricing residuals. Its computed quantities include stationary household welfare and linear aggregate responses. |
| `solve_impulse_nonlinear` | The same shock arrays; optional native `solver_options.tol,maxit,verbose` | Calls `Block.solve_impulse_nonlinear` with household internals. Saves nonlinear equilibrium levels/deviations, household consumption/hours/assets/distributions, residuals and transition welfare. |

Shock arrays are deviations in levels from the stationary calibration. `scenario={"name":"policy comparison","shocks":{...}}` adds a separately solved shock path with the same parameters and initial population. `outputs` can add native model outputs; the original market/pricing outputs are retained in every transition. `transition`, when supplied, is exactly `{"unknowns":["w","Y","pi"],"targets":["asset_mkt","goods_mkt","nkpc_res"]}`. `equilibrium_tolerance` defaults to `1e-7` and applies to all four saved market/pricing residuals. The nonlinear API also applies its native stopping rule. Diagnostics report household budgets, interior/binding Euler gaps, labor first-order conditions, distribution propagation, total mass, consumption and asset-grid boundaries at the actual adopted grid.

For subsequent operations, pass `saved_solution={"path":"previous-result.json","sha256":"..."}`. That result restores native `SteadyStateDict` and `JacobianDict` objects from their saved numerical coordinates. The runner checks the source version and rejects changed stationary inputs; a changed calibration requires its own stationary solution. Matching partial Jacobians are reused for the saved horizon, outputs and shocks. `computation.requested_operation`, `operations_executed`, `parameter_operation`, `computed_quantities` and `reused_objects` distinguish the actual stage and reused solution. Subsequent transitions retain the original primitives and complete stationary result.

Stationary values solve `(I−beta*P_policy)V=u(c,n)` using the official household lottery and Markov expectation operators. This evaluates the already solved policy. The runner saves the original value-equation residual. Transition welfare is `Σ_(t=0)^(T−1) beta^t Σ D_t u(c_t,n_t)+beta^T Σ D_T V_ss`; terminal `D_T` is propagated from the final computed asset policy and the same income process. Backward state values and this forward-distribution sum must agree within `1e-9`. All scenarios use the baseline initial distribution. The common consumption equivalent permanently scales baseline consumption while retaining baseline hours. With `p=1−1/eis`, baseline discounted consumption utility `C_b`, baseline labor cost `L_b` and scenario welfare `W_s`, it is `((W_s+L_b)/C_b)^(1/p)−1`; at `eis=1`, it is `exp((1−beta)*(W_s−W_b))−1`.

The reusable result contains `native_solution`, `jacobian_cache`, `jacobian` or `impulses`, `welfare`, named `statistics`, the full adopted specification and provenance. Display blocks retain equilibrium quantities, household policies, stationary distributions, welfare and impulse paths for the existing gallery and writing lifecycle. Equations, parameter sources/roles, normalization, horizon and welfare definitions accompany those saved rows.

## Dolo YAML and time iteration

Set `model="dolo_yaml"` to execute the supplied native YAML through the same `research_dsge.py run` command. The existing `model="stochastic_rbc", options.algorithm="dolo_time_iteration"` dispatch reaches the same Dolo implementation; the supplied YAML defines its actual model and the result retains that definition. A contract selecting the fixed-labor log-utility RBC must match those restrictions. The official [RBC IID YAML](https://github.com/EconForge/dolo.py/blob/33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825/examples/models/rbc_iid.yaml) instead specifies endogenous labor, consumption curvature five and nonnegative investment. It runs as `dolo_yaml` with its own adopted equations and calibration.

Install `skills/econ-data/scripts/requirements-dolo.txt` in the Dolo stage environment. The selected official revision is `33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825`, version 0.4.9.20, Python `>=3.12,<3.14`. Its native dependency requires QuantEcon `>=0.7.2,<0.8`; the macro family's Aiyagari implementation uses 0.13.1. Both calculations retain their selected environment identities.

Both HANK and Dolo use the existing `research_dynamic.official_package` resolver before calculation or saved-result reuse. It obtains the installed distribution version and commit from `direct_url.json`; for a local checkout installation it resolves that checkout's Git HEAD. Both must equal the adopted package and revision. The returned identity supplies `provenance.official_package` and `upstream_revision`; subsequent reuse also compares the saved native source hashes.

The specification supplies `yaml_model={"path":"model.yaml","sha256":"...","source":"..."}` and `calibration_source`. Optional `calibration` overrides named native symbols through the official immutable `Model.recalibrate` operation. `discretization` passes native `grid_options` and `dprocess_options` to one `DiscretizedModel`. The result embeds the complete adopted YAML, its hash, symbol ordering, original equations, effective calibration, state grid, exogenous regimes, integration nodes and weights.

`operation="time_iteration"` calls official [`yaml_import`](https://github.com/EconForge/dolo.py/blob/33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825/src/dolo/compiler/model_import.py), [`find_steady_state` and `residuals`](https://github.com/EconForge/dolo.py/blob/33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825/src/dolo/algos/steady_state.py), and [`time_iteration(details=True)`](https://github.com/EconForge/dolo.py/blob/33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825/src/dolo/algos/time_iteration.py). `solver` accepts `engine,backend,interpolation,tol_eps,tol_eta,maxit,inner,accelerate,ignore_constraints,verbose`. Defaults are the vectorized engine, cubic interpolation, `tol_eps=1e-8`, `tol_eta=1e-9`, `maxit=500`, inner tolerance `1e-10`, and the declared constraints. The original steady-state transition residual `g−s` and arbitrage residual `f` must meet `validation.steady_state_tolerance`, default `1e-8`.

The native convergence record preserves `iterations,converged,error_res,error_step`. The native algorithm declares convergence when either its Euler-residual or policy-update criterion holds; its returned `error_res` precedes the final policy update. The runner evaluates the returned decision rule at every saved state/regime and calls official [`F(dmodel,controls,dr,...)`](https://github.com/EconForge/dolo.py/blob/33f873eef42d9644b5a0705a63fb3a34de5c2825/src/dolo/numeric/residuals.py) with the same process and interpolation. It saves raw arbitrage residuals, bound values and feasibility, interior indicators and complementary residuals. The sign convention is `F=0` in the interior, `F>=0` at the lower bound and `F<=0` at the upper bound. `validation.arbitrage_tolerance` defaults to `1e-7`; `validation.bound_tolerance` defaults to `1e-8`. Original final-policy conditions determine completed output.

`native_solution` saves grid values and native interpolation coefficients. `operation="reuse_solution"` with a hash-bound `saved_solution` reconstructs the official decision rule from saved values, reuses the saved steady state and reevaluates final-policy residuals. It uses the embedded YAML when no input path is supplied. Model, calibration, discretization, interpolation and native source hashes must match. A subsequent `time_iteration` can use the same saved rule as its native starting rule. These operations retain `computation`, equations, parameter definitions, state/control coordinates, units, `statistics`, display rows and provenance through the existing result lifecycle; they estimate no parameters.
