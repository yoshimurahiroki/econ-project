## Core causal and empirical methods

- `rdd_localrand`: restrict the sample to a prespecified symmetric window around the cutoff. Under complete randomization of assignment within that window, `scipy.stats.permutation_test` tests the individual sharp null by permuting assignment at the observed arm counts. An HC1 Wald interval describes the local mean contrast; excluded window IDs are recorded.
- `rdd_sharp`, `rdd_fuzzy`: a local linear regression on each side of a cutoff with triangular kernel and local quadratic bias correction. `rdrobust.rdrobust` supplies the bias-corrected jump and its robust nearest-neighbor covariance and interval. The fuzzy coefficient is the outcome jump divided by the treatment jump; its local complier interpretation requires continuity, exclusion, monotonicity and a nonzero treatment jump. The options select the cutoff, running variable and either an explicit positive bandwidth or `mserd`; fuzzy RDD adds the binary receipt column. Fitted bandwidths and local IDs are recorded.
- `rct`: under complete randomization with the observed treated and control counts, compare their means. `scipy.stats.permutation_test` permutes assignment to test the sharp null of zero treatment effect for every unit. The accompanying `statsmodels.OLS` HC1 covariance and Wald interval describe the average assignment contrast; the permutation p value is a separate sharp-null test.
- `rct_design`: use the [adopted randomization design](randomization.md#rct-design-assignment-and-diagnosis) for individual, block, cluster, block-cluster, multiple-arm, factorial and covariate-balanced allocation. The same `randomizr` declaration supplies assignment probabilities, official `estimatr` inference, sharp-null resampling and `DeclareDesign` power/MDE diagnosis.
- `gls`: y = Xβ + u with a specified diagonal variance matrix Ω. `statsmodels.GLS` minimizes (y−Xβ)'Ω⁻¹(y−Xβ); Ω is read from the positive `variance` column. Independent observations and correct conditional mean identify β. The supported covariance is HC1 or one-way cluster robust.
- `iv_gmm`: y = Xβ + Dτ + u, E[Z̃u] = 0, with Z̃ = [X,Z]. `linearmodels.iv.IVGMM` computes two-step heteroskedasticity-robust GMM; valid excluded instruments and the rank condition identify τ. The output includes the overidentification J statistic and full covariance. This is conventional GMM inference, not weak-instrument-robust inference.
- `iv_ar`: the same linear IV model with one endogenous regressor, independent homoskedastic normal disturbances and fixed instruments. For each candidate τ, regress y−Dτ on X and Z and test the joint zero excluded-instrument coefficients using the exact F reference. Analytic inversion of this Anderson–Rubin test solves a quadratic inequality; the returned confidence set can be bounded, disconnected, empty or unbounded. A null endpoint denotes infinity. Conventional 2SLS standard errors and Wald intervals are saved separately and do not replace that confidence set.
- `iv_ar_robust`: `ivDiag::AR_test` 1.0.8 or later inverts the heteroskedastic or clustered Anderson–Rubin test for one endogenous-regressor coefficient. Supply excluded instruments, exogenous controls, adopted fixed effects, weights and cluster columns. The official confidence-set components, bounds, empty-set classification and null test are saved. `ivDiag::ivDiag(bootstrap=FALSE,run.AR=FALSE,parallel=FALSE)` supplies the corresponding first stage, reduced form and conventional 2SLS result on the same complete-case sample. Their official 95% Wald intervals retain their own level beside the AR set's chosen level.
- `distribution_effect`: define Z_i = 1{y_i ≤ c} at the prespecified threshold c. `statsmodels.OLS` of Z on a binary randomized treatment and an intercept gives the treated-minus-control outcome CDF at c with HC1 or cluster covariance. Random assignment identifies the marginal potential-outcome CDF contrast at that threshold; the interval is pointwise. Coefficients, standard errors and interval endpoints use probability units; covariance uses squared probability units. `spec.unit` supplies the original outcome and threshold unit. The saved main display uses probability; `displays.percentage_points` multiplies the treatment contrast, its standard error and both endpoints by 100. Its p value and sample remain unchanged. Writing can bind the corresponding `coefficient_pp`, `standard_error_pp` and `confidence_95_lower_pp` / `confidence_95_upper_pp` statistics in percentage points. The original adopted specification and threshold remain attached to both displays.
- `multitreatment_did`: in a balanced two-period panel, estimate Δy_i = α + X_i,preβ + Σ_k τ_k D_ik,post + ε_i with `statsmodels.OLS`. Each intervention or dose is zero before treatment, at least one unit has all zero post doses, and controls are measured before treatment. The separate linear coefficients describe simultaneous interventions and dose under conditional parallel trends, no anticipation and additive effects; inference is HC1 or one-way clustered at the unit-change level.
- `generalized_synth`: `gsynth::gsynth` estimates an interactive fixed-effects counterfactual with a specified factor count and unit and time effects. A balanced panel contains never-treated units and one common absorbing adoption date with at least five pretreatment periods. A seeded parametric bootstrap provides the joint covariance of the average effect and event-time effects. Identification requires a stable factor structure, adequate untreated comparison units and an appropriate error model.
- `staggered_did`: `did::att_gt` estimates group-time ATT and `did::aggte` supplies the adopted `dynamic`, `simple`, `group` or `calendar` aggregation. The runner forwards panel/repeated-cross-section structure, unbalanced-panel handling, comparison group, anticipation, base period, sampling weights, weight timing, score, clustering, multiplier bootstrap, repetitions, significance level and simultaneous-band options. Existing specifications retain `panel=TRUE`, `allow_unbalanced_panel=FALSE`, `anticipation=0`, `base_period="varying"`, `est_method="dr"`, `bstrap=FALSE`, `cband=FALSE` and dynamic aggregation. Package preprocessing determines the fitted observations; the result saves those IDs and its exclusions. Each group-time cell saves its actual base period, eligible comparison sample, official normalized sampling weights, original input weights and role (`pre_diagnostic`, `anticipation`, `att` or normalized `reference`). Each aggregate saves its contributing cohorts, periods, target sample and normalized cohort weights. Saved effective aggregation conditions follow the executed did version: 2.5.1 balances dynamic effects by calendar reach after missing ATT cells are removed; 2.5.1.906 and later require common nonmissing event-time support and apply the group `min_e` window. Positive group `min_e` is rejected before estimation on earlier versions. The runtime did version and effective aggregation conditions are recorded. Standard errors and band critical values come from the official fitted objects. The saved joint covariance is the analytical covariance of the official influence functions with the adopted clustering; bootstrap standard errors remain separate saved quantities. See the versioned [att_gt source](https://github.com/cran/did/blob/453a1be859411edc1c8933891940cb3a570d7095/R/att_gt.R), [aggregation source](https://github.com/cran/did/blob/453a1be859411edc1c8933891940cb3a570d7095/R/compute.aggte.R), [updated aggregation source, did 2.5.1.906](https://github.com/bcallaway11/did/blob/9ead26b9d37c7f2bc202bebc4f5e8220ab7e8c13/R/compute.aggte.R), [att_gt interface](https://bcallaway11.github.io/did/reference/att_gt.html) and [aggte interface](https://bcallaway11.github.io/did/reference/aggte.html).
- `synthetic_control`: fit nonnegative donor weights summing to one with `pysyncon.Synth` on a specified training period, using equal predictor importance for the training outcomes. The post-treatment effect is the mean treated-minus-synthetic gap in post periods minus its mean in held-out untreated validation periods. `statsmodels.OLS` HC1 covariance across these held-out time gaps treats fitted weights as fixed; stable untreated expected gaps, untreated donors, and independent held-out time errors identify this specified contrast. Record weights, pre-fit errors, period gaps and time partitions. Save the calculated actual and synthetic trajectories as `display.kind="scm"` and their gap as `displays.scm_gap`; rows use calendar `time`, `estimate` and the trajectory `component`. Metadata records donor weights and the first observed post-treatment time. The series preserve the calculated paths; the fitted post-versus-validation contrast retains its separate coefficient inference.
- `dynamic_panel`: y_it = ρ y_i,t−1 + X_itβ + α_i + u_it. `pydynpd.regression.abond` fits two-step difference GMM with y levels at lags 2–3 as instruments for the differenced lag and declared strictly exogenous X as standard instruments. A balanced consecutive panel with independent units, valid lag moments and no serial correlation in level errors supplies the identification. The result records the Windmeijer-corrected covariance, Hansen test, first- and second-order differenced residual tests, and IDs excluded for initial lags.
- `tobit`: latent y* = Xβ + σu, u standard normal, and observed y = max(c,y*) at a specified left-censoring threshold c. The `statsmodels.GenericLikelihoodModel` likelihood uses the normal log density for uncensored observations and `scipy.special.log_ndtr` for censored probabilities, estimating β and log σ by BFGS. The full likelihood covariance includes log σ; the result records the censored count and convergence. Normality, homoskedasticity and exogeneity identify the latent conditional slope.
- `heckman`: use the [sample-selection contract](outcomes.md#heckman-sample-selection) for a continuous outcome observed when a binary selection indicator equals one. Official `sampleSelection::heckit` estimates the adopted joint Gaussian model by two-step or maximum likelihood, with an explicit selection-only exclusion and unweighted independent observations.
- `network_spillover`: specify y_i = α + X_iβ + τD_i + γΣ_j w_ijD_j + u_i. `libpysal` constructs the row-standardized neighbor exposure from a known edge file and `statsmodels.OLS` fits the joint direct and spillover contrasts. Random assignment, a correct exposure mapping and independent errors or declared independent clusters establish the specified comparison. The output records every exposure and the edge-file hash.
- `policy_tree`: the original four-option specification uses `econml.policy.DRPolicyTree` and the existing held-out binary IPW gain with HC1 inference. An explicit `options.policy` activates [multi-arm policy learning and evaluation](policy.md#multi-arm-policy-learning-and-evaluation): training-only cross-fitted AIPW rewards, official `econml.policy.PolicyTree`, known or estimated propensities, baseline/value/net-benefit contrasts, target weights and cluster inference. Exact batch budget/capacity allocation uses its separately declared fixed-cohort randomization target.
- `shift_share`: form Z_i = Σ_k s_ik g_k using specified nonnegative shares that sum to one and fixed shocks, then estimate a one-endogenous-variable linear IV model with `linearmodels.iv.IV2SLS`. The stated supported design conditions on shocks and assumes independent unit errors or one-way independent clusters, instrument exogeneity and relevance; this covariance is not shock-level inference. The first-stage partial R² and test statistic are saved.
- `spatial_lag`: y = ρWy + Xβ + u with an explicitly supplied network W and independent Gaussian innovations. `spreg.ML_Lag` fits the spatial autoregressive model by maximum likelihood, reports the full covariance and simple impact multipliers. The edge CSV has exactly `source,target,weight`; every observation has a positive-weight neighbor, self-edges are excluded and weights are row-standardized. Exogenous X and a known W identify the parametric model. The estimate of ρ is dimensionless.
- `causal_forest`: `econml.dml.CausalForestDML` uses cross-fitted random forest nuisance predictions and an honest forest to estimate heterogeneous binary-treatment effects τ(x). Conditional exchangeability and treatment overlap identify these effects. The engine returns individual fitted CATEs and intervals, plus the sample-average CATE and its population-summary standard error bound; the latter is not the covariance of all individual predictions. The same sample defines the target aggregation. The code fixes two folds, one thread, and the specified seed, tree count and minimum leaf size.
- `hdfe`: the same additive fixed-effect linear model with any declared list of effect columns; `pyfixest.feols` absorbs the effects and uses the adopted analytic/frequency weights, small-sample correction and heteroskedastic or one-way CRV1/CRV3 covariance. Singleton observations are retained; collinear regressors or dropped observations cause rejection. The full covariance and pointwise intervals come from the fitted object at the adopted confidence level. Fitted row IDs and weight values retain their input order; row count and frequency-weight total are saved separately.
- `panel_fe`: y_it = X_itβ + α_i + λ_t + u_it. `linearmodels.panel.PanelOLS` absorbs the selected entity and/or time effects. Identification requires sufficient within variation and strict conditional exogeneity; entity-clustered covariance allows within-entity dependence. Entity-time pairs must be unique.
- `quantile`: Q_q(y|X) = Xβ_q. `statsmodels.QuantReg` minimizes the check loss at the chosen q, with the engine's robust kernel covariance, Epanechnikov kernel and Hall–Sheather bandwidth. β_q is a conditional quantile contrast, and causal interpretation requires the relevant exogeneity and quantile specification.
- `poisson`: E[y|X] = exp(Xβ) for nonnegative y, including continuous outcomes and observed zeros. `statsmodels.GLM` with the Poisson log link solves the PPML score Σ_i X_i[y_i−exp(X_iβ)] = 0 using the intercept and declared regressors. The conditional-mean restriction, exogeneity and full-rank variation identify β as a log-mean contrast. The existing HC1 or one-way cluster covariance supplies inference without imposing equality of the conditional mean and variance; negative outcomes are rejected. ECON-03 connects this mean specification to `fixest::fepois` for adopted models with absorbed effects and offsets.
- `logit`: P(y=1|X) = exp(Xβ)/(1+exp(Xβ)). `statsmodels.GLM` fits the binomial logit likelihood and returns robust covariance. β is a conditional log-odds contrast under the specified conditional probability and exogeneity.
