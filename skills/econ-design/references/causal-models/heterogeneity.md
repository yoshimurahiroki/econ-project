## Official DoubleML fits

Select `design="orthogonal_score"` with the native family in `score`. Target kinds `qte`, `local_qte`, `tail_effect`, `potential_quantile`, `tail_mean` and `potential_mean` map to their corresponding native functions and coordinates. `implementation="doubleml"` also connects `sample_selection` and `quantile_effect` to SSM and QTE. The existing Heckman and R distributional-effect choices retain their original defaults.

`dml_plr`, `dml_did`, `dml_irm`, `dml_pliv`, `dml_iivm`, `dml_ssm`, `dml_qte`, `dml_pq`, `dml_cvar` and `dml_apo` use the common `run` schema and the same official fitting function. `regressors` names the covariates. PLIV uses one treatment in `endogenous` and one or more excluded instruments. IIVM uses one binary treatment in `endogenous` and one binary instrument. The other models use `options.treatment` and `endogenous=[]`; their `instruments` is empty except for nonignorable SSM and local QTE. DID takes one row per unit, the post-treatment outcome in `outcome`, and the pretreatment outcome column in `options.outcome_pre`; the runner passes their difference to `DoubleMLDIDData`. Its covariates describe the pretreatment information set. Each binary variable contains 0 and 1. All model columns have distinct roles.

| Method | Official score and target | Required nuisance learners |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dml_plr` | `partialling out`: E[(D−m(X)){Y−l(X)−θ(D−m(X))}]=0; `IV-type`: E[(D−m(X)){Y−Dθ−g(X)}]=0. The partially linear conditional mean and exogenous residual treatment identify the slope. Under heterogeneous effects, its residual-treatment weighting defines the target; a population ATE additionally requires the adopted constant-effect restriction. | `ml_l`: E[Y given X], `ml_m`: E[D given X]; `IV-type` also requires `ml_g`: E[Y−Dθ given X]. `ml_m` may be a classifier for binary D. |
| `dml_did` | `observational`: two-period ATT under no anticipation, conditional parallel trends and treatment-group overlap. `experimental` adds independence of treatment and pretreatment covariates. | `ml_g`: E[Y_post−Y_pre given D=0,X] for the observational score. The official fit also learns E[Y_post−Y_pre given D=1,X], used by the experimental score. `observational` also requires classifier `ml_m`: P(D=1 given X). |
| `dml_irm` | `ATE`: E[Y(1)−Y(0)]; `ATTE`: E[Y(1)−Y(0) given D=1]. Conditional exchangeability and overlap identify the chosen population contrast. | `ml_g`: E[Y given D,X]; classifier `ml_m`: P(D=1 given X). |
| `dml_pliv` | `partialling out` uses residualized Y, D and Z. With one Z its moment is E[(Z−m(X)){Y−l(X)−θ(D−r(X))}]=0. `IV-type` uses E[(Z−m(X)){Y−Dθ−g(X)}]=0 and one instrument. Preserve the partially linear IV coefficient. | Regressors `ml_l`: E[Y given X], `ml_m`: E[Z given X], `ml_r`: E[D given X]; `IV-type` also requires `ml_g`: E[Y−Dθ given X]. |
| `dml_iivm` | `LATE`: the complier effect under conditional instrument independence, exclusion, monotonicity, instrument overlap and a nonzero first stage. | `ml_g`: E[Y given Z,X]; classifiers `ml_m`: P(Z=1 given X), `ml_r`: P(D=1 given Z,X). |
| `dml_ssm` | `missing-at-random` or `nonignorable`: E[Y(1)−Y(0)] in the retained selection population. Both scores require treatment exchangeability and treatment/selection overlap. MAR adds outcome-selection exchangeability given D,X. Nonignorable selection uses an excluded selection instrument, scalar threshold/rank conditions and conditional effect homogeneity. | `ml_g` learns outcomes among S=1 by treatment; classifier `ml_m` learns treatment propensity; classifier `ml_pi` learns selection propensity. Nonignorable fitting augments X with the estimated selection propensity and requires a regression `ml_g`. |
| `dml_qte` | `PQ`: Q_Y(1)(τ)−Q_Y(0)(τ) under conditional treatment exchangeability and overlap. `LPQ`: the corresponding marginal quantile contrast among compliers under binary-IV independence, exclusion, monotonicity, overlap and positive complier probability. `CVaR`: C_τ(1)−C_τ(0), the contrast in upper-tail means. | `ml_g` and `ml_m` are classifiers for PQ/LPQ; CVaR uses regression `ml_g` and classifier `ml_m`. Native paired potential-outcome models share the outer folds, and native framework subtraction forms their effect and joint inference. |
| `dml_pq` | `PQ`: the marginal potential-outcome quantile Q_Y(d)(τ) at the declared binary treatment level d, under conditional exchangeability, overlap and the native density regularity conditions. | Classifier `ml_g` estimates the conditional outcome CDF at the preliminary quantile; classifier `ml_m` estimates treatment propensity. The native derivative uses its weighted Gaussian KDE with Silverman bandwidth. |
| `dml_cvar` | `CVaR`: C_τ(d)=q_τ(d)+E[(Y(d)−q_τ(d))_+]/(1−τ), the upper-tail potential-outcome mean. | Regression `ml_g` learns the threshold-transformed outcome; classifier `ml_m` learns treatment propensity. Native preliminary potential-quantile estimation supplies the threshold. |
| `dml_apo` | `APO`: E[Y(d)] at one observed numeric treatment level d in a binary or multivalued treatment, under conditional exchangeability and overlap for that level. | `ml_g` learns conditional outcomes at d and its complement; classifier `ml_m` learns P(D=d given X). Binary outcomes also permit a classifier `ml_g`. |

Required common options are `score`, integer `n_folds >= 2`, nonnegative integer `seed` and `learners`. Each learner maps its official name to `{"type":"linear","params":{}}`, with native constructor parameters. Types are `linear`, `ridge`, `lasso`, `logistic`, `random_forest_regressor`, `random_forest_classifier`, `hist_gradient_boosting_regressor` and `hist_gradient_boosting_classifier`. The runner supplies the declared seed to stochastic learners and one worker when omitted; explicit parameters are retained. The official class checks the required regression/classification interface. IRM and IIVM permit a classifier for `ml_g` with a binary outcome.

SSM additionally requires `selection`, the binary observation-status column passed as `DoubleMLSSMData.s_col`. `instruments=[]` for MAR; nonignorable selection requires its excluded selection instruments in `instruments`. Its identifying restrictions include relevance of Z for S conditional on D,X, outcome exclusion and conditional independence of Z, scalar threshold selection S=1{V≤ξ(D,X,Z)}, independence of V from D,Z conditional on X, treatment overlap conditional on X and the selection propensity, and conditional effect homogeneity between selected and full populations. These conditions belong to the adopted `identification` and optional `definitions`. The official [SSM model guide](https://docs.doubleml.org/stable/guide/models.html#sample-selection-models-ssm) supplies the model. The Heckman route retains its joint-Gaussian latent-outcome coefficient target.

SSM retains rows with S=0 and missing outcomes. Complete-case selection applies to treatment, selection, covariates and selection instruments; S=1 additionally requires a finite outcome. The native data backend requires finite outcomes, so the runner sets Y=0 for S=0 only in the estimator frame. The native score multiplies their outcome residuals by S. Saved `selection_ids`, `observed_outcome_ids` and `unselected_ids` retain the population and its observed subset. The native propensity processor applies to `ml_m`; the native `ml_pi` predictions remain untrimmed. MAR can use a classifier for a binary outcome. The nonignorable native branch uses `predict` for outcomes and therefore takes a regressor.

QTE requires a nonempty, strictly increasing `quantiles` list inside (0,1). `score="LPQ"` additionally requires exactly one binary instrument. PQ and CVaR require scalar `quantile` inside (0,1) and integer `treatment_level` equal to 0 or 1; the runner binds that level to their native `treatment` argument. APO requires finite numeric `treatment_level` present in the sample, with observations at another level; its native model requires at least five observations at the selected level. QTE/PQ/CVaR retain binary 0/1 treatment. These marginal quantiles, upper-tail means and potential means keep their target coordinates in `orthogonal_score`; the existing `qte`/`qtt` and distributional-DID routes retain their own population and trend contracts.

Optional `n_rep` defaults to 1. The official class draws folds at the declared independent unit: DID, IRM, APO and QTE/PQ/CVaR stratify by D, IIVM by D+2Z, SSM by D+2S, and clustered data split the cluster labels. Set `inference={"type":"official"}` for independent observations, or `{"type":"cluster","cluster":"column"}` / `{"type":"cluster","clusters":["column1","column2"]}` for one-/two-way clustering. QTE/PQ/CVaR and nonignorable SSM require independent observations: their native preliminary nuisance steps split rows within each training fold. QTE also synchronizes its component models through observation-only splitting. MAR SSM and APO support the common cluster path. `n_folds` is the number of folds per cluster dimension. An adopted `sample_splitting` supplies a list of repetitions, each containing fold objects with string `train_ids` and `test_ids`. Clustered folds also contain `train_clusters` and `test_clusters`, each mapping the declared cluster columns to their labels. Each fold must partition every cluster dimension, and its observation IDs must equal the intersection of the declared cluster sets. The official `set_sample_splitting` then validates the row partitions. Nested training splits retain the official half-sample split with seed 42, its strata and observation IDs; quantile/tail models also retain the preliminary stratified propensity folds.

`confidence_level` defaults to .95. The runner saves official `coef`, `se`, `pval` and `confint(level=...)`, preserving the package's repeated-split aggregation. `joint=true` requires `bootstrap={"method":"normal","n_rep_boot":500}`; `Bayes` and `wild` are also official multiplier choices. Bootstrap calls retain their returned t statistics. DoubleML 0.11.4 implements this bootstrap for independent observations; clustered estimates use the official analytical score variance.

IRM, IIVM, SSM, QTE, PQ, CVaR and APO accept native `normalize_ipw`, `ps_processor_config`, `trimming_rule` and `trimming_threshold`. IIVM accepts `subgroups={"always_takers":true,"never_takers":true}` and retains an adopted one-sided-compliance restriction. IRM `score="ATE"` and APO `score="APO"` accept `weights` as an analysis-column name or the official input-order array/dictionary (`weights`, `weights_bar`); dictionary dimensions are input rows and, for `weights_bar`, repetitions. The runner subsets these arrays to the recorded sample and passes their scale unchanged. A dictionary containing only `weights` becomes the native vector form; a dictionary with `weights_bar` retains both arrays. The native moment uses `psi_a=-weights/mean(weights)` and weights both the outcome regression and residual correction through `weights` and `weights_bar`. Its target-weight scale belongs to the adopted estimand; probability normalization is a separate `normalize_ipw` option. SSM and quantile/tail constructors have no sampling-weight argument.

DID accepts `in_sample_normalization` (official default `true`) and `clipping_threshold` (default .01, strictly between zero and .5). Its native score forms the ATT weights; PLR's native score supplies residual-treatment weighting. These constructors provide no sampling-weight argument, and the selector rejects a requested sampling-weight contract for PLR/DID. Both use the declared independent-unit folds and official score variance. The saved DID design and diagnostics preserve the pre/post columns, transformation, realized outcome changes, normalization and clipping. Official versioned interfaces: [PLR 0.11.4](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/plm/plr.py), [DID 0.11.4](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/did/did.py) and [DID data 0.11.4](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/data/did_data.py).

The published 0.11.4 IIVM implementation normalizes P(Z=1 given X) with D when `normalize_ipw=true`. The official [correction e31e61e](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/commit/e31e61efe8864c160c04488d91f1306e1e25c129) changes that argument to Z. The runner rejects this setting on 0.11.4 and requires an official release containing the correction. It preserves `normalize_ipw=false` and the supplied specification.

SSM 0.11.4 also requires `normalize_ipw=false` or its default. Its normalized branch multiplies each residual correction by a treatment-arm-share factor. The official [correction 7d2225b](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/commit/7d2225b6756f4329ce7fe47a3002f48eb3b30dff), merged in [469daf2](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/commit/469daf260d4ea3520b8265acf17a9d29e800c5e0), changes the inverse-probability weights to mean one. An explicit normalized SSM specification on 0.11.4 is rejected before fitting and requires an official release containing this correction.

Results retain fitted/excluded IDs, actual outer/nested fold IDs and cluster labels, official score components and out-of-fold nuisance predictions, effective learner parameters, repeated estimates/SEs, score weights, clipping/normalization/subgroup settings, package versions and actual data/estimator/fit/inference signatures. `orthogonal_score` binds the model, score, target expression, population, unit, treatment levels, quantile/tail coordinates, selection mechanism/exclusions, weight equation and sample-splitting role. QTE retains every native potential-outcome model's coordinates, estimates, predictions and score components, together with the native effect framework's scaled scores and variance scaling factors. Its covariance saves official marginal variances and JSON `null` for cross-quantile entries that the API does not export; simultaneous intervals come directly from the native joint bootstrap. The common `statistics` and `display.rows` contain every requested quantile. Optional `definitions` adds the adopted interpretation to display metadata. Existing saved results supply figures and prose through the existing gallery.

Official 0.11.4 sources: [IRM](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/irm.py), [PLIV](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/plm/pliv.py), [IIVM](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/iivm.py), [SSM and its native score](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/ssm.py), [SSM data](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/data/ssm_data.py), [QTE and paired model composition](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/qte.py), [potential quantiles](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/pq.py), [local potential quantiles](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/lpq.py), [CVaR](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/cvar.py), [APO and weights](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/irm/apo.py), [sample splitting](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/double_ml_sampling_mixins.py), and [native framework inference](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/0.11.4/doubleml/double_ml_framework.py). The existing causal requirements pin supplies these APIs.


## GRF heterogeneous treatment effects

`research_design.py select` binds `design="heterogeneous_effect"` to the adopted target. `cate`, `conditional_iv_effect` and `multi_arm_cate` select conditional output; `ate`, `att`, `aclate` and `multi_arm_ate` select their population aggregate. `survival_effect` also binds `target.quantity` to `conditional` or `average`. The proposal records target-implied option changes before adoption.

The existing official R path calls the pinned GRF 2.6.1 functions directly. `inference={"type":"official"}` and `regressors` bind the pretreatment effect covariates. `options.args` supplies native options, and `options.columns` binds vectors or ordered matrices to analysis columns. `quantity="average"` is the default: the main rows contain the native population aggregate and its standard error, with out-of-bag conditional predictions saved separately. `quantity="conditional"` makes the out-of-bag conditional predictions, native pointwise variances and normal intervals the main rows and executes no average-effect calculation. The existing `grf` default and the separate EconML `causal_forest` path retain their targets.

| Method | Required role bindings | Conditional target and aggregate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `grf` | `treatment` | `causal_forest`: binary treatment identifies `E[Y(1)-Y(0) | X=x]` under conditional exchangeability and overlap. Continuous treatment estimates `Cov[Y,W | X=x]/Var[W | X=x]`. Native `aggregation.target.sample` selects `all`, `treated`, `control` or `overlap` where supported; `aggregation.method` selects `AIPW` or `TMLE` under the native sample/cluster restrictions. |
| `grf_instrumental` | `treatment`, `instrument`, `definitions.iv_estimand` | `instrumental_forest`: `tau(x)=Cov[Y,Z | X=x]/Cov[W,Z | X=x]`. `iv_estimand="conditional_late"` requires binary 0/1 treatment and instrument plus a nonempty `definitions.compliance_population`; relevance, exclusion, conditional instrument exogeneity and monotonicity identify this conditional complier effect. `"conditional_iv_ratio"` retains the ratio target and its adopted structural interpretation. |
| `grf_multi_arm` | `treatment`, `arms` | `multi_arm_causal_forest`: `E[Y(a)-Y(reference) | X=x]` for every nonreference arm under conditional exchangeability and arm overlap. `arms` contains distinct strings in adopted order; its first element is the reference factor level. Every arm occurs in the fitted sample. The common run has one outcome, matching native pointwise variance support. |
| `grf_survival` | `treatment`, `status`, `definitions.time_unit`, `definitions.censoring`, `args.target`, `args.horizon` | `causal_survival_forest`: `target="RMST"` estimates the effect on `min(T,horizon)`; `"survival.probability"` estimates the survival-probability difference at that horizon. `status` is 1 for an observed event and 0 for right censoring. The positive finite horizon has `time_unit`. Conditional treatment exchangeability, overlap, independent right censoring given treatment and covariates, and follow-up support identify the target. Continuous treatment uses the corresponding conditional covariance-to-variance slope. |

The three additional forests aggregate through `average_treatment_effect(method="AIPW", target.sample="all")`. Instrumental aggregation requires a binary 0/1 instrument in the native `get_scores` implementation. Its complier aggregate is ACLATE, `E_X[tau(X)]`, using the declared covariate population and observation weights. A pooled compliance-share-weighted LATE has a different population weighting. Numeric instruments are supported for `quantity="conditional"`. Native `compliance.score`, `debiasing.weights` and `num.trees.for.weights` remain available through `aggregation`. The result distinguishes a conditional IV ratio from a conditional complier LATE. These R role bindings use empty top-level `endogenous` and `instruments` lists; the treatment and instrument columns are explicit options.

Multi-arm `columns.W.hat` names one propensity column per arm in `arms` order; the runner assigns those native matrix labels and checks positive probabilities summing to one. Native estimates retain their contrast labels. `predict.multi_arm_causal_forest` supplies a pointwise variance per contrast and `average_treatment_effect` supplies each contrast's standard error. The saved joint covariance is unavailable for multiple contrasts because those native returns contain marginal variances. A single contrast retains its scalar covariance. The survival result preserves event coding, horizon, native target, supplied failure-time grid and the adopted censoring definition. Binary survival-probability contrasts use probability-difference units; RMST and continuous-treatment slopes retain the adopted coefficient unit.

All four forests forward native honesty, nuisance predictions, tree settings, sample weights and clusters. Cluster columns are read as text and then factored, preserving their original labels. `equalize.cluster.weights=TRUE` gives every cluster equal weight and requires absent `sample.weights`; the default gives observations equal weight, or uses the supplied sample weights. Native `observation_weights` normalizes the resulting weights. `aggregation.subset` identifies a prespecified covariate-defined population; its IDs and target IDs are retained. The treated/control/overlap aggregate applies its own native score weights after this observation weighting. A conditional prediction, ATE, ATT, ACLATE and independently evaluated policy value keep their own definitions.

`result.heterogeneity` and `display.metadata.heterogeneity` save the conditional target, chosen output quantity, treatment type and contrasts, reference arm, IV/compliance population or survival target, aggregation, population, units, observation weights and identifying conditions. `diagnostics.cate` retains the original `grf` prediction owner; the added methods use `diagnostics.conditional_effects`. Predictions and weights reference `sample.included_ids`. Native forest options, aggregate subset IDs, cluster count, function signatures, package version and serialized forest accompany the results. The official R program lives in `research_causal.R`; Python loads it at execution, prefixes the existing shared-table reader and records the executed companion and reader-prelude hashes.
