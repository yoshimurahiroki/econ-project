# Executable causal and empirical models

`skills/econ-data/scripts/research_causal.py` implements the following finite set of specifications. The target, population, assignment mechanism, identifying restrictions and inference convention belong to the adopted specification. A fitted coefficient has a causal interpretation when those restrictions establish the stated target.

- `rdd_localrand`: restrict the sample to a prespecified symmetric window around the cutoff. Under complete randomization of assignment within that window, `scipy.stats.permutation_test` tests the individual sharp null by permuting assignment at the observed arm counts. An HC1 Wald interval describes the local mean contrast; excluded window IDs are recorded.
- `rdd_sharp`, `rdd_fuzzy`: a local linear regression on each side of a cutoff with triangular kernel and local quadratic bias correction. `rdrobust.rdrobust` supplies the bias-corrected jump and its robust nearest-neighbor covariance and interval. The fuzzy coefficient is the outcome jump divided by the treatment jump; its local complier interpretation requires continuity, exclusion, monotonicity and a nonzero treatment jump. The options select the cutoff, running variable and either an explicit positive bandwidth or `mserd`; fuzzy RDD adds the binary receipt column. Fitted bandwidths and local IDs are recorded.
- `rct`: under complete randomization with the observed treated and control counts, compare their means. `scipy.stats.permutation_test` permutes assignment to test the sharp null of zero treatment effect for every unit. The accompanying `statsmodels.OLS` HC1 covariance and Wald interval describe the average assignment contrast; the permutation p value is a separate sharp-null test.
- `gls`: y = Xβ + u with a specified diagonal variance matrix Ω. `statsmodels.GLS` minimizes (y−Xβ)'Ω⁻¹(y−Xβ); Ω is read from the positive `variance` column. Independent observations and correct conditional mean identify β. The supported covariance is HC1 or one-way cluster robust.
- `iv_gmm`: y = Xβ + Dτ + u, E[Z̃u] = 0, with Z̃ = [X,Z]. `linearmodels.iv.IVGMM` computes two-step heteroskedasticity-robust GMM; valid excluded instruments and the rank condition identify τ. The output includes the overidentification J statistic and full covariance. This is conventional GMM inference, not weak-instrument-robust inference.
- `iv_ar`: the same linear IV model with one endogenous regressor, independent homoskedastic normal disturbances and fixed instruments. For each candidate τ, regress y−Dτ on X and Z and test the joint zero excluded-instrument coefficients using the exact F reference. Analytic inversion of this Anderson–Rubin test solves a quadratic inequality; the returned confidence set can be bounded, disconnected, empty or unbounded. A null endpoint denotes infinity. Conventional 2SLS standard errors and Wald intervals are saved separately and do not replace that confidence set.
- `iv_ar_robust`: `ivDiag::AR_test` 1.0.8 or later inverts the heteroskedastic or clustered Anderson–Rubin test for one endogenous-regressor coefficient. Supply excluded instruments, exogenous controls, adopted fixed effects, weights and cluster columns. The official confidence-set components, bounds, empty-set classification and null test are saved. `ivDiag::ivDiag(bootstrap=FALSE,run.AR=FALSE,parallel=FALSE)` supplies the corresponding first stage, reduced form and conventional 2SLS result on the same complete-case sample. Their official 95% Wald intervals retain their own level beside the AR set's chosen level.
- `distribution_effect`: define Z_i = 1{y_i ≤ c} at the prespecified threshold c. `statsmodels.OLS` of Z on a binary randomized treatment and an intercept gives the treated-minus-control outcome CDF at c with HC1 or cluster covariance. Random assignment identifies the marginal potential-outcome CDF contrast at that threshold; the interval is pointwise.
- `multitreatment_did`: in a balanced two-period panel, estimate Δy_i = α + X_i,preβ + Σ_k τ_k D_ik,post + ε_i with `statsmodels.OLS`. Each intervention or dose is zero before treatment, at least one unit has all zero post doses, and controls are measured before treatment. The separate linear coefficients describe simultaneous interventions and dose under conditional parallel trends, no anticipation and additive effects; inference is HC1 or one-way clustered at the unit-change level.
- `generalized_synth`: `gsynth::gsynth` estimates an interactive fixed-effects counterfactual with a specified factor count and unit and time effects. A balanced panel contains never-treated units and one common absorbing adoption date with at least five pretreatment periods. A seeded parametric bootstrap provides the joint covariance of the average effect and event-time effects. Identification requires a stable factor structure, adequate untreated comparison units and an appropriate error model.
- `staggered_did`: `did::att_gt` estimates group-time ATT and `did::aggte` supplies the adopted `dynamic`, `simple`, `group` or `calendar` aggregation. The runner forwards panel/repeated-cross-section structure, unbalanced-panel handling, comparison group, anticipation, base period, sampling weights, weight timing, score, clustering, multiplier bootstrap, repetitions, significance level and simultaneous-band options. Existing specifications retain `panel=TRUE`, `allow_unbalanced_panel=FALSE`, `anticipation=0`, `base_period="varying"`, `est_method="dr"`, `bstrap=FALSE`, `cband=FALSE` and dynamic aggregation. Package preprocessing determines the fitted observations; the result saves those IDs and its exclusions. Each group-time cell saves its actual base period, eligible comparison sample, official normalized sampling weights, original input weights and role (`pre_diagnostic`, `anticipation`, `att` or normalized `reference`). Each aggregate saves its contributing cohorts, periods, target sample and normalized cohort weights. Saved effective aggregation conditions follow the executed did version: 2.5.1 balances dynamic effects by calendar reach after missing ATT cells are removed; 2.5.1.906 and later require common nonmissing event-time support and apply the group `min_e` window. Positive group `min_e` is rejected before estimation on earlier versions. The runtime did version and effective aggregation conditions are recorded. Standard errors and band critical values come from the official fitted objects. The saved joint covariance is the analytical covariance of the official influence functions with the adopted clustering; bootstrap standard errors remain separate saved quantities. See the versioned [att_gt source](https://github.com/cran/did/blob/453a1be859411edc1c8933891940cb3a570d7095/R/att_gt.R), [aggregation source](https://github.com/cran/did/blob/453a1be859411edc1c8933891940cb3a570d7095/R/compute.aggte.R), [updated aggregation source, did 2.5.1.906](https://github.com/bcallaway11/did/blob/9ead26b9d37c7f2bc202bebc4f5e8220ab7e8c13/R/compute.aggte.R), [att_gt interface](https://bcallaway11.github.io/did/reference/att_gt.html) and [aggte interface](https://bcallaway11.github.io/did/reference/aggte.html).
- `synthetic_control`: fit nonnegative donor weights summing to one with `pysyncon.Synth` on a specified training period, using equal predictor importance for the training outcomes. The post-treatment effect is the mean treated-minus-synthetic gap in post periods minus its mean in held-out untreated validation periods. `statsmodels.OLS` HC1 covariance across these held-out time gaps treats fitted weights as fixed; stable untreated expected gaps, untreated donors, and independent held-out time errors identify this specified contrast. Record weights, pre-fit errors, period gaps and time partitions. Save the calculated actual and synthetic trajectories as `display.kind="scm"` and their gap as `displays.scm_gap`; rows use calendar `time`, `estimate` and the trajectory `component`. Metadata records donor weights and the first observed post-treatment time. The series preserve the calculated paths; the fitted post-versus-validation contrast retains its separate coefficient inference.
- `dynamic_panel`: y_it = ρ y_i,t−1 + X_itβ + α_i + u_it. `pydynpd.regression.abond` fits two-step difference GMM with y levels at lags 2–3 as instruments for the differenced lag and declared strictly exogenous X as standard instruments. A balanced consecutive panel with independent units, valid lag moments and no serial correlation in level errors supplies the identification. The result records the Windmeijer-corrected covariance, Hansen test, first- and second-order differenced residual tests, and IDs excluded for initial lags.
- `tobit`: latent y* = Xβ + σu, u standard normal, and observed y = max(c,y*) at a specified left-censoring threshold c. The `statsmodels.GenericLikelihoodModel` likelihood uses the normal log density for uncensored observations and `scipy.special.log_ndtr` for censored probabilities, estimating β and log σ by BFGS. The full likelihood covariance includes log σ; the result records the censored count and convergence. Normality, homoskedasticity and exogeneity identify the latent conditional slope.
- `network_spillover`: specify y_i = α + X_iβ + τD_i + γΣ_j w_ijD_j + u_i. `libpysal` constructs the row-standardized neighbor exposure from a known edge file and `statsmodels.OLS` fits the joint direct and spillover contrasts. Random assignment, a correct exposure mapping and independent errors or declared independent clusters establish the specified comparison. The output records every exposure and the edge-file hash.
- `policy_tree`: `econml.policy.DRPolicyTree` learns a binary treatment rule on a distinct training partition with cross-fitted nuisance models. On the evaluation partition, form the inverse-probability-weighted difference in outcomes between that rule and treating nobody, using the known assignment probabilities. A `statsmodels.OLS` intercept and HC1 covariance estimate the holdout value gain. Identification requires independent training and evaluation samples, randomized or conditionally unconfounded assignment, positivity and correct propensities; the interval is conditional on the trained rule. The output records training IDs and evaluation actions.
- `shift_share`: form Z_i = Σ_k s_ik g_k using specified nonnegative shares that sum to one and fixed shocks, then estimate a one-endogenous-variable linear IV model with `linearmodels.iv.IV2SLS`. The stated supported design conditions on shocks and assumes independent unit errors or one-way independent clusters, instrument exogeneity and relevance; this covariance is not shock-level inference. The first-stage partial R² and test statistic are saved.
- `spatial_lag`: y = ρWy + Xβ + u with an explicitly supplied network W and independent Gaussian innovations. `spreg.ML_Lag` fits the spatial autoregressive model by maximum likelihood, reports the full covariance and simple impact multipliers. The edge CSV has exactly `source,target,weight`; every observation has a positive-weight neighbor, self-edges are excluded and weights are row-standardized. Exogenous X and a known W identify the parametric model. The estimate of ρ is dimensionless.
- `causal_forest`: `econml.dml.CausalForestDML` uses cross-fitted random forest nuisance predictions and an honest forest to estimate heterogeneous binary-treatment effects τ(x). Conditional exchangeability and treatment overlap identify these effects. The engine returns individual fitted CATEs and intervals, plus the sample-average CATE and its population-summary standard error bound; the latter is not the covariance of all individual predictions. The same sample defines the target aggregation. The code fixes two folds, one thread, and the specified seed, tree count and minimum leaf size.
- `hdfe`: the same additive fixed-effect linear model with any declared list of effect columns; `pyfixest.feols` absorbs the effects and uses the adopted analytic/frequency weights, small-sample correction and heteroskedastic or one-way CRV1/CRV3 covariance. Singleton observations are retained; collinear regressors or dropped observations cause rejection. The full covariance and pointwise intervals come from the fitted object at the adopted confidence level. Fitted row IDs and weight values retain their input order; row count and frequency-weight total are saved separately.
- `panel_fe`: y_it = X_itβ + α_i + λ_t + u_it. `linearmodels.panel.PanelOLS` absorbs the selected entity and/or time effects. Identification requires sufficient within variation and strict conditional exogeneity; entity-clustered covariance allows within-entity dependence. Entity-time pairs must be unique.
- `quantile`: Q_q(y|X) = Xβ_q. `statsmodels.QuantReg` minimizes the check loss at the chosen q, with the engine's robust kernel covariance, Epanechnikov kernel and Hall–Sheather bandwidth. β_q is a conditional quantile contrast, and causal interpretation requires the relevant exogeneity and quantile specification.
- `poisson`: E[y|X] = exp(Xβ) for nonnegative y, including continuous outcomes and observed zeros. `statsmodels.GLM` with the Poisson log link solves the PPML score Σ_i X_i[y_i−exp(X_iβ)] = 0 using the intercept and declared regressors. The conditional-mean restriction, exogeneity and full-rank variation identify β as a log-mean contrast. The existing HC1 or one-way cluster covariance supplies inference without imposing equality of the conditional mean and variance; negative outcomes are rejected. ECON-03 connects this mean specification to `fixest::fepois` for adopted models with absorbed effects and offsets.
- `logit`: P(y=1|X) = exp(Xβ)/(1+exp(Xβ)). `statsmodels.GLM` fits the binomial logit likelihood and returns robust covariance. β is a conditional log-odds contrast under the specified conditional probability and exogeneity.

## Specification and result

Run `python skills/econ-data/scripts/research_causal.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json`. A specification contains exactly these fields: `method`, `data`, `id`, `outcome`, `regressors`, `endogenous`, `instruments`, `population`, `period`, `unit`, `estimand`, `identification`, `assignment`, `inference`, and `options`. Data is a CSV path relative to the specification, IDs are unique and nonempty, and the model uses complete cases. Regressor, endogenous and instrument fields are lists of distinct column names; Python numerical estimators require numeric columns, and the official R function determines its supported factor encoding. `endogenous` and `instruments` are nonempty for `iv_gmm`, `iv_ar` and `iv_ar_robust`; both Anderson–Rubin methods require exactly one endogenous regressor. `shift_share` requires exactly one endogenous regressor and an empty instruments list. Regression specifications include an intercept except where fixed effects or differencing absorb it. `unit` names the coefficient scale, such as outcome units, log mean or log odds.

`inference` is `{"type":"gsynth"}` for generalized synthetic control, `{"type":"did"}` for staggered DID, `{"type":"synthetic_holdout"}` for synthetic control, `{"type":"dynamic_gmm"}` for dynamic panel, `{"type":"likelihood"}` for Tobit, `{"type":"policy"}` for policy learning and evaluation, `{"type":"spatial_ml"}` for the spatial model, `{"type":"forest"}` for causal forest, `{"type":"rdrobust"}` for RDD, `{"type":"randomization"}` for RCT and local-randomization RDD, `{"type":"robust"}` or `{"type":"cluster","cluster":"column"}`; `iv_ar` requires `{"type":"homoskedastic"}`. Quantile supports only its robust kernel covariance; panel fixed effects require clustering by entity. Distribution-effect options are `{"treatment":"column","threshold":0}` and the regressors list contains only treatment. Multiple-treatment DID uses robust or cluster inference and options `{"entity":"column","time":"column","pre":0,"post":1,"treatments":["d1","d2","dose"]}`. Generalized synthetic-control options specify `{"entity":"column","time":"column","treatment":"column","factors":1,"bootstraps":50,"seed":42}`. Staggered DID requires `{"entity":"column","time":"column","cohort":"column","control_group":"nevertreated"}`; `notyettreated` is also accepted. Optional fields use the official names `panel`, `allow_unbalanced_panel`, `anticipation`, `weightsname`, `fix_weights`, `alp`, `bstrap`, `biters`, `clustervars`, `cband`, `est_method`, `base_period`, `faster_mode`, plus a nonnegative integer `seed`. `aggregation` contains the official `aggte` arguments `type`, `balance_e`, `min_e`, `max_e`, `na.rm`, `bstrap`, `biters`, `cband`, `alp` and `clustervars`. Aggregation clustering must match the first-stage clustering. Simultaneous bands require multiplier bootstrap. Period codes are consecutive integers; an unbalanced panel can omit unit-period observations. A repeated-cross-section input uses a distinct `entity` value for each observation, separate from the row ID column. The runner preserves the project R startup profile and inherited library environment. The did branch requires did >= 2.5.1; saved runtime fields record the actual R library paths, working directory and `R_PROFILE_USER` value. Synthetic-control options specify `{"entity":"column","time":"column","treated":"unit","training_end":23,"pre_end":39}`; the regressors list contains the time column. Dynamic panel options specify `{"entity":"column","time":"column"}`. Tobit options specify `{"lower":0}`. Network options specify `{"weights":"edges.csv","treatment":"column"}`. Policy options specify `{"treatment":"column","propensity":"column","partition":"column","seed":512}`; the partition column is `train` or `evaluation`. Shift-share options specify `{"shares":["s1","s2"],"shocks":[-1,1]}`. Local-randomization options specify `{"running":"column","cutoff":0,"bandwidth":0.15,"permutations":1999,"seed":202}`. Spatial options specify `{"weights":"edges.csv"}` relative to the specification. Forest options specify `{"treatment":"column","seed":404,"trees":100,"min_leaf":10}`; regressors name the effect covariates. RDD options are `{"running":"column","cutoff":0,"bandwidth":"mserd"}` (add `"treatment":"column"` for fuzzy RDD); the regressors list contains only the running variable. RCT options specify `{"treatment":"column","permutations":1999,"seed":123}` and its regressors list contains only that treatment column. `options` is `{}` for IV GMM, Poisson and logit; `{"variance":"column"}` for GLS; `{"fixed_effects":["entity","time"]}` for HDFE; `{"alpha":0.05}` for IV Anderson–Rubin confidence sets; `{"quantile":0.5}` for quantile regression; or `{"entity":"column","time":"column","entity_effects":true,"time_effects":true}` for panel fixed effects. At least one panel effect must be selected.

The result records actual engine versions, named coefficients, standard errors, full covariance, intervals at the adopted level, p values, fitted and excluded IDs, input-row and fitted-observation counts, numerical diagnostics, adopted definitions, input hashes and semantic `statistics` with units. Writes use the existing state atomic-save path. `case --output NEWDIR` generates independent seeded data, specifications, results and preset numerical comparisons for the original twenty-four specifications using random seed 10198 and method-specific seeds; the directory must not already exist. Add repeatable `--method` arguments to execute only the requested original examples, for example `case --method staggered_did --output NEWDIR`. The selected examples retain the same random stream, inputs, specifications and numerical comparisons as the complete case; only their required CSV inputs and results are published. These are simulated evaluations and do not change an existing research project's specification or output.

For HDFE, `options.fixed_effects` remains required. Optional flat fields are `weights` (the analysis column), `weights_type="aweights"` or `"fweights"`, `ssc` with official keys `k_adj`, `k_fixef`, `G_adj`, `G_df`, `vcov`, and `confidence_level`. Weights must be positive and finite; frequency weights must be integers. The complete-case sample includes the weight column. Robust inference accepts `vcov="hetero"` or `"HC1"`; clustered inference accepts `{"CRV1":"column"}` or `{"CRV3":"column"}` matching the single declared cluster column. Defaults remain unweighted, `aweights`, official SSC, hetero/CRV1 and 95%. The runner calls `confint(alpha=1-confidence_level)`, retains singletons and verifies the official row IDs. `sample.observations` counts rows; `diagnostics.effective_observations` retains the official frequency total where applicable. The [current official feols interface](https://github.com/py-econometrics/pyfixest/blob/2fb86f30a65f4ad9fa70b1e69bc8da6d0bbcd5db/pyfixest/estimation/api/feols.py) defines these weight, covariance and SSC arguments.

The existing `research.py estimate` owns OLS, 2SLS, partially linear DML and two-period DML DID. Use its own specification and result contract for those estimators.


## Select the implementing function

Run `python skills/econ-data/scripts/research_causal.py select DESIGN.json --output SELECTION.json`. Supply `question`, `estimand`, `identification`, `design`, `outcome_scale`, `data_structure`, `treatment_pattern` and `dependence`; each is a nonempty string. `options` carries the proposed native arguments. The output supplies `method`, `engine`, `entry_point`, determining conditions, the selected method's quantity and the unchanged options. Selection executes no estimation.

An optional `model_contract_id` resolves through the existing knowledge reader and the catalog's `selection_binding`. It carries the full input/output/equation contract, body locators and hashes, actual check/result provenance and knowledge prerequisites into `method_selection`. The existing structural branch checks the requested target, declared economic model and numerical method, then matches its runner command or official function to the contract implementation. A conflicting declared model or target is rejected. Contracts without a selector binding return `implementation_required`, with `theory_only` or `implementation_only` identifying the existing content. For bound growth, market, dynamic and finite numerical models, a matching `adopted_specification` is projected into the selected owner's public run schema; missing fields remain explicit. The adopted object is returned unchanged. The [finite numerical contracts](numerical-models.md#finite-economic-model-execution) connect correlated equilibrium, Shapley/core allocation, interbank clearing and multitask incentives to `research_numerics.py run`, with the existing function signatures determining required input names. The replacement runner records its actual NFXP, CCP, finite-horizon and scenario components, plus SMM when the adopted options request it.

`MC-aiyagari-stationary` selects the existing macro runner's finite-grid stationary equilibrium, with `numerical.solution_method="policy_iteration_brentq"` and target `equilibrium` or `welfare`. The adopted calibration, baseline/scenario productivity and solver tolerances pass to its exact public specification. `MC-hank-one-asset` selects the official SSJ `Block` operation named by `numerical.operation`: `solve_steady_state`, `solve_jacobian`, `solve_impulse_linear` or `solve_impulse_nonlinear`; these entries target `equilibrium` and return their distinct object contracts. The official `dag()` example performs its public steady-state calibration. A chosen model and saved steady state must carry the adopted grids, parameters and population into each operation. `stochastic_rbc` retains the existing observed-productivity/local-policy runner; `implementation="dolo"` selects official time iteration for that same RBC model. `dolo_yaml` accepts another explicitly declared native model and assigns no RBC contract to it. External Dolo entries target equilibrium solution, retain calibration and save final-policy original residuals alongside the solver's returned diagnostics.

`adopted_specification` accepts the existing analysis specification as an object and returns that object unchanged. The selector derives its adopted method from `method` or `model`; an explicit `adopted_method` must agree. Supply `population`, `comparison_group`, `observation_unit`, `assignment_unit`, `inference_unit`, `period`, `assignment`, `weights` and `inference` to retain those definitions in `design_contract`. `unit` remains the existing specification's coefficient scale; `observation_unit` names the observed economic unit. Definitions already present in the adopted specification are carried forward, including `options.definitions` and population/weights declared in `target`. DID comparison-group and weight definitions and native weight arguments are also read from its existing options. The economic chain uses these recovered weights and inference units. Changed methods, estimands, identification statements, supplied scientific definitions or options produce `status="design_change"` with the adopted and selected values and their stated estimand relation. The selector does not rewrite or execute the adopted specification.

The design values are `conditional_mean`, `did`, `rdd`, `density_at_cutoff`, `rd_power`, `iv` (also `instrumental_variables`), `weak_instrument_inference`, `synthetic_control`, `generalized_synth`, `orthogonal_score`, `heterogeneous_effect`, `policy_learning`, `parallel_trend_sensitivity`, `wild_cluster_inference`, `small_cluster_inference` and `structural`. Conditional means use `outcome_scale="level"` or `"proportional_nonnegative"`. DID uses `treatment_pattern="two_period"`, `"absorbing_staggered"`, `"reversals"`, `"changing_dose"` or `"treatment_histories"`; two-period data use `data_structure="panel"` or `"repeated_cross_section"`. Orthogonal-score selection takes `score="plr"` or `"did"` and returns the existing `research.py estimate` owner. Structural selection takes `structural_model` and returns its existing market, dynamic, macro or numerical owner.

The chain retains the economic decision, target, population/observations, identifying comparison, estimator/weights/numerics and function/options. `forward_options` preserves the supplied or inherited native options. `official_implementation.options` returns the proposed target-consistent options, and `option_changes` records each changed effective setting. Existing top-level fields remain available. `identification` is retained as source prose; its words never select a package. Selection records `executed=false` and leaves the adopted specification unchanged.

`target.kind` must match the selected mathematical target. A two-period DID targets ATT; an absorbing event study targets its stated ATT aggregation; a changing-path estimator targets the history contrast or normalized dose effect; sharp RD targets the cutoff effect and fuzzy RD additionally supports LATE. Forest CATEs and policy value use their respective branches. A partially linear coefficient implements ATE only with an explicit constant-effect restriction. The selector rejects a target outside that method's contract. Declared `continuity=false` rejects continuity-based RDD; `exclusion=false` rejects IV moments, instrument scores and their test inversion; `overlap=false` rejects selection-on-observables and policy/forest comparisons requiring that support. Fuzzy RD additionally requires exclusion and local monotonicity. The selector keeps adopted structured conditions when the request does not replace them. New results and selections carry `result_format_version=1`.

`official_implementation.execution_mode="external_official_entry"` identifies a verified official interface called from the existing project analysis. This mode does not claim a built-in runner execution. Heteroskedastic or clustered weak-IV inference selects the existing `iv_ar_robust` runner, which calls [`ivDiag::AR_test` 1.0.8](https://github.com/apoorvalal/ivDiag/blob/cbc391f4b1013c837743593bcde54efd5d733fb7/R/AR_test.R) with one endogenous treatment, excluded instruments, controls, fixed effects, weights and the adopted cluster columns. `options.args.CI=true` requests the official confidence set; `options.args.alpha` must agree with the requested confidence level. The function uses robust covariance without `cl` and clustered covariance with `cl`; its one-instrument implementation solves the quadratic inequality, and its multiple-instrument implementation uses a transformed whole-line search and boundary refinement. The runner preserves the returned endpoints, disconnected pieces, unbounded pieces and empty-set classification.

Supply the actual analysis columns in `inference.cluster` (one name) or `inference.clusters` (a list); the prose label `inference_unit="state"` names the economic unit and does not bind a column. The selector passes DID clusters to both `att_gt.clustervars` and `aggte.clustervars`, and passes robust IV clusters to `AR_test.cl`. DID accepts the entity column and one additional independent cluster. An explicit `comparison.confidence_level`, `inference.confidence_level` or `inference.alpha` sets the corresponding native level; conflicting level and alpha are rejected. A simultaneous DID family sets `cband=true` and `bstrap=true` at estimation and aggregation, whereas a pointwise family sets `cband=false`. Cluster-robust analytical inference remains available in did 2.5.1; clustering alone does not force a bootstrap. A two-period design requiring clustering or a level/family beyond DRDID's saved 95% pointwise output uses the existing group-time runner. The selector retains native changes in `option_changes` and marks changes to an adopted specification as proposals. The [versioned did interface](https://github.com/cran/did/blob/453a1be859411edc1c8933891940cb3a570d7095/R/att_gt.R) defines these arguments.

Each alternative's `eligibility.excluded_by` lists contradictions with the requested target, panel/history structure, dependence and required error model. `requires_identifying_conditions` means its listed conditions must be established from the economic design and observations. A compatibility match does not establish identification. For example, independent-unit DRDID inference is excluded for state-clustered sampling, and exact Anderson–Rubin F inversion requires explicit independent homoskedastic normal errors.

For clustered designs, `conditions.cluster_count`, `treated_cluster_count` and `common_shocks` accompany the assignment and inference units in `method_selection.inference.cluster_design`. Counts refer to the declared design and must match the fitted sample. The inference comparison returns the existing `wild_cluster_bootstrap` and `club_sandwich` entries with their supported saved-model classes, native options and required contrast/null. The former accepts the runner's saved `lm` or linear `fixest` fits; the latter comparison specifies `CR2` with Satterthwaite inference for a saved `lm`. Their official APIs determine each model's support. The selector applies no fixed cluster-count cutoff and preserves the adopted inference. It does not substitute linear-regression bootstrap or CR2 output for group-time DID or weak-IV test inversion. See [boottest.fixest](https://s3alfisc.github.io/fwildclusterboot/reference/boottest.fixest.html), [coef_test](https://jepusto.github.io/clubSandwich/reference/coef_test.html) and [vcovCR.lm](https://jepusto.github.io/clubSandwich/reference/vcovCR.lm.html).

For `fixest_ols`, `fixest_ppml` and `sunab`, an adopted cluster binding reaches a cluster formula in `args.vcov` with the actual cluster columns. The requested level reaches the runner's `confidence_level` and official pointwise `confint` calculation. A simultaneous family requires its own implemented inference procedure. For RDD, the same declared level reaches `rdrobust::rdrobust(level=...)` in percentage units; one independent cluster partition binds through `columns.cluster`. Fuzzy RD requires the actual receipt column in `columns.fuzzy` or the explicit native receipt vector. Missing receipt/cluster bindings remain in `unbound_inputs`. The selected RD result uses the official robust bias-corrected estimate and pointwise interval.

For `implementation="pyfixest"`, the conditional-mean selector passes the declared weight column and confidence level into the flat HDFE options and binds one cluster column to native CRV1 or the adopted CRV3. Its executable inference object uses the existing `robust` or `cluster` schema. A simultaneous interval family or multiple cluster partitions require their own implemented procedure. Native `weights_type`, `ssc` and covariance choices remain in the proposed specification and the adopted object remains unchanged.

The selector also accepts the existing run fields `data`, `id`, `outcome`, `regressors`, `endogenous` and `instruments`, or recovers them from `adopted_specification`. For an existing causal runner, `official_implementation.execution_specification` returns its exact public run schema, proposed native options, required command and missing field/cluster bindings. `status="specified"` denotes completed input binding; the runner then validates the data and executes the official estimator. `adoption_required` preserves a changed adopted specification. External official entries retain their own constructor and output contract. `official_implementation.dependencies` resolves the existing registry's requirements resources and paths; those files remain the sole package-version authority. Bind selected pins and the requirement file through the existing research runtime, or use `runtime.requirements` when restoring its complete family.

External score entries include `doubleml.DoubleMLIRM` (`ATE`/`ATTE`), `DoubleMLPLIV` (partially linear IV) and `DoubleMLIIVM` (`LATE`). The selector chooses these from the target and score conditions and returns the required nuisance/fold/input contract. Existing PLR and two-period DID continue through `research.py`. A requested Rust replacement MPEC returns the official `ruspy.get_criterion_function` contract with `init_dict.method="MPEC"` and its returned criterion, derivatives and Bellman constraints. BLP uses GMM; finite-state game selection accepts its implemented NFXP/MPEC; latent replacement uses EM; entry inequalities and share inversion retain their actual algorithms. `fixes::att` supplies the external `simple` aggregation when the requested fixes target is overall ATT.

For external DoubleML scores, the selected constructor `score` is materialized in `official_implementation.options`. ATT sets `score="ATTE"`; ATE sets `"ATE"`, and the instrumental interactive model sets `"LATE"`. The [official IRM interface](https://github.com/DoubleML/doubleml-for-py/blob/a3493c3b7a444b6ddd730ad05c2c0e6c67ac19c3/doubleml/irm/irm.py) permits its `weights` argument only for the weighted ATE score. An ATT request with that constructor weight argument is rejected. Preserve sampling weights, target-population weights and residual-treatment weighting as distinct economic objects.

For `design="structural"`, `structural_model="finite_horizon_choice"` selects the official respy criterion for `target.kind="structural_parameter"`. `numerical.estimation_method="smm"` selects the weighted simulated-moment distance; `"maximum_likelihood"` selects the mean log likelihood. `structural_model="lifecycle_choice"` selects `lcm.Model.solve` and `Model.simulate` for `target.kind="policy_value"` or `"welfare"`, using `numerical.estimation_method="backward_induction"` and fixed or previously estimated parameters. These are `external_official_entry` contracts with `executed=false`. Their [finite-horizon and lifecycle input contract](dynamic-models.md#official-finite-horizon-and-lifecycle-selection) preserves states, actions, transitions, normalizations, seeds and aggregation. Lifecycle selection rejects `equilibrium` and structural-parameter estimation. Finite-horizon estimation rejects policy and welfare targets. Adopted specifications remain unchanged; proposed `return_scalar=false` supplies the official criterion's named output for saved diagnostics.

## Official R calls in the same runner

These methods use the same `run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json` command and common scientific specification fields above. Set `inference={"type":"official"}`. Put the selected function's named options in `options.args`. Bind a function argument to a CSV column through `options.columns`, for example `{"i.weights":"sampling_weight"}` or `{"cluster":"state"}`. A list of column names binds a matrix. An argument appears in exactly one of `args` and `columns`. `options.seed` sets the R seed. The runner checks the installed function's `formals`, rejects unsupported names, invokes the official function and saves its returned numerical objects and native fit in the result JSON. The native fit includes the analysis data needed by official post-estimation. Files stay in the current project result lifecycle. Display metadata contains sample counts, model definitions and aggregation weights; observation-ID arrays stay in the result sample and diagnostics.

The runner accepts `options.definitions` for the adopted observation/inference unit, comparison eligibility, treatment history, weighting and aggregation definitions. Saved metadata combines these definitions with actual functions, argument bindings, package versions, reference periods and interval families. Package output determines fitted counts and identities. When an aggregate-only API does not export fitted row identities, save the input IDs with `included_ids_available=false`; do not relabel every input row as a fitted observation. Cross-horizon covariance is saved only when returned by the selected official interface.

For `iv_ar_robust`, `inference.confidence_sets` and `diagnostics.anderson_rubin` preserve the official connected components. An unbounded endpoint is null with its corresponding display flag; `set_type` records bounded, disconnected, unbounded, real-line or empty output. The main `display.kind="iv_confidence_set"` uses those endpoints. `displays.iv_first_stage`, `iv_reduced_form` and `iv_2sls` hold the official stage estimates, standard errors and 95% intervals. All stages reference `sample.included_ids`, the same complete-case sample over outcome, treatment, instruments, controls, fixed effects, clusters and weights. Excluded IDs, cluster columns/counts, actual arguments and package versions accompany the results. `definitions.first_stage_unit` and `definitions.reduced_form_unit` supply the desired stage units; the defaults express the outcome or treatment column per instrument unit. The native serialized result retains both the AR result and the official stage object. The stage function uses its own fixed 1.96 Wald multiplier and 95% level; the requested AR alpha governs the main confidence set.

`diagnostics.first_stage_tests` preserves the official names `F.standard`, `F.robust`, `F.cluster` and `F.effective` with their returned values. Match the test to the adopted disturbance and clustering model when interpreting instrument strength; the Anderson–Rubin set retains its own test inversion. In fixest 0.14.2, `sunab` is evaluated inside the `feols` formula namespace. The runner obtains that helper's documented arguments with `getFromNamespace` and executes the official formula, preserving the package's export boundary.

For `rdrobust_r`, omit `options.plot` to execute the original estimation path. A supplied object accepts native `rdplot` options `p`, `nbins`, `binselect`, `scale`, `kernel`, `h`, `covs_eval`, `covs_drop`, `ginv.tol`, `support`, `masspoints` and `ci`; omitted values use the official plotting defaults. Outcome, running variable, cutoff and supplied weight/covariate/subset inputs are inherited from the fitted specification, and `hide=TRUE` suppresses rendering during analysis. `displays.rdd` stores official `vars_bins` means, bin boundaries, counts, SEs and returned pointwise CI endpoints; its `component="line"` rows store official `vars_poly` coordinates. Both polynomial sides retain their cutoff endpoint with an explicit `side`, assigned from the official left-grid/right-grid output order. Metadata records the plotting function, package version, native options, cutoff, plotting and estimation bandwidths, bin counts and interval target. Bin intervals refer to binned means; polynomial intervals have availability `false`. The original `rdrobust` estimate and native fit remain the estimation result. The gallery consumes these saved coordinates. Implementation source: [`rdplot` 4.1.1](https://github.com/rdpackages/rdrobust/blob/0e990b829bc123334b5227f1ae7d820faff14726/R/rdrobust/R/rdplot.R).

The RDD sample records the official post-preprocessing side counts `N` and effective local counts `N_h`. The official object does not export fitted row identifiers, so `included_ids_available=false` and `sample.input_ids` retains the supplied rows. `diagnostics.bandwidth_input_ids` identifies input scores within the returned estimation bandwidths; it is distinct from fitted-row membership after auxiliary-variable and subset processing. The estimator's count and each plot's own count accompany their saved quantities.

For `fixes_event_study`, `never_treated` (default 0) is mapped to the package's required `NA` cohort code and recorded in `effective_options.cohort_coding`. The inspected fixes SA fit accepts neither covariates nor sampling weights, and its CS fit supplies unadjusted unit-level comparisons. The runner rejects those unsupported nuisance/weight/cluster combinations; the corresponding `sunab` formula term in `fixest::feols` or `did::att_gt` contract supplies them. The BJS, TWM and FLEX fits also do not forward sampling weights, so the runner rejects `weights` in `args` or `columns` for those estimators. DID rows use `role="pre_diagnostic"`, `"reference"`, `"anticipation"` or `"att"`, retaining the actual base period and interval family in metadata.
