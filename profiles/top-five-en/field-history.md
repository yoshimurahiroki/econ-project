Railroads and Mobility in the Orrin Valley, 1880 to 1940
I. Archival Sources
We digitize the census manuscripts of the Orrin Valley for 1880, 1900, 1920, and 1940. The manuscripts list each resident's name, age, birthplace, occupation, and household position, and the four censuses contain 3.4 million person records. The Orrin Railway Company ledgers report the opening date of each of 187 stations between 1862 and 1938, and we geocode each station to its county. The occupation of each worker maps to an income score from the 1940 census, which reports earnings by occupation.
II. Linking Fathers and Sons
We link each father in the 1880 census to his sons in the 1900, 1920, and 1940 censuses. The linking algorithm compares first name, last name, birth year, and birthplace, and it accepts a pair if the match score exceeds 0.92 and exceeds the score of the second-best candidate by 0.05. The algorithm links 41 percent of fathers aged 25 to 55 in 1880 to one or more sons, which produces 482,000 father-son pairs. We weight each pair by the inverse of the predicted linking probability, which depends on name frequency, birthplace, and household size. The weighted sample matches the occupation distribution of the full 1880 census within 1.1 percentage points in every one of 14 occupation groups.
III. Measures of Mobility
We rank fathers and sons by income score within their census year and cohort. The rank-rank slope, the coefficient of the son's income rank on the father's income rank, equals 0.31 (s.e. 0.01) in the linked sample. The upward mobility of a county equals the expected income rank of sons whose fathers rank at the 25th percentile, which equals 41.2 in the average county and ranges from 33.8 to 47.9 across the 61 counties of the valley.
