Delayed Retirement and the Employment of Younger Workers
Pension age reforms extend the working lives of older workers. This paper measures the effect of the extension on the employment of younger workers. A 2011 reform in Marden raised the pension eligibility age from 60 to 63 for workers born on or after January 1, 1958. Administrative records on every employee and firm show that the reform raises employment at ages 60 to 62 by 14.2 percentage points. Each older worker retained at a firm reduces hiring of workers under 30 by 0.31 and leaves hiring of workers aged 30 to 49 unchanged. Firms with deep promotion ladders and high shares of older workers account for 71 percent of the decline in youth hiring. Entry wages stay constant, and the adjustment runs through quantities. Net employment rises by 6.9 persons for every 10 older workers retained.
