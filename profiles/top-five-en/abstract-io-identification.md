Identifying Heterogeneity in Demand from Diversion Data
We study the identification of differentiated products demand when the analyst observes market shares, prices, and the share of consumers who switch to each product after a stockout. Switching shares deliver the cross-price derivatives of demand at observed prices, and we show that these derivatives identify the joint distribution of consumer preferences over any two products. With exogenous cost shifters, the demand system and the distribution of unobserved product quality are identified with zero parametric restrictions on either. The identification argument yields a two-step estimator that solves a linear inverse problem in each market. We apply the estimator to 3,800 retail markets in Marden and recover price elasticities that decline with income at a rate of 0.17 per income decile.
KEYWORDS: Demand estimation, differentiated products, identification, diversion, nonparametric methods.
