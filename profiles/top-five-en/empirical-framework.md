III. Conceptual Framework
This section develops a model of a firm that receives older workers through a retention shock. The model delivers the object of interest, the displacement ratio, as a function of one primitive and generates four predictions that the empirical sections test.
A. Production and Hiring
Firm j produces output Qj from efficiency units of labor Lj. Each young worker supplies one efficiency unit, and each older worker supplies θj units, with θj in (0, 1). Output equals
Qj = Aj Ljγ,   Lj = yj + θj oj,   (1)
where yj counts young workers, oj counts older workers, Aj is firm productivity, and γ in (0, 1) governs returns to scale. The firm pays the young wage wy and the older wage wo, both fixed by the collective agreement. Older employment equals oj = oj0 + Rj, where oj0 is the number of older workers employed before the reform and Rj is the number of older workers the reform retains. The firm takes Rj as given and chooses yj to maximize profit
πj = Aj Ljγ − wy yj − wo oj.   (2)
The first-order condition for yj equals γ Aj Ljγ−1 = wy, and the efficiency-unit input satisfies
Lj* = (γ Aj / wy)1/(1−γ).   (3)
The firm hires young workers until the marginal revenue of an efficiency unit equals the entry wage, and young employment equals yj* = Lj* − θj oj whenever this quantity is positive.
PROPOSITION 1: If yj* > 0, each retained older worker lowers young employment by θj and raises total employment by 1 − θj. Output stays constant in Rj, and payroll rises by wo − θj wy per retained worker.
PROOF: Equation (3) fixes Lj* at a level that depends on Aj, γ, and wy. Substituting Lj* = yj* + θj(oj0 + Rj) and differentiating gives dyj*/dRj = −θj. Total employment yj* + oj changes by 1 − θj. Output Qj = Aj Lj*γ stays constant. Payroll wy yj* + wo oj changes by −θj wy + wo. Q.E.D.
The proposition equates the displacement ratio with the relative productivity of an older worker. A firm that values an older worker at θj young workers gives up θj hires for each retained worker, because the entry wage prices the marginal efficiency unit and the firm holds the total number of efficiency units at the profit-maximizing level. The retained worker raises the wage bill and leaves output unchanged.
B. Promotion Ladders
A ladder with Dj rungs places older workers at the top rungs, where productivity exceeds productivity at the entry rung. We model the link with θj = θ(Dj) and θ′(D) > 0.
PROPOSITION 2: If θ′(D) > 0, the displacement per retained worker, −dyj*/dRj = θ(Dj), increases in ladder depth Dj.
PROOF: Proposition 1 gives −dyj*/dRj = θj, and θj = θ(Dj) increases in Dj. Q.E.D.
The ladder carries the vacancy chain of Section I. A retained worker at the top rung delays a chain that ends with a hire at the entry rung, and a deeper ladder lengthens the chain and raises the number of positions blocked per retained worker.
C. Predictions
The model delivers four predictions.
The displacement ratio equals θj, and total employment rises by 1 − θj per retained worker.
Entry wages stay constant, and headcount carries the full adjustment.
Sales stay constant, and payroll rises by wo − θj wy per retained worker.
Displacement rises with ladder depth Dj.
Section VI estimates the displacement ratio. Section VII tests the predictions on wages, sales, payroll, and ladder depth in turn.
