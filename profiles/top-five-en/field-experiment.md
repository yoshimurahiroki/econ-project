Matching Grants for Savings
I. Experimental Design
Setting and sample. The Karsten Cooperative Bank (KCB) operates in 212 villages of Karsten Province. We draw 9,420 households from the village census lists. A household enters the sample if a woman aged 25 to 60 heads it and if it holds zero formal savings accounts at baseline. The baseline survey took place between March and June 2022. The median household has 5 members and annual income of 11,800 kora.
Randomization. We assign the 9,420 households to four arms, stratified by village and by terciles of baseline wealth. The control arm receives a 0 percent match. The treatment arms receive a match on deposits of 25, 50, or 100 percent. Each arm contains 2,355 households. The match applies to the first 400 kora of deposits during the 12 months after the offer, and the KCB credits the match to the household's account at the end of each quarter. Enumerators announce the match rate at a village meeting and in a household visit.
Elicitation of willingness to pay. Before the offer, every household states the maximum fee w in [0, 120] kora that it pays for a 100 percent match on its first 100 kora of deposits. The enumerator draws a fee f uniformly from [0, 120] kora. The household receives the match and pays the fee f from a participation payment of 120 kora if w ≥ f. Stating the true value is optimal for every household, because the drawn fee is independent of the stated value and the household buys exactly when its true value exceeds the fee. The outcome of the lottery enters every regression as a control.
Outcomes. We measure account opening within six months and total deposits within 12 months from the administrative records of the KCB. The primary outcomes and the heterogeneity analysis by wealth tercile are registered in the Karsten Registry of Trials under the number KRT-0412.
II. Balance and Take-up
Baseline characteristics balance across the four arms. The joint test of equality of 14 baseline variables across arms returns a p-value of 0.46. The KCB records account opening for all 9,420 households, so attrition equals zero. Take-up of the match among households that open an account equals 97 percent.
III. Estimation and Results
We estimate the effect of the match rate with the regression
Yiv = αv + Σm ∈ {25, 50, 100} βm 1[mi = m] + Xi′γ + εiv,   (1)
where Yiv is an outcome of household i in village v, αv are village effects, mi is the assigned match rate, and Xi contains baseline wealth, household size, and the lottery outcome. Standard errors are clustered by village. The coefficient βm measures the effect of a match rate of m percent relative to the control arm.

Table 9 reports the estimates. A 25 percent match raises the probability of opening an account by 9.1 percentage points (s.e. 1.2), a 50 percent match raises it by 17.3 percentage points (s.e. 1.3), and a 100 percent match raises it by 31.2 percentage points (s.e. 1.4) from a control mean of 18.4 percent. Deposits over 12 months rise by 61 kora (s.e. 14), 122 kora (s.e. 15), and 254 kora (s.e. 17) from a control mean of 96 kora. The elasticity of deposits with respect to the match rate equals 0.62 (s.e. 0.09) in the lowest wealth tercile and 0.21 (s.e. 0.07) in the highest tercile.
Willingness to pay predicts the response to the match. Households in the top quartile of stated willingness to pay save 2.4 times more per kora of subsidy (s.e. 0.3) than the average household. A targeting rule that offers the match to households with stated willingness to pay above the median raises total deposits per kora of subsidy by 38 percent (s.e. 6).
