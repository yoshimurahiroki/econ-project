VII. Mechanisms and Heterogeneity
A. Wages, Sales, and Payroll
Proposition 1 predicts that entry wages and sales stay constant and that payroll rises. The data match each prediction. Entry wages of new hires change by 0.3 percent (s.e. 0.4), and wages of incumbent workers change by 0.1 percent (s.e. 0.2). Sales change by 0.3 percent (s.e. 0.8). Payroll rises by 3.8 percent (s.e. 0.7) at the mean exposure. The model implies a payroll increase of 3.9 percent at the mean exposure, from the retention of 2.55 workers per 100 employees at a payroll cost of 21,280 kora each on a baseline payroll of 1.40 million kora per 100 employees, where the cost equals the older wage of 24,100 kora minus 0.31 times the entry wage of 9,100 kora.
Collective scales fix the entry wage in covered sectors, and firms in uncovered sectors set it freely. Entry wages change by 0.2 percent (s.e. 0.5) in covered sectors and by 0.6 percent (s.e. 0.9) in uncovered sectors. The adjustment runs through hiring in both groups.
B. Promotion Ladders
Proposition 2 predicts that displacement rises with ladder depth. We split firms by terciles of ladder depth and estimate equation (6) in each group. The displacement ratio equals 0.08 (s.e. 0.09) in firms with 4 rungs or fewer, 0.24 (s.e. 0.10) in firms with 5 or 6 rungs, and 0.57 (s.e. 0.13) in firms with 7 or more rungs. The gap between the deepest and the shallowest group equals 0.49 (s.e. 0.16).
Firms in the top tercile of both ladder depth and the share of workers aged 60 to 62 hold 27 percent of employment and account for 71 percent of the decline in youth hiring. In these firms, the share of workers aged 30 to 39 who receive a promotion within two years falls by 2.2 percentage points (s.e. 0.5) from a baseline of 11.4 percent. The decline in promotions reflects the vacancy chain: a retained worker at the top rung delays a sequence of promotions that ends with a hire at the entry rung.
C. Sectors
The displacement ratio equals 0.44 (s.e. 0.12) in manufacturing, 0.35 (s.e. 0.15) in construction, 0.22 (s.e. 0.09) in services, and 0.05 (s.e. 0.11) in the public sector. The sector ordering follows the ordering of mean ladder depth, which equals 6.4 rungs in manufacturing, 5.9 in construction, 4.8 in services, and 4.1 in the public sector.
