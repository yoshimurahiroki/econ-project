Credit Conditions and House Prices
I. A Model of Housing and Collateral
The model has a continuum of borrowers and a fixed stock of housing H̄. Borrowers have discount factor βb and preferences
E0 Σt=0∞ βbt (ln ct + χ ln ht−1),   (1)
where ct is consumption, ht−1 is the housing stock bought in period t − 1 that delivers services in period t, and χ is the weight on housing services. The borrower earns income y, pays the gross interest rate R on debt, and buys housing at price qt. The budget constraint and the collateral constraint equal
ct + qt (ht − (1 − δ) ht−1) + R bt−1 = y + bt,   bt ≤ φ qt ht,   (2)
where bt is debt, δ is the depreciation rate of housing, and φ is the loan-to-value limit. Let μt denote the multiplier on the collateral constraint. The first-order conditions for debt and housing equal
1/ct = βb R Et[1/ct+1] + μt,   qt/ct = βb Et[χ/ht + (1 − δ) qt+1/ct+1] + φ qt μt.   (3)
The multiplier raises the value of housing at the margin by the amount of borrowing that a unit of housing supports. In the steady state, μ = (1 − βb R)/c > 0 because borrowers discount the future at a rate above the interest rate, and the collateral constraint binds.
PROPOSITION 1: In the steady state with a binding collateral constraint, the price of housing equals
q = κ(φ) y / (H̄ (1 + κ(φ) [δ + (R − 1) φ])),   κ(φ) = βb χ / (1 − βb(1 − δ) − φ(1 − βb R)).   (4)
The price rises with φ if 1 − βb R > βb χ (R − 1).
PROOF: Substituting the multiplier into the housing condition of (3) at the steady state gives q h/c = κ(φ). The budget constraint with b = φ q h gives c = y − q h [δ + (R − 1) φ]. Combining the two relations and setting h = H̄ gives (4). The price equals 1/(1/κ(φ) + δ + (R − 1) φ) times y/H̄. The derivative of the denominator with respect to φ equals −(1 − βb R)/(βb χ) + (R − 1), which is negative under the stated condition. Q.E.D.
The proposition states the mechanism. A higher limit raises the debt that each unit of housing supports, which raises the value of housing to the borrower at the margin and the price that clears the market.

Table 8 reports the calibration. Four parameters match direct observations, and the discount factor βb = 0.9192 matches the estimated price response of 6.8 percent (s.e. 0.9) to a 10 percentage point increase in the limit. The condition of Proposition 1 holds at these values, with 1 − βb R = 0.053 and βb χ (R − 1) = 0.007.
III. The 2013 to 2018 Boom
The boom combines a 10 percentage point relaxation of the limit, from 0.80 to 0.90, with a decline of the mortgage rate from 3.6 percent to 3.0 percent. House prices in Marden rise by 18.3 percent over the period. The model delivers a price increase of 15.0 percent, which equals 82 percent of the observed boom. With the rate held at 3.6 percent, the relaxation of the limit raises prices by 5.3 percent. With the limit held at 0.80, the decline of the rate raises prices by 7.6 percent. The interaction of the two raises prices by 2.0 percent, or 14 percent of the explained boom. The interaction arises because a lower rate raises the debt service capacity that a higher limit converts into purchasing power.
