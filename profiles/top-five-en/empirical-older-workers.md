V. Effects on Older Workers
A. Pension Claims, Employment, and Earnings
Figure 2 plots the share of workers who claim a pension by age 61 against birth month. The share equals 71.8 percent for workers born before the cutoff and falls to 13.4 percent for workers born on or after the cutoff. Column 1 of Table 2 reports the drop of 58.4 percentage points (s.e. 0.9), which equals 81 percent of the pre-reform claim rate. The 13.4 percent of workers who claim after the cutoff hold exemptions for careers of 42 years or more.

Column 2 reports the employment effect. Employment at ages 60 to 62 rises by 14.2 percentage points (s.e. 0.7) from a baseline of 52.1 percent, an increase of 27 percent. Column 3 reports the earnings effect. Annual earnings rise by 3,420 kora (s.e. 210) from a baseline of 12,900 kora, an increase of 27 percent that matches the employment increase. The earnings gain per additional worker equals 24,100 kora, the annual earnings of a full-time worker at ages 60 to 62 on the collective scale.
The reform delays pension claims for 58.4 workers per 100. Three destinations absorb the delayed claims. Employment absorbs 14.2 percentage points, or 24 percent. Disability and unemployment insurance absorb 30.4 percentage points, or 52 percent, through increases of 11.8 percentage points in DI receipt (column 4) and 18.6 percentage points in UI receipt (column 5). Exit from the labor force with zero benefit receipt absorbs 13.8 percentage points, or 24 percent (column 6). The fuzzy design gives an effect of 0.243 additional employed persons (s.e. 0.012) per delayed pension claim.
B. Dynamics and Heterogeneity
The employment effect shrinks with age. Employment rises by 15.6 percentage points (s.e. 0.8) at age 60, by 14.8 percentage points (s.e. 0.8) at age 61, and by 12.2 percentage points (s.e. 0.9) at age 62. The pattern reflects the growth of DI receipt with age, which reaches 21.4 percent of the affected cohorts at age 62.
The effect varies with sex and education. Employment rises by 16.3 percentage points (s.e. 0.9) for men and 12.1 percentage points (s.e. 0.9) for women. Employment rises by 15.9 percentage points (s.e. 0.8) for workers with a secondary education and 8.7 percentage points (s.e. 1.3) for college graduates, so workers with a secondary education account for 86 percent of the employment gain. Occupational plans cover 41 percent of workers with a secondary education and 83 percent of college graduates, and the second tier replaces the public pension for the covered workers who retire.
Each additional year of the eligibility age raises employment at ages 60 to 62 by 4.7 percentage points (s.e. 0.2). The estimate lies within the range of 3.0 to 5.3 percentage points per year that Havrick and Dellamore (2014) and Ostrander, Quillon, and Pellam (2019) report for Eastfold and the Tessaly Coast.
