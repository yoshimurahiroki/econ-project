## Conclusion and comparison

Example ID: default-micro.en.conclusion.

**Input:**
Choose one service to maximize annual net benefit. The shuttle's benefit is £84,000 and its cost is £60,000. The bicycle service's net benefit is £19,000. Each option fits the £65,000 budget. One implementation slot is available.

**Ideal output:**
Choose the shuttle service. Its annual benefit of £84,000 less its cost of £60,000 gives a net benefit of £24,000. The gain over the bicycle service's £19,000 net benefit is £5,000. Both options fit the £65,000 budget, so allocate the implementation slot to the service with the higher net benefit.

**Generalizable operation:**
Lead with the choice, calculate net benefits in the same period and currency, and state the difference determining the allocation among feasible options.

### Paper conclusion from a completed trial

**Input:**
Original fictional study: Cedar Plain has 48 municipalities with 100 eligible households each. A lottery assigned 24 municipalities to send households a benefit-application notice giving the deadline and office address. Application completion within eight weeks was 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in the controls. The saved assignment-effect estimate is 8 percentage points, with a municipality-clustered 95 percent interval of 3 to 13 percentage points. Write the conclusion using this evidence.

**Ideal output:**
Notices giving the application deadline and office address increased completion within eight weeks by 8 percentage points. Random assignment across 48 municipalities identifies this response among 4,800 eligible households in Cedar Plain. The 95 percent confidence interval is 3 to 13 percentage points.

**Generalizable operation:**
End with the measured behavioral response and the evidence establishing it. Keep the intervention, outcome, horizon and population fixed as the result becomes the conclusion.

## Institution and mechanism

Example ID: default-micro.en.mechanism.

**Input:**
A cooperative introduced morning and afternoon reservations for a shared mill. Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon use those vehicles to carry grain. Hired transport carries a fee. Explain the relation between reservation time and transport cost.

**Ideal output:**
Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon reserve the afternoon mill slot and carry their grain in those vehicles. Matching the reservation to the vehicle's availability saves the fee for hiring transport for that load.

**Generalizable operation:**
Connect the reservation, available vehicle and transport fee. Explain the farmer's timing choice through the payment that choice changes.

### Theory proposition and economic mechanism

**Input:**
Original model: two firms simultaneously choose quantities q_i >= 0. Inverse demand is p = a - b(q_1 + q_2), marginal cost is c, a > c >= 0 and b > 0. Each firm maximizes (p-c)q_i. Write the equilibrium result and explain the response to c.

**Ideal output:**
Each firm supplies (a-c)/(3b) in the unique Cournot equilibrium, and the market price is (a+2c)/3. The first-order condition equates a-c-bq_j to 2bq_i. Solving the two conditions gives the equilibrium quantities. The derivatives with respect to marginal cost are -1/(3b) for each firm's output and 2/3 for the market price. Lower production costs therefore expand output through both firms' quantity choices.

**Generalizable operation:**
Present the economic object, derive it from the firms' optimal choices and explain the comparative static through the same equations. Preserve the parameter domain and the equilibrium concept in the surrounding model.

### The same model for two requested audiences

**Input:**
Use the original Cournot model above with \(a=90,\ b=2\) and common marginal cost falling from 30 to 18. The model gives each firm's output as 10 and 12, total output as 20 and 24, and price as 50 and 42. Prices and costs use the same currency unit. A best-response diagram places \(q_1\) on the horizontal axis and \(q_2\) on the vertical axis. Write a clear, short passage for economics researchers and a second passage explicitly requested for readers without economics training.

**Research audience:**
The common cost reduction moves the Cournot equilibrium from \((q_1,q_2,p)=(10,10,50)\) to \((12,12,42)\). Each firm's output rises according to \(dq_i/dc=-1/(3b)\), and the price response is \(dp/dc=2/3\). In the best-response diagram, both curves shift outward and their intersection moves from \((10,10)\) to \((12,12)\).

**Explicitly requested non-economist audience:**
Each producer chooses how much to sell, accounting for the price reduction caused by greater total supply. Lowering both producers' unit costs from 30 to 18 makes extra production more profitable. Each raises output from 10 to 12, and the resulting increase in total supply lowers the price from 50 to 42. In the diagram, the axes show the two producers' quantities. Each line gives one producer's choice for a given quantity supplied by the other. Lower costs move both lines outward; their intersection shifts from 10 units per producer to 12.

**Generalizable operation:**
Use the same model and quantities for both readers. A request for clarity retains the research audience's comparative-static language. The explicit audience change introduces the firms' choice and price tradeoff, then explains the diagram's curves and moving intersection.

### Model counterfactual and transfer accounting

**Input:**
Original numerical model: one firm faces weekly demand Q(p) = 120-2p and constant marginal resource cost 20 currency units. It maximizes (p-20+s)Q(p), where s is a per-unit production subsidy. The baseline has s=0; the intervention has s=10. Saved solutions give (p,Q)=(40,40) and (35,50). Consumer surplus rises from 400 to 625, producer profit including the subsidy rises from 800 to 1,250, and subsidy payments rise from 0 to 500. Social surplus equals consumer surplus plus producer profit less subsidy payments; public funds have unit resource cost.

**Ideal output:**
A production subsidy of 10 per unit lowers the model's price from 40 to 35 and raises weekly output from 40 to 50. Consumer surplus increases by 225 and producer profit by 450. Public subsidy payments amount to 500 per week. Social surplus therefore rises by 175, from 1,200 to 1,375. The firm's output response creates the gain, while the transfer enters producer income and the public budget with opposite signs.

**Generalizable operation:**
Name the model operation, show the quantity response and carry all transfers through the stated welfare criterion. Use the saved scenario values and preserve their weekly horizon and currency unit.

## Revision and translation

Example ID: default-micro.en.translation.

### Preserve an author's objects, notation and citation

**Input:**
Original fictional study: 90 food-processing estates are randomly assigned to private activation, joint activation or control. The saved comparison gives a 1.3 MW larger reduction in estate peak demand under joint activation than under private activation. The author uses the terms *joint activation*, *private activation* and *estate peak demand*. Each \(L_{it}\) is plant \(i\)'s load in MW at common 15-minute time \(t\); \(e\) denotes an estate. The citation key `activation-protocol` identifies the fictional protocol supplied in this input.

Revise this passage while preserving its adopted content:

> Relative to private activation, a reduction of 1.3 MW in estate peak demand is produced by joint activation. The definition of estate peak demand is \(P_e=\max_t\sum_{i\in e}L_{it}\). Joint activation means that linked schedule changes enter into operation on complete agreement of the required plants \citep{activation-protocol}.

**Ideal output:**
Joint activation implements linked schedule changes once every required plant agrees \citep{activation-protocol}. Estate peak demand is \(P_e=\max_t\sum_{i\in e}L_{it}\), where \(L_{it}\) measures plant \(i\)'s load in MW at the common 15-minute time \(t\). The randomized comparison shows that joint activation lowers estate peak demand by 1.3 MW relative to private activation.

**Generalizable operation:**
Put the implementation rule and the measured object before the comparison. Preserve the author's terms, the aggregate-at-a-common-time equation, the MW unit and the citation's attachment to the rule. Compare the meaning of each relation across the two passages.

### Retain a supported equilibrium result in a referee response

**Input:**
Original fictional model and revision record: two firms choose quantities simultaneously, inverse demand is \(p=a-b(q_1+q_2)\), both firms have marginal cost \(c\), and \(a>c\geq0,\ b>0\). A referee asks why the reported derivative is \(-1/(3b)\) when a firm's best response has derivative \(-1/(2b)\) with respect to its own cost while its rival's quantity is fixed. Section 2 now states that the comparison changes both firms' common cost. The proof of Proposition 1 now differentiates both best responses. Proposition 1 and the Figure 1 caption retain the equilibrium derivative \(-1/(3b)\).

**Ideal output:**
Section 2 now specifies a change in the common marginal cost of both firms. Each firm's best response satisfies \(q_i=(a-c-bq_j)/(2b)\). Along the symmetric equilibrium, differentiating this relation gives \(dq_i/dc=-1/(2b)-(1/2)dq_j/dc\), so each firm's equilibrium output changes by \(-1/(3b)\). The proof of Proposition 1 now includes this derivation, and the Figure 1 caption states the same common-cost comparison.

**Generalizable operation:**
Recover the referee's comparison and the model's adopted change. Use the best-response equations to establish the retained result, then identify the completed changes to its definition, proof and figure. Keep this one comparison consistent across the response and manuscript.

## Abstract and Introduction

### Abstract

Example ID: default-micro.en.abstract.

**Input:**
A study randomly assigned 800 households in Alder Bay to an envelope group or a control group for a housing survey. The envelope group received a prepaid return envelope. Response rates were 64 percent in that group and 51 percent in the control group. Write the study's Abstract.

**Ideal output:**
Including a prepaid return envelope increased the housing survey response rate by 13 percentage points. We estimated this effect by randomly assigning 800 households in Alder Bay to an envelope group, with a 64 percent response rate, or a control group, with a 51 percent response rate.

**Generalizable operation:**
Lead with the main finding and give the group rates, population and assignment establishing it. Express the difference between the rates in percentage points.
