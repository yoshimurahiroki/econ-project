# Short functional examples: English

Profile: default-micro. All settings, designs, quantities and source sentences below are original fictional examples. Apply [profile principles](PROFILE.md#principles); read the function needed for the current writing choice.

## Conclusion and comparison

Example ID: default-micro.en.conclusion.

**Input:**
Choose one service to maximize net benefit over one year. The shuttle's benefit is £84,000 and its cost is £60,000. The bicycle service's net benefit is £19,000. Both fit the £65,000 budget, and only one can be implemented.

**Ideal output:**
Choose the shuttle service. Its annual benefit of £84,000 less its cost of £60,000 gives a net benefit of £24,000, exceeding the bicycle service's £19,000 by £5,000. Both options fit the £65,000 budget, and only one can be implemented.

**Generalizable operation:**
State the choice once at the beginning and compare alternatives using the adopted objective, common period and units. State the net-benefit difference that determines the choice.

### Information costs and utility units

Two states each have probability one half. Action A pays (64, 64), action B pays (144, 4), utility is the square root of the amount, and perfect information costs 4 monetary units.

Acquire the information. The best immediate action is A with expected utility 8. After observing the state, choose B in the first state and A in the second. Deducting the monetary cost before utility gives (√140 + √60)/2 ≈ 9.789063. With utility 10u + 5, immediate utility is 85 and acquisition gives about 102.890631, preserving the choice. For amounts expressed in units one hundredth as large, multiply amounts and the monetary cost by 100 and use utility √(amount/100). Deduct any separately defined utility-unit cost in the same utility scale.

## Institution and mechanism

Example ID: default-micro.en.mechanism.

**Input:**
A cooperative introduced morning and afternoon reservations for a shared mill. Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon can use those vehicles to carry grain and avoid hiring transport. Revise this sentence to explain the mechanism: “This is not merely a change in reservations but a reform that affects farmers' transport choices.”

**Ideal output:**
Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon can reserve the afternoon mill slot and carry their grain in those vehicles. Matching the reservation to the vehicle's availability saves the cost of hiring transport for that load.

**Generalizable operation:**
Remove the “not merely” contrast that does no work in the conclusion and connect the farmer's reservation, available vehicle and transport cost.

## Quantities and relationship

Example ID: default-micro.en.quantities.

**Input:**
Describe observational data from 160 repair shops in 2024. Shops with a certified-worker share 20 percentage points higher had average revenue per worker-hour €12 higher. Report the between-shop association.

**Ideal output:**
Among 160 repair shops surveyed in 2024, shops with a certified-worker share 20 percentage points higher had average revenue per worker-hour €12 higher.

**Generalizable operation:**
Put the observed relationship, population, period, comparison width and outcome unit in the sentence. State the quantity directly.

## Revision and translation

Example ID: default-micro.en.translation.

**Input:**
Translate this lease rule into natural English: 各借主は、家賃を全額支払い、建物の使用許可が有効である場合に、賃貸借契約を1年間更新できる。 Full payment and a valid occupancy permit are jointly required; the rule grants permission to renew.

**Ideal output:**
Each tenant has the right to renew the lease for one year if rent is paid in full and the building retains its occupancy permit.

**Generalizable operation:**
Rebuild the sentence in the target language.

## Abstract

Example ID: default-micro.en.abstract.

**Input:**
A study randomly assigned 800 households in Alder Bay to receive a housing survey with or without a prepaid return envelope. Response rates were 64 percent with the envelope and 51 percent without it. Write the study's Abstract.

**Ideal output:**
Including a prepaid return envelope increased the housing survey response rate by 13 percentage points. Response rates were 64 percent with the envelope and 51 percent without it. We estimated this effect by randomly assigning 800 households in Alder Bay to two groups, sending one group a prepaid return envelope with the survey.

**Generalizable operation:**
Lead with the principal result, then give the 64 versus 51 percent comparison, population and assignment. Express the difference as 13 percentage points and make the Abstract self-contained.

## Introduction opening

Example ID: default-micro.en.introduction.

**Input:**
Write the opening of the same study's Introduction. Households without a prepaid envelope pay the postage to return the survey. Including the envelope removes that payment. Random assignment among 800 households produced a 13-percentage-point difference in response rates.

**Ideal output:**
Including a prepaid return envelope increased the housing survey response rate by 13 percentage points. The envelope removes the household's postage payment when returning the survey. We estimated the effect of this change on response rates through random assignment among 800 households in Alder Bay.

**Generalizable operation:**
Connect the main result to the institution changing households' costs and the comparison measuring its effect. The Introduction explains the return process in place of repeating the Abstract's group-specific response rates.

## Assignment and comparison

Example ID: default-micro.en.assignment.

**Input:**
An oversubscribed vocational training program allocates training opportunities by lottery. Explain a research design comparing subsequent paid employment among lottery winners and losers.

**Ideal output:**
We compare subsequent paid employment among applicants who won and lost the training lottery to estimate the effect of receiving an opportunity to attend vocational training.

**Generalizable operation:**
Use the assignment mechanism and outcome comparison to explain the design. Include a regression specification when it determines the estimate's meaning or is needed for the requested explanation of the method.
