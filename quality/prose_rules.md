# Expression review pointer

1. **Source first → located passage → composer.** For every element of English and Japanese prose, papers, materials, slides and Beamer, including short passages, single words, labels, translations and existing drafts, search for, obtain and read the relevant passage and surrounding section, page, slide or figure in actual 2020-or-later top-five economics journal papers or slides, major economics handbooks, reviews or guides, economics textbooks, or economics course materials (lecture notes, course slides and handouts). Inspect visual sources as well. Read [PROSE Common output](../runtime/PROSE.md#common-output) with that source requirement. Map content selection, argument structure, style, vocabulary, transitions, endings, placement, design and appearance to corresponding source locations; borrow and imitate those actual elements. For Japanese, use corresponding Japanese originals rather than newly translating English prose. Adjust only what is surgically necessary for established subject facts, names, variables and notation, population and period, numbers and units, citations, grammatical agreement, and the requested medium and 28pt body text. Do not transfer the source's different empirical conditions or conclusions. If a corresponding source is missing, return to search and reading; do not fill gaps with invented examples, free paraphrase, or independently created structure or design. This applies at every scale and in every mode. The [recurring-pattern table](prose_rules.json) maps locators to those rule steps; `python harness/local_checks.py scan-text --input FILE --dictionary quality/prose_rules.json` returns source positions.
2. **Revised artifact → final review.** Apply the complete [finish procedure](../runtime/PROSE.md#finish-the-passage) to meaning and rhetorical function, including passages beyond the listed locators. Record source-to-output locations and necessary adjustments in the existing source, specification or work records, then compare every output element against the original. Preserve the subject's scientific definitions, conditions, quantities, uncertainty and attribution. Exclude disclaimers, reservations, concessions, limiting, cautious, conservative, vague, redundant, weak or ornamental framing, supplements and not-A / not-A-but-B / A-rather-than-B or distinction rhetoric and synonyms. Avoid exaggeration and abstract value judgments; select direct, natural, neutral expressions from inspected sources. Scientific fidelity does not authorize excluded rhetoric. Japanese composition and translation use the [shared language rules](../runtime/PROSE.md#revise-for-the-requested-language) and corresponding Japanese originals; pattern matches locate passages for that review. Never treat a locator or a record as a substitute for reading the source.
