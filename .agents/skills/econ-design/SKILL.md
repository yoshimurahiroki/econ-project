# Research design

## Purpose and input

For a new economics task, complete the brief [workflow entry](../econ-workflow/SKILL.md#task-entry) once, then continue here. Support calls within an existing task do not restart entry.

Develop or assess a requested economics question, model, identification problem, estimator or inference choice.

Input: Requested economic question, model or empirical design, closest literature and documented source fields.

Apply [the common prose rule](../econ-assertive/SKILL.md#common-prose-rule) to every output from this task.

## Procedure

### Identify the requested design work

Develop the requested question or derivation, or assess the specified design. Match idea generation, ranking, derivation or reassessment to the user's request. Assess a research question or contribution through its economic behavior, incentives, information, allocation, externalities or equilibrium. Establish novelty by comparing the closest papers. Use econ-paper or econ-writing to select and explain the method's central economic comparison.

### For an empirical specification

Define an empirical specification's unit, treatment or exposure, population, period and aggregation. Explain its assignment mechanism, timing, risk set and counterfactual. State the parameter and the assumptions that identify it. Map controls and fixed effects to the variation they absorb. Predetermined measurement establishes timing; assignment evidence supports exogeneity. Distinguish source-observation units, analysis units, policy-assignment units and independent inference units. Name each unit from the actual design. Preserve the source's unit label without assigning it an undocumented policy or inference role.

Use [design-specific methods](references/designs.md) to match estimation and inference to the parameter, treatment path, heterogeneity, dependence and assignment units. Interpret each requested diagnostic through its null, precision and sampling design.

### For a theoretical model

Define a theoretical model's agents, timing, information, actions, objectives, constraints and equilibrium. Derive the requested proposition or comparative static for its defined domain and equilibrium conditions. Establish a numerical solution through its convergence criterion. Connect the model's predictions to measurements and evidence that separates mechanisms. Read the descriptive-model reference in econ-workflow to connect observed facts to a requested model or counterfactual.

### For behavioral responses and group comparisons

Distinguish a behavioral mechanism's total or fixed cost from its cost of an additional unit. Use the actor's decision rule and initial choice to derive the response to a cost change. Establish which choices cross the participation or supply threshold, or which feasible alternative becomes the chosen treatment. Express changes in net gains and changes in actions as separate economic objects.

An individual's monotone comparative static gives the direction of that actor's response. To compare group quantity changes, convert choices crossing the threshold into the comparison's quantity unit and apply population weights. Establish comparability of initial choice distributions, production opportunities and response margins at the affected thresholds.

### Establish feasibility

Map a proposed design's required inputs to documented fields and inspect source records to establish feasibility. Apply the task's cost and access conditions. Assess the specified candidates from evidence and preserve their IDs and scope. Identify each demonstrated defect through its evidence. Record unresolved evidence requirements in the operational record. Read econ-literature for a novelty comparison and econ-data for source-field or record-linkage analysis.

### If a consequential research choice is requested

For a requested consequential research choice, compare the recommendation with its nearest feasible alternative. Identify the evidence that would change the recommendation and the scientific consequences of each specification. Keep recommendations, project plans, user decisions and adoption status in canonical operational records outside research artifacts. Express an artifact's scientific content through its economic objects, comparisons, assumptions and results.

## Output

The requested question, derivation or design assessment, supported by its economic comparison and evidence.
