# Research design

Resolve the requested question, plan, derivation or audit. Evaluate economic importance through behavior, incentives, information, allocation, externalities or equilibrium. For contribution or novelty judgments, compare with the closest papers read fully. Match generation, ranking, derivation or reassessment to the requested operation.

Define the unit, treatment comparison or exposure path, population, period and aggregation. Explain who is compared with whom, actual assignment, timing, risk set and counterfactual. State the maintained assumptions and diagnostic evidence. Map controls and fixed effects to the variation they absorb. Predetermined measurement establishes timing; exogeneity requires assignment evidence.

Read the relevant section of [design decisions](references/designs.md). Select estimation and inference for the parameter, treatment path, heterogeneity, dependence and assignment units. Interpret diagnostics through their actual null, precision and sampling design.

For theory, define agents, timing, information, actions, objectives, constraints and equilibrium. Derive the proposition or comparative static. State the domains and equilibrium conditions the result requires. For numerical solutions, use the convergence criterion needed to establish that result. Link predictions to measurements and mechanism-separating evidence. Use the Mahoney reference when facts guide model choices or counterfactuals.

For plans, match required inputs to documented fields and inspect records for schema-dependent feasibility. Apply the task's actual cost and access conditions. For audits, judge each specified candidate from evidence before comparing the prior verdict. Preserve IDs and scope. Distinguish a demonstrated defect from an evidence requirement. Use econ-literature for novelty and econ-data for measurement.
