# Overlapping-generations general equilibrium

`.agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_macro_extended.py` implements a two-period deterministic-perfect-foresight model conditional on the supplied technology path. Each period a unit mass of young households and a unit mass of old households coexist. Young types `i` have weights `ω_i` summing to one and labor efficiencies `e_i`; effective labor is `L = Σω_i e_i`. Young income is `m_i = w e_i + Π − G`; young consumption and investment are `c_i = m_i − s_i`. Old consumption is `d_i = (1−δ+r)s_i` one period after saving. Young households maximize `log(c_i)+β log(d_i)` subject to `0 ≤ s_i ≤ θ m_i`. Hence `s_i = min{β/(1+β), θ} m_i`. The multiplier on the investment limit is `β/s_i − 1/c_i`; it is zero when slack and positive when binding. The limit represents financial capacity tied to current disposable income. It is an explicit saving/investment constraint, whose collateral capacity is `θ m_i`.

Firm `j` has fixed labor fraction `ℓ_j`, productivity `A_j` and output `A_j K_j^α(ℓ_j L)^(1−α)`. Firms pay common factor prices equal to marginal products divided by the gross markup `μ ≥ 1`, and distribute profits uniformly to the young. The allocation equalizes marginal products: `K_j/K = ℓ_j A_j^(1/(1−α)) / Σ_j ℓ_j A_j^(1/(1−α))`. Aggregate output is `Y = exp(z) A K^α L^(1−α)`, with `A = [Σ_j ℓ_j A_j^(1/(1−α))]^(1−α)`. Factor prices are `w=(1−α)Y/(μL)` and `r=αY/(μK)`; profits are `Π=(1−1/μ)Y`. The markup is an exogenous reduced-form competition wedge, so this is a macro–IO comparison through factor prices and profit rebating. Government absorbs `G=gY`, financed by a uniform lump-sum levy on young households. Inputs must make every type's disposable income positive.

Markets clear when `K[t+1]=Σω_i s_i[t]`. Given the steady saving fraction `b=min{β/(1+β), θ}`, stationary capital is `[b((1−α)/μ+1−1/μ−g) A L^(1−α)]^(1/(1−α))`. The reported resource residual is `Σω_i(c_i[t]+d_i[t])+G[t]+K[t+1]−[(1−δ)K[t]+Y[t]]`; the capital-market residual is `K[t+1]−Σω_i s_i[t]`. Cohort welfare uses its actual next-period return, including the scenario's next technology value. The final cohort uses the AR(1) continuation value of technology and constant policy. The initial old cohort holds the baseline steady-state asset distribution. Thus transitions include both old and young allocations. The model supplies a finite-horizon perfect-foresight transition conditional on the stated technology path and steady state, with type distributions, investment, government absorption, profits and welfare.

The observation equation is `z[t]=ρ z[t−1]+ε[t]`. `scipy.optimize.least_squares` minimizes the sum of squared innovations over `−.99 ≤ ρ ≤ .99`. The estimand is conditional persistence; identification uses nonzero lag variation and orthogonality of innovations to lagged productivity. The result records the actual residuals, objective, Jacobian rank and norm, and optimizer settings. The example generates a 101-row seeded AR(1) CSV and estimates persistence through the same file reader used for supplied observations. Each specification identifies a CSV path, SHA-256, period column, productivity column, source and observation type. Period IDs must be ordered consecutive integers; productivity must be finite and represent equally spaced log productivity. Relative paths resolve against the specification directory. The result records the verified hash, column IDs, period bounds and row count.

The official Sequence-Jacobian `simple` block and `solve_jacobian` solve the capital-clearing equation and map a vector of log technology changes to output changes at the baseline steady state. Since `d log K[t+1]=α d log K[t]+dz[t]`, `dY[t]/dz[s]=Y*α^(t−s)` for `s ≤ t` and zero otherwise. The program checks the official result against the analytic matrix and checks every analytic entry against central differences of the full nonlinear model with step `1e−5`; each comparison must have maximum absolute error below `1e−7`. The general transition compares a specified technology shock `z[t]=shock*ρ^t` and scenario policy against the stationary baseline; its differences are the joint policy and technology response. Run one change at a time to attribute individual shocks.

A separate HARK `PerfForesightConsumerType.solve()` lifecycle comparison uses CRRA 2, two periods, discount factor β, gross return 1.04, young cash-on-hand 2, unit old-age endowment and a zero borrowing limit. Young consumption is checked against the unconstrained solution `(1.04×2+1)/(1.04+√(1.04β))`; its saving and old consumption are also returned. HARK’s model therefore has different preferences and endowments from the log-utility OLG equilibrium.

The JSON specification requires `parameters` with `alpha`, `beta`, `delta`, `household_weights`, `household_efficiency`, `firm_labor_shares`, `firm_productivity`; `observations.path`, `observations.sha256`, `observations.period_column`, `observations.value_column`, `observations.type`, `observations.source`; baseline and scenario objects with `markup`, `collateral_income_limit`, `government_share`; `horizon` and `technology_shock`. Shares are positive and sum to one. Periods are discrete and all levels are model units per cohort. The optimizer runs in SciPy and the aggregate solution uses the equations above. Resource residuals exceeding `1e−8` cause failure. Named finite statistics carry units; paths, the observation equation, the full specification and package versions are retained.

Run `python .agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_macro_extended.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json` or `python .agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_macro_extended.py case --output NEW_DIRECTORY`. The second command atomically publishes a seeded example and its actual result using `.agents/harness/local_checks.py`. `.agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/requirements-macro.txt` specifies NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels 0.14.6, Sequence-Jacobian 1.0.0 and HARK 0.17.2 (`econ-ark`). The existing `.agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_macro.py` separately solves an infinite-lived representative household over a finite horizon with a fixed terminal capital condition.
