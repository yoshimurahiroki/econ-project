# Household–firm market simulation

`.agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_abm.py` simulates households bargaining with firms over a storable consumption good and tracks unsold inventories. Each of `m` firms produces `firm_supply` units per period. A household's marginal willingness to pay after purchasing `q` units is `v/(1+q)`. It encounters all firms in a seeded random order; the order of households is seeded too. Given the firm's ask `p` and sales tax `τ`, trade occurs when the buyer's bid is at least `p(1+τ)`. The gross negotiated unit price is `(1−b)p(1+τ)+b v/(1+q)` where `b` is `bargaining_weight`. Traded quantity is the minimum of one unit, firm inventory and household cash divided by gross price. The firm receives the gross price divided by `1+τ`; the difference is tax revenue. At the end of each period all firm revenue and taxes are equally returned to households. The full household cash and firm inventory vectors, sales, prices, tax and individual consumption are returned each period.

A firm's ask for the next period is `clip[p exp(η(I_stockout−1/2)), .05, 2v]`, where the indicator equals one if inventory is exhausted. The learning rate `η ∈ [0,1]` bounds each update. This rule adapts to observed stockouts and does not require an external language model. The resource identities are initial cash equals household plus firm cash, and cumulative production equals cumulative consumption plus remaining inventories. The reported period residuals establish both identities. Welfare is the explicitly adopted sum of `log(1+q)` across households and periods; all money is a transfer within the population and leisure, production costs and terminal inventory utility are excluded by the model.

Candidates have distinct IDs and may change only `sales_tax` and `learning_rate`. Every candidate starts from the same initial state and seed, with the same number of periods, households, firms, productivity and outcome definition. The selected candidate maximizes summed utility, breaking a tie by lexicographically larger ID; the next proposal retains its changes and adds the specified `next_rate_step` to its learning rate, clipped to `[0,1]`. The result records every simulation and the proposal, leaving adoption of the proposal to the existing research process.

The structural equilibrium reference uses the continuous price-taking version of household utility `v log(1+q)` and aggregate demand `n max(v/p−1,0)`. `scipy.optimize.brentq` finds the price clearing total supply `m*firm_supply`, and returns its demand residual. It is a static comparison with no cash constraints or inventory carryover; it is not asserted to be the decentralized simulation's equilibrium.

The JSON specification contains `seed`, `model` fields `households`, `firms`, `periods`, `firm_supply`, `household_cash`, `reservation_value`, `initial_price`, `learning_rate`, `sales_tax`, `bargaining_weight`, a list of `candidates` containing `id` and `changes`, and `next_rate_step`. Inputs, seeds, engine and unit-tagged statistics are recorded as a simulation, not observed-data estimation. Run `python .agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_abm.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json`. The result is written atomically. NumPy and SciPy implement this market; the shared `.agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/requirements-macro.txt` additionally lists the macro specialist engines.
