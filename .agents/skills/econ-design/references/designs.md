# Design-specific methods

Read the section for the requested design, estimation or inference task. Select diagnostics that examine its identifying assumptions. Use econ-paper to select and explain a paper's central economic comparison.

## Design elements

Define the comparison's unit, treatment path or exposure mapping, potential outcomes, population, period and target parameter. Explain its identifying variation, risk set, assignment and counterfactual through institutions, timing and observed operations. Connect the maintained assumptions to the estimator, aggregation weights and inference unit.

Trace a causal threat through its common cause, timing, effect on treatment or selection, and effect on the outcome. Select controls from the causal structure and measurement timing. Explain the variation removed by a fixed-effect cell and the comparison that remains after conditioning. Predetermined measurement supplies temporal ordering. Assignment evidence supports exogeneity.

Map each diagnostic to the assumption it examines. Interpret the diagnostic through its statistic and uncertainty. State identifying assumptions as conditions linking the estimator to the parameter. Present evidence as observations or estimates. Name the resulting object accurately: association, prediction, causal effect, causal mediation or model counterfactual.

## Difference-in-differences and event studies

Specify the comparison's adoption dates, reversals or intensity, cohorts, outcomes and comparison eligibility. Define group-time effects and their aggregation. Define the counterfactual trend. Explain how anticipation, interference, composition, support and concurrent interventions affect the comparison.

Choose the estimator from the treatment path and effect heterogeneity. Identify the cohort-time contrasts and weights that produce the estimate. Interpret a two-way fixed-effect coefficient through those contrasts. For an event study, specify the reference period, event window, cohort composition, comparison groups and interval type. Distinguish simultaneous intervals from pointwise intervals.

For matching, specify pre-treatment variables, measurement dates, common support, matching weights and the subsequent counterfactual-trend assumption. For future-treated comparisons, specify the eligible pre-treatment window and event-timing assumptions. Map age, birth cohort, calendar time and event time to their roles in the design.

Interpret pre-treatment dynamics for the stated comparison. Define a precision or equivalence claim through its economic tolerance and corresponding interval or test. Interpret a conventional pre-trend test through its null and sampling uncertainty. Choose concurrent-policy or placebo checks to examine a specified alternative causal path.

## Random assignment and interventions

Specify the parameter's assignment unit, mechanism, stratification and probabilities. Explain how implementation, take-up, attrition and spillovers enter the comparison. Distinguish assignment effects from treatment-receipt effects. Define receipt estimands through their compliance assumptions. Match adjustment, weighting, randomization inference and cluster inference to the assignment structure.

## Regression discontinuity

Specify the running variable, cutoff, assignment rule, compliance, support and local parameter. Connect continuity or local randomization to manipulation, heaping, discretization and other cutoff rules. Specify the result's bandwidth, polynomial, kernel, bias correction and uncertainty. For a fuzzy design, connect outcome and treatment jumps to the local compliance parameter.

## Instrumental variables and assignment leniency

Define the treatment, instrument, first stage and target population. Explain the economic paths that support independence, exclusion and monotonicity. Assess instrument strength with diagnostics for the model and inference procedure. Use weak-instrument-robust inference when the result requires it.

For judge, examiner or officer leniency, establish actual assignment and within-cell variation. Choose the leave-out unit from that assignment structure. Establish how instrument-estimation error, examiner sample size, treatment dimensions, sorting and monotonicity enter the requested parameter. Map that parameter to its compliance population and institutional margin.

## Shift-share designs

Identify whether shocks or shares support exogeneity. Specify share dates, shocks, concentration, exposure, leave-out construction, common components and effective assignment units. Match inference to shock dependence and exposure overlap. Define the estimand through the chosen exogeneity argument.

## Networks and spillovers

Define the relationship, measurement date, exposure mapping, own and peers' treatments, and interference set for the requested effect. Distinguish common shocks, network formation, endogenous peer selection, direct effects and spillovers through the design. Trace assignment to downstream exposure. Establish how later treatment, migration, link changes and outcome recording enter the effect. Match inference to assignment and dependence.

## Synthetic controls and panel counterfactuals

Define the treated unit, donors, pre-treatment support and counterfactual structure. Establish how fit, contamination, extrapolation and regularization enter the estimate. Select placebo or sensitivity exercises to examine the specified identifying premise. Explain uncertainty through the assignment model, sampling framework or inferential procedure used.

## Inference and interpretation

Choose standard errors, clustering, randomization inference or another justified procedure from the design's sampling and assignment variation. Map effective assignment units, dependence and small-sample corrections to that procedure. Preserve prespecified outcome families and multiple-testing adjustments. Use direct contrasts for subgroup differences.

Report the defined parameter and its numerical uncertainty. Assess the named design through its comparison, estimand and supporting evidence. For a requested repair, define the resulting comparison and estimand and give their supporting evidence. Explain the design features that establish the requested paper claim. Keep repair history and execution status in operational records outside research artifacts.
