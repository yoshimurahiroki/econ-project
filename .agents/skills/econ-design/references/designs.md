# Design-specific decisions

Use the section for a requested design, estimation or inference task. Select diagnostics for the identifying question being assessed. A general paper explanation selects its core comparison through econ-paper; these details supply understanding and verification.

## Design elements

Use the unit, treatment path or exposure mapping, potential outcomes, population, period and target parameter needed to define or assess the comparison. Explain its identifying variation, risk set, assignment and counterfactual through institutions, timing and observed operations. Connect the maintained assumptions to the estimator, aggregation weights and inference unit.

Explain a relevant causal threat through its common cause, timing, effect on treatment or selection, and effect on the outcome. Select controls through the causal structure and measurement timing. Explain the variation removed by a fixed-effect cell and the comparison remaining after conditioning. Predetermined measurement supplies temporal ordering. Assignment evidence supports exogeneity.

Map a diagnostic to the assumption it examines. Give its statistic and uncertainty when these determine the interpretation. Preserve the distinction between assumptions and evidence. Define the supported parameter as association, prediction, causal effect, causal mediation or model counterfactual.

## Difference-in-differences and event studies

For the requested comparison, specify adoption dates, reversals or intensity, cohorts, outcomes and comparison eligibility. Define group-time effects and their aggregation. Explain the counterfactual trend and the anticipation, interference, composition, support or concurrent interventions that affect that comparison.

Choose the estimator for the treatment path and effect heterogeneity. Identify the cohort-time contrasts and weights supplying the estimate. Interpret a two-way fixed-effect coefficient through those contrasts. For an event study, specify the reference period, event window, cohort composition, comparison groups and interval type. Preserve simultaneous and pointwise uncertainty.

For matching, specify pre-treatment variables, measurement dates, common support, matching weights and the subsequent counterfactual-trend assumption. For future-treated comparisons, specify the eligible pre-treatment window and event-timing assumptions. Map age, birth cohort, calendar time and event time when they determine the design.

Interpret requested pre-treatment dynamics for the stated comparison. Give an economic tolerance and corresponding interval or test for a precision or equivalence claim. Give a conventional pre-trend test its actual inferential meaning. Select concurrent-policy or placebo checks for a specific alternative causal path that affects the requested assessment.

## Random assignment and interventions

Specify the assignment unit, mechanism, stratification and probabilities for the requested parameter. Connect implementation, take-up, attrition and spillovers to that comparison. Distinguish assignment effects from treatment-receipt effects. Preserve compliance assumptions for receipt estimands. Match adjustment, weighting, randomization inference and cluster inference to the assignment structure.

## Regression discontinuity

Specify the running variable, cutoff, assignment rule, compliance, support and local parameter. Connect continuity or local randomization to the relevant manipulation, heaping, discretization and other cutoff rules. Specify the bandwidth, polynomial, kernel, bias correction and uncertainty used for the result. For a fuzzy design, connect outcome and treatment jumps to the local compliance parameter.

## Instrumental variables and assignment leniency

Define the treatment, instrument, first stage and target population. Explain the economic paths supporting independence, exclusion and monotonicity. Assess strength with diagnostics suited to the requested model and inference. Use weak-instrument-robust inference when that result requires it.

For judge, examiner or officer leniency, establish actual assignment and within-cell variation. Use the appropriate leave-out unit. Address instrument-estimation error, examiner sample size, treatment dimensions, sorting and monotonicity where they determine the requested parameter. Map the parameter to the compliance population and institutional margin.

## Shift-share designs

Identify whether shocks or shares support exogeneity. Specify the relevant share dates, shocks, concentration, exposure, leave-out construction, common components and effective assignment units. Match inference to shock dependence and exposure overlap. Explain the estimand under the chosen exogeneity argument.

## Networks and spillovers

Define the relationship, measurement date, exposure mapping, own and peers' treatments, and interference set for the requested effect. Distinguish common shocks, network formation, endogenous peer selection, direct effects and spillovers through the design. Trace assignment to downstream exposure. Account for later treatment, migration, link changes and outcome recording when they affect that effect. Select inference for assignment and dependence.

## Synthetic controls and panel counterfactuals

Define the treated unit, donors, pre-treatment support and counterfactual structure. Connect fit, contamination, extrapolation and regularization to the requested estimate. Select placebo or sensitivity exercises for the identifying premise being assessed. Explain uncertainty through the assignment model, sampling framework or inferential procedure used.

## Inference and interpretation

Select standard errors, clustering, randomization inference or another justified procedure from the design's sampling and assignment variation. Give effective assignment units, dependence and small-sample corrections when they determine inference. Preserve prespecified outcome families and multiple-testing adjustments. Use direct contrasts for subgroup differences.

Report the supported parameter and numerical uncertainty. For a requested repair, explain the revised comparison, changed estimand and supporting evidence. Assess the named design and the conditions needed to judge it. Explain the design features needed to establish the requested paper claim.
