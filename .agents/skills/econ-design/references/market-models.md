# Executable market models

The market runner accepts JSON containing `model`, a complete `baseline`, a complete `scenario` (or null when the selected engine owns its counterfactual), `unit`, and `sources`. Sources identify the input with a description, path, or URL. Objects reject unknown fields. Run `python .agents/skills/econ-data/scripts/research_markets.py run SPEC.json --output RESULT.json`; `case --output NEW_DIRECTORY` creates separate synthetic specifications and computed results. The caller provides an existing output parent. Outputs contain model, engine, adopted inputs, options, finite named statistics with units, scenario results, and the specification hash. Existing research DAG commands can invoke this CLI with an argument array.

## Differentiated demand and supply

For consumer i and product j in market t let utility be `delta_jt + mu_ijt + epsilon_ijt`, outside utility be epsilon_0, and `delta = X1 beta + xi`. Heterogeneity is `mu = X2 Sigma nu` with integration nodes and weights. Independent extreme-value errors give conditional logit probabilities; integrating them gives shares. Nested logit adds within-group correlation rho. Recover delta with `delta <- delta + log(s_observed) - log(s_model)` until simulated and observed shares agree. Excluded instruments Z satisfy `E[Z'xi]=0`; for endogenous characteristics, include distinct excluded instruments with variation that shifts those characteristics and is independent of xi. Supply instruments satisfy the analogous cost-error restriction. GMM minimizes the quadratic form of sample moments using the supplied weighting and nonlinear optimization settings. Identification of each endogenous characteristic, heterogeneity parameter and nesting correlation requires independent moment variation; choose instruments and integration using the research design.

For single-product prices, own and cross derivatives form D, with `D_jk = ds_j/dp_k`. The elasticity is `D_jk p_k / s_j`; diversion from j to k is `-D_kj / D_jj`. Multi-product firm f chooses prices to maximize `sum_{j in f} (p_j-c_j)s_j`; the ownership-adjusted derivative matrix gives the first-order markup vector and marginal cost. Consumer surplus is the expected log sum divided by the absolute marginal utility of money; multiply shares, per-consumer profit and consumer surplus by market population when reporting aggregate amounts. With quasilinear utility and fixed production costs, producer surplus is profit plus fixed costs; social surplus is consumer surplus plus producer surplus plus government revenue. An ownership change changes the first-order conditions. Changes to marginal costs, product attributes, utility, product availability or policy alter the equilibrium and welfare; retain product market size and common currency units in the input interpretation.

`model: blp` accepts a baseline object with exactly `products`, `linear`, `nonlinear`, `supply`, `integration`, `solve`, `counterfactual`, `optimal_instruments`, and `add_exogenous`. Product records require market_ids, product_ids, firm_ids, positive prices and shares, positive outside share in each market, and excluded demand_instruments fields. Add nesting_ids for nesting and supply_instruments fields for endogenous supply. Set `add_exogenous` to false when a characteristic is endogenous, and provide all exogenous regressors, the intercept and excluded instruments explicitly in the demand_instruments columns; their projected design must identify the parameters. Formulation strings are PyBLP formulas. `nonlinear`, `supply`, `integration`, and `counterfactual` accept null when absent; integration has method, size and options. `solve` uses PyBLP Problem.solve arguments; optimization and iteration each have method and options. The runner uses `Problem`, `Formulation`, `Integration`, `Optimization`, `Iteration` and `ProblemResults` methods for estimated coefficients, shares, costs, elasticities, diversion, markups, consumer surplus, and optional approximate optimal instruments. A counterfactual contains `keep_product_ids`, `firm_ids`, `costs` and `utility_shifts`, with one value per retained observation in original order. `Simulation.replace_endogenous` solves new multiproduct Bertrand prices; the runner checks convergence, profit gradients, and profit Hessians. Its profits are per potential consumer, and market-level conversion is determined by the supplied population. Retention implements product exit and ownership changes. Optional `entry_products` provides new product records in existing markets and `entry_xi` supplies one structural utility shock per entrant; arrays for costs, firms and shifts cover retained observations followed by entrants. Specify the entrant characteristics, utility shock and cost from the adopted identification and simulation design. Specify availability and the resulting market population consistently.

`model: equity` delegates its baseline directly to `equity.fit`; the `scenario` is null and the adopted equity counterfactual belongs in that specification. The same owner supports estimated demand, PyBLP simulations, and `model: cournot`. For the latter, inverse demand is `P=a-bQ`, firm cost is `c_i q_i + d_i q_i^2/2`, and a firm's best response is `clip((a-c_i-b Q_-i)/(2b+d_i), 0, capacity_i)`. Supply-side instruments identify inverse demand when observed quantity is endogenous. The shared equity solver estimates inverse demand by 2SLS or accepts supplied coefficients and checks every capacity-constrained best response. Under linear demand the consumer surplus is `b Q^2/2`; profit equals revenue less variable cost, and their sum gives welfare when production has no external cost.

## Auction models

All private-value cases have n risk-neutral symmetric bidders with independent uniform values on [0,1], observed n and format, a public reserve r, simultaneous sealed bids, independent tie breaking and zero value for no allocation. In a second-price auction each active bidder bids value. In a first-price auction a type v >= r bids `v - (v^n-r^n)/(n v^(n-1))`; lower types do not participate. Uniform zero-reserve bid recovery is `v=n b/(n-1)` in the first-price case and `v=b` in the second. This is structural inversion using the adopted bid distribution, whose support and bidder count are identifying inputs. Expected revenue integrates the equilibrium payment over the largest order statistic; bidder surplus is expected realized value less revenue. The runner optimizes reserve over [0,1] and calculates sale probability, revenue, expected total and bidder surplus. `baseline` and `scenario` each require `n`, `format` (first or second), `information` (private or common), `reserve`, and `bids`; recovery observations use zero reserve.

The common-value model has independent uniform signals X_1,...,X_n, common realized value `V=sum X_i/n`, and each bidder observes only its own signal before bidding. At own x and highest rival signal t, `E[V|x,max rival=t]=x/n+t/2`; this incorporates the information conveyed by winning. With zero reserve the second-price equilibrium bid is `(n+2)x/(2n)` and the first-price bid is `(n-1)(n+2)x/(2n^2)`. The runner numerically maximizes expected profit over the rival-signal threshold at x=0.6 and reports the distance from the equilibrium threshold. Common-value recovery data and nonzero reserves require their own identifying distribution and are outside this specified model.

## Search, bargaining and platforms

Search has n independent uniform [0,1] offers, search cost c per inspection, a known outside option and free recall. The consumer inspects sequentially until the best observed value reaches `z=1-sqrt(2c)` or no offers remain. This satisfies `E[(X-z)+]=c`. For outside below z, expected inspections are `sum_{k=0}^{n-1} z^k`; the integrated distribution of the final maximum gives expected gross and net surplus. `offers`, `search_cost`, `outside_option` are the required inputs. The scenario changes the search environment and recomputes the rule and value.

In bargaining, the buyer values trade v, the seller bears cost c and a per-transaction tax t, outside payoffs are d_b and d_s, and buyer bargaining weight is w. A deal occurs when `S=v-c-t-d_b-d_s>0`. Maximizing `(u_b-d_b)^w (u_s-d_s)^(1-w)` gives buyer gain wS and seller gain (1-w)S. Price is v minus buyer payoff. The output includes tax revenue, markup, allocation and the Nash first-order residual. Required fields are `buyer_value`, `seller_cost`, `buyer_outside`, `seller_outside`, `buyer_weight`, `tax`.

For the two-sided platform, side participation q solves inverse demands `p_A=a_A+g_A q_B-q_A` and `p_B=a_B+g_B q_A-q_B`. The profit-maximizing monopolist chooses both participation levels with unit costs c_A,c_B. Its FOCs are `2q_A-(g_A+g_B)q_B=a_A-c_A` and the symmetric equation for B. Nonnegative cross effects, sum below two and positive participation give an interior unique maximum. Each side's consumer surplus is q²/2; social welfare is the sum of both consumer surpluses and platform profit. Required arrays `intercepts`, `cross_effects`, `costs` each have two entries. Policy or externality changes enter the scenario arrays.

## Matching

The matching market consists of equal-size sets with strict complete preferences and one-to-one assignments. Proposers apply sequentially to their next choice; each receiver holds its preferred offer and rejects the others until no proposer is free. No blocking pair remains. Welfare is the sum of provided cardinal partner values; these values strictly follow each supplied preference ordering. Specify arrays `proposers`, `receivers` and maps `proposer_preferences`, `receiver_preferences`, `proposer_values`, `receiver_values`. A changed institution or preference profile is represented by a full scenario. Outcomes report each match, total cardinal welfare, proposals and the number of blocking pairs.

## Estimated nesting, demand and supply examples

`blp-nested-free` generates 120 markets with four single-product firms each. Product characteristics and cost shifters vary independently. Each market has two nests, with group size cycling among 1, 2 and 3. For price coefficient alpha = -1.1, correlation rho = 0.4, mean utility `1 + 0.5x + alpha p + xi`, and costs `1.3 + 0.3z + omega`, the generator solves each firm's Bertrand FOC. Demand instruments include the excluded own cost shifter and the number and cost shifters of products in the same nest. The estimator starts rho at 0.2 and estimates it inside the bounds [0.01, 0.9], while concentrating the linear demand parameters. The observed within-nest variation and excluded cost shifters supply the identifying variation. Independent, small demand and cost errors are generated with a fixed seed.

`blp-supply-free` uses the same Bertrand construction with rho = 0, estimates price coefficient alpha jointly with the cost equation, and estimates the cost intercept and cost shifter coefficient by GMM. Supplying the finite initial value -0.9 for the price coefficient places it in the nonlinear optimization; omitted initial values for the remaining linear coefficients allow them to be concentrated. The price coefficient has no equality bounds. Demand instruments include cost shifters; supply instruments include demand shifters. The generated shocks are independent of these instruments. Each example records the actual optimizer gradient, inverted shares and estimated coefficients.

## Estimating auction value distributions

`model: auction_fit` accepts complete records from zero-reserve, independent-private-value auctions with n symmetric risk-neutral bidders and values drawn independently from `Uniform(0,U)`, where U is unknown. Each model object contains `n`, `format` (`first` or `second`), `records`, and `counterfactual_reserve`. Each record has a unique string `auction_id`, a list of exactly n objects with `bidder_id` and `amount`, a `winner_id`, and a `payment`. The winner is the unique highest bidder. Payment equals the highest bid under first price and the second-highest bid under second price. The runner validates these relations before estimation.

For first price, the equilibrium bid is `(n-1)v/n`, so each observed bid yields `v=nb/(n-1)`; second-price bidding gives `v=b`. The likelihood of the recovered independent values under Uniform(0,U) is `U^(-N)` for U at least the largest value, so the maximum-likelihood estimate is their sample maximum. `scipy.stats.uniform.fit` fixes the location at zero and estimates U; the output also reports the unbiased order-statistic correction `(N+1)U_hat/N`, all recovered bidder values, recorded payments and their mean. Using U_hat, the runner evaluates expected revenue and welfare at the supplied reserve and calculates the optimal reserve and its revenue. `auction-records` generates 200 three-bidder first-price auctions with independent uniform values on [0,2.5], verifies winners and payments, estimates U from 600 recovered values, and evaluates a reserve of 1.25. The same complete-record input contract supports second-price auctions. These formulas use all bids; estimation from winning bids alone requires the winning-order-statistic likelihood.
