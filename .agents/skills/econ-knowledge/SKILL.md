## Select and read knowledge

The [retrieval program](scripts/knowledge.py) provides `domains`, bounded `search`, and `read` commands. Select the relevant domain from `domains`, compare the candidate's use, inputs and outputs with the request, and read the selected IDs. `search --domain DOMAIN --query TEXT --limit 8` returns at most eight ranked candidates in that domain. When independent search questions share the domain and limit, repeat `--query TEXT` in one invocation. Each list in `searches` keeps its own ranking and matches; the index-read report is shared. Omitting `--domain` searches the available domain indexes under one shared limit. Use that cross-domain search when the question spans mechanisms or the relevant field is itself being selected; refine the question's economic objects through the returned domain and unit descriptions. A merger question can require demand, ownership, equilibrium, consumer welfare and business valuation. Search those objects together or through the corresponding focused queries, then select the complete chain the requested result uses. Exact IDs take priority, followed by matches in the indexed title, domain, topic, purpose, inputs and outputs through normalized words, bilingual aliases and Japanese character fragments. The lead makes the economic selection from the candidates' meanings and the adopted assumptions.

Search weights words by their specificity across the selected indexes, normalizes English plurals, and counts each matched concept once. Inspect `matched_concepts` and their definition owners to distinguish meanings of a shared term. `concepts --query TEXT` accepts a full question and returns the matching labels with each distinct concept. Compare the definition and economic object before selecting a meaning. If a domain-restricted search returns no applicable candidate, repeat the same question without `--domain` and inspect the returned definition owners before classifying a knowledge gap. Numerical methods can be owned by the substantive field that uses them; Bayesian posterior simulation is under econometrics.

Both `search` and `concepts` accept registered JEL codes in the query. `I32` selects poverty measurement and analysis, `C2` selects mapped codes under that group, and `JEL Q` selects the mapped environmental, resource and agricultural fields. Exact knowledge IDs rank first, followed by JEL matches and the query's economic concepts. Inspect `matched_jel_codes` together with the unit's purpose and model conditions; a classification match identifies a field, while the body establishes its substantive coverage.

Concept `mappings` retain versioned STW correspondences from the local concept to the external URI. `exactMatch` records equivalent concepts, `closeMatch` a close correspondence, `broadMatch` a broader external concept, `narrowMatch` a narrower external concept, and `relatedMatch` an associative link. Establish each mapping from the source's labels, scope and the local definition. Keep distinct meanings of matching, CV, BIC and Hotelling under their own definition IDs. The local bilingual labels supply retrieval terms; the typed unit relations supply theoretical and computational prerequisites.

The mappings reference ZBW — Leibniz Information Centre for Economics, [STW Thesaurus for Economics](https://www.zbw.eu/stw/version/latest/download/about.en.html), under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Each mapping records its source version; the local Japanese labels and mapping judgments are authored here. The [STW–JEL crosswalk](https://www.zbw.eu/stw/version/latest/mapping/jel/about.en.html) is published under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) and records its target JEL edition as `20210225` (2021-02-25). Interpret its category links against that edition. Obtain current JEL codes and English labels from the [AEA classification tree](https://www.aeaweb.org/econlit/classificationTree.xml). For example, the crosswalk labels `P16` as “Political Economy”; the current AEA tree labels it “Capitalist Institutions • Welfare State”. Use each unit's JEL codes to locate its economic field and its full body to establish model coverage. Preserve local definitions when an external label groups distinct methods or objects, such as resampling and cross-validation, or a consumer price index and a constant-utility cost-of-living index.

Run `read ID...` to retrieve those IDs and their topologically ordered required prerequisites. Related and alternative IDs support selection and enter the read only when the operation needs them. Verify each returned body's definition, assumptions and implementation conditions against the requested use. Additional technical dependencies discovered during the work trigger the corresponding additional ID read.

For a version 2 collection, use `concepts --query TEXT` to inspect bilingual names, aliases, broader and narrower concepts, definitions and JEL mappings. Use `relations ID...` to inspect typed incoming and outgoing relations; `--type TYPE` restricts the relation and repeated `--context KEY=VALUE` supplies an adopted applicability selector. Use `plan ID...` with the same optional context to identify understanding prerequisites, applicable implementation dependencies, unresolved conditions and model contracts. The plan also returns `inapplicable_relations` with the authored conditions that conflict with the supplied context. A selector reports whether the supplied value matches a registered condition; establish the condition from the actual model and evidence before relying on it. Use related and alternative relations to compare candidates under their stated target, identifying observations and conditions.

Use `plan ID... --context KEY=VALUE --read` when the next step needs the planned bodies. It returns the plan and each resolved required unit once, in dependency order, including its full body and version. Unresolved conditions remain in the plan and do not add dependencies. An application cycle or required body outside the snapshot returns the plan with `read_blocked`, no bodies and a nonzero exit. Resolve the cycle or expand the snapshot before reading. Omit `--read` to inspect the plan alone; `read ID...` retains its direct prerequisite and task-cache behavior. The shared report's `files` and `bytes` count distinct knowledge files read in this invocation. Standard output also contains metadata and JSON formatting; body bytes are the UTF-8 lengths of the returned unit texts. `plan` also accepts model contract IDs. For example, `plan MC-hark-consumption-saving --context calculation_target=market_equilibrium` starts from that contract's units and its selection, assumptions, identification, equations, numerical diagnostics and example locators, then follows the applicable prerequisite relations. The market-equilibrium condition returns the related household-distribution and market-clearing unit; select it with `plan MC-hark-consumption-saving HET-02 --context calculation_target=market_equilibrium --read`. `calculation_target=household_policy` leaves that condition in `inapplicable_relations`. Compare the actual actors, information, choice timing, feasible actions and target before supplying either value. A contract ID locates its model; its name supplies no economic adoption decision.

Contracts expose `availability`, derived from their existing implementations and example locators: `theory_reference` identifies mathematical bodies, `numerical_example` a worked numerical calculation, `internal_runner` a registered econ-data binding with its public command, `internal_function` a registered callable, and `external_official_api` a pinned official module. A contract can expose several uses. The selection and numerical-diagnostic locators point to the owning chapter text and carry its body hash. Static availability describes those interfaces; installation, optimization, simulation and saved results use their actual execution records. A pinned external source can carry both its package `version` and 40-character `revision`, or the revision and its `source_url` when the selected interface is distributed at that commit.

The result includes the complete contract, knowledge plan, checks and saved-result provenance. `selection_status="theory_only"` supplies equations; `"implementation_only"` supplies its authored callable or command without a selector binding.
