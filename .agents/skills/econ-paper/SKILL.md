# Understanding and evaluating a paper

Read the specified version and the evidence supporting its main argument. Keep editions and passage locations in existing source metadata. A complete explanation uses the following five questions to understand and evaluate the research, rather than to fill five short summary boxes. Select the substance each paper needs; let theory, empirical work and reviews answer them in their own way.

1. **What is the research question?** State the economic question, normally as one interrogative sentence. Identify what the author wants to explain, rather than naming the policy or institution.

2. **Why important? — Significance of the paper.** Explain the economic behavior, incentives, mechanism or theoretical problem that makes the question matter, and why the chosen setting helps answer it. Distinguish that economic significance from the urgency or popularity of a policy.

3. **How do they answer the question?** Explain the theory or mechanism, the important data and observation unit, and the comparison or model that answers the question. In empirical work, identify the outcomes, treatment or explanatory variation, method and comparison. Retain the author’s distinctive solution to a consequential identification problem and assess any design weakness that materially affects its persuasiveness. In theory, explain the assumptions and reasoning that deliver the result. These are content-selection perspectives, not mandatory subheadings. Select the design’s core; omit routine specifications, controls, inference settings and robustness inventories unless they do essential work in this paper’s argument.

4. **Results.** Give the findings that answer the question, important economic magnitudes and their interpretation. Explain what they reveal about behavior or theory and any consequential policy implication. Compression should remove secondary estimates, not the size or meaning of the main finding.

5. **Why was this paper published in a top journal? — Scholarly evaluation.** Make an independent assessment of the research’s academic value: what advances beyond the closest work, why its question, reasoning, design, data, measurement or result constitutes an important contribution, and which strength or weakness governs that judgment. Judge the research rather than its journal label. Do not infer the editor’s or referees’ actual acceptance reasons, assume every source is a top-journal article, or substitute a contribution synopsis for an assessment.

Use [econ-assertive](../econ-assertive/SKILL.md) for natural prose and econ-writing for a seminar’s medium and production entry point. Read the relevant econ-design section for a substantive methodological dependency, and [the descriptive-model reference](../econ-workflow/references/descriptive-model.md) when the interpretation connects observed evidence with model features or counterfactual assumptions. Follow the requested citation format.
