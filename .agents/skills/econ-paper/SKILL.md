# Understanding and evaluating a paper

Read the specified version and the evidence supporting its main argument. Keep editions and passage locations in existing source metadata. A complete explanation uses the following five questions to understand and evaluate the research, rather than to fill five short summary boxes. Select content by the paper's question and contribution before drafting; let theory, empirical work and reviews answer them in their own way. These questions organize economic understanding, not technical coverage. Across these questions, use the Abstract–Introduction benchmark in econ-assertive for content and explanatory density, while consulting the full source to establish the facts and make an independent assessment.

1. **What is the research question?** State the economic question, normally as one interrogative sentence. Identify what the author wants to explain, rather than naming the policy or institution.

2. **Why important? — Significance of the paper.** Explain the economic behavior, incentives, mechanism or theoretical problem that makes the question matter, and why the chosen setting helps answer it. Distinguish that economic significance from the urgency or popularity of a policy.

3. **How do they answer the question?** Explain the theory or mechanism, what the important data measure and why they answer the question, and the core comparison or model. In empirical work, identify the outcomes, treatment or explanatory variation, method and comparison. Retain the author’s distinctive solution to a consequential identification or measurement problem. In theory, explain the assumptions and reasoning central to the economic result. These are content-selection perspectives, not mandatory subheadings. Select the design’s core; omit routine specifications, controls, inference settings and robustness inventories unless they do essential work in this paper’s argument.

4. **Results.** Give the findings that answer the question, important economic magnitudes and their interpretation. Explain the economic behavior or theoretical insight they reveal and a policy implication when it is central to the finding. Compression should remove secondary estimates, not the size or meaning of the main finding.

5. **Why was this paper published in a top journal? — Scholarly evaluation.** Make an independent assessment of academic value through the question's importance, distinctive reasoning, identification or measurement, main discovery and advance beyond the closest work. Explain concretely why that advance matters. A demonstrated major problem enters when it changes this judgment; a separate methodological critique belongs to a requested scientific assessment. Judge the research rather than its journal label. Assess what the research adds to economic knowledge, regardless of its publication label.

Use [econ-assertive](../econ-assertive/SKILL.md) for natural prose and econ-writing for a seminar’s medium and production entry point. Read the relevant econ-design section for a substantive methodological dependency, and [the descriptive-model reference](../econ-workflow/references/descriptive-model.md) when the interpretation connects observed evidence with model features or counterfactual assumptions. Follow the requested citation format.
