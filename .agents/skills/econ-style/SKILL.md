# Economics writing profiles

Define the requested profile's reader, purpose, languages and intended products. Use the supplied complete examples and existing profile before obtaining additional material needed for the request. Choose examples that demonstrably connect the economically important question, comparison, central findings and contribution while omitting technical detail that does not advance that argument. Judge their expression in full context, including the amount of explanation needed by the intended reader. Include empirical and theoretical arguments when they illuminate different choices. For source-derived examples, prefer final authoritative editions. Verify the exact version and complete passage boundaries; a fragment or an earlier version does not establish the contents of a complete later passage.

Read each complete passage as a connected argument, including its substantive subsections, exhibits and relevant footnotes. Inspect page images when extraction obscures the text. Use the underlying research and appendices to establish facts, mechanisms, results and citations. Keep specialist-section conventions separate from general exposition. Choose Japanese terms and sentence connections in context; the requested product determines its organization and explanatory density.

Distinguish a summary's compression from an opening's developed argument. Identify the economically important information, why the setting or comparison answers the question, and how evidence changes the reader's understanding. Account for omitted technical material by its role in that argument. Express useful choices in a few high-level principles and complete original connected passages. Show what a passage establishes, how its sentences carry the reasoning, and which available information was left out. A compact Abstract and a developed Introduction can demonstrate different treatments of the same research content. The reader, purpose and target material determine the amount and form of writing; avoid fixed paragraph templates, word bans, length limits and item inventories. Keep example-specific facts separate from claims about the user's study.

For a requested opening written from a study body, save a reusable brief of the body and its section relationships, retaining central and supporting technical material without preselecting the opening. Keep the original Abstract, Introduction and their analysis outside the author's input. Save the argument plan and complete prose before opening the same-edition reference. Compare the chosen and omitted information, the economic argument and its expression. Identify important content lost, peripheral technical propositions added and qualifications that do not advance the argument. Reuse the fixed brief for an authorized revision and preserve separate inputs for independent confirmation.

For a requested writing comparison, preserve the exact task and allowed inputs, save complete drafts before reading a reference answer, and assess important content, connected economic reasoning and expression. A sound alternative argument can be preferable. Attribute an omission to its actual cause: absent evidence, content selection or expression. Retain original outputs and decisions when revising the profile; feedback used to make a revision is not independent confirmation. Use the existing task-verification procedure only when the requested work calls for it, rather than adding a comparison to ordinary writing.

Keep input identities, versions, reading scope and detailed comparison records in the private research or evaluation area. Save the useful principles and original worked passages at the requested profile destination. Clearly label fictional examples and their quantities. Preserve any required third-party notices. Try a proposed profile on representative target prose with fixed facts and adopt a revision only when the actual writing supports it.
