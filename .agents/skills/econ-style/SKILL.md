# Economics writing profiles

For general economics writing, use complete connected examples that make the economically important question, comparison, central findings and contribution intelligible. Include empirical and theoretical examples when they illuminate different choices; select relevant papers from existing holdings before obtaining missing primary sources. Prefer published articles or final publisher proofs and record the inspected edition and complete section boundaries. An older manuscript is not evidence that the published Introduction was read.

Read the whole sections, including introduction subsections and relevant footnotes, as connected arguments. Inspect page images when extraction obscures prose or an example. Full articles and appendices may verify facts, mechanisms, results, and citations; keep their specialist-section conventions separate from the general writing profile. Choose Japanese terms and sentence connections in context; the requested product determines its organization and explanatory density.

Learn how the papers choose economically important information, explain why the setting or comparison answers the question, and connect evidence to meaning. Study which technical material the authors omit and why it contributes less to that argument. Express the useful choices in a few high-level principles. A brief original adaptation can show content selection in context. Let the reader, purpose, and target material determine the amount and form of writing; avoid fixed paragraph templates, word bans, length limits, and item inventories. Source-study facts remain examples of those studies.

For a requested Introduction reconstruction exercise, save a reusable brief of the non-introduction body and its section relationships, retaining central and supporting technical material without preselecting the Introduction. Keep the Abstract, original Introduction and its analysis out of the reconstruction input. Save the content/argument plan and complete prose before opening the same-edition original Introduction. Compare adopted and omitted information, the economic argument and its expression. Identify important content lost, peripheral technical propositions added and qualifications that do not advance the argument. Distinguish missing inputs from selection or expression failures; a different sound argument can be better. Reuse the fixed brief after a repair and keep unused papers separate from development feedback. Use the existing [harness-improvement comparison](../econ-workflow/references/team-execution.md#verifying-a-proposed-change); this exercise is not a routine extra step for ordinary writing.

Record exact sources, versions, and reading scopes. Keep source originals and detailed reading evidence in the private research or evaluation area; save the profile's principles and original worked passages at the requested destination. Try the profile on representative target prose with its established facts and improve it only for a demonstrated problem in the writing.
