# Writing and artifacts

Produce the requested argument plan or artifact for its reader, purpose, language and medium using the supplied research content. Use [econ-assertive](../econ-assertive/SKILL.md) and its selected examples to guide the prose. Before composing, identify the economic knowledge the research adds beyond its particular setting. Select the mechanism, comparison and main findings that establish that contribution, and give the argument the depth it needs. Explain what generates the key comparison, who is compared and how the evidence answers the question. Keep a distinctive method or measurement solution when the discovery depends on it. The source's sequence of analyses does not set the content or structure of an Introduction. Routine validity defenses, implementation details and secondary findings do not enter to complete its coverage. Draw implications from demonstrated findings rather than adding interventions or analyses discussed by the source.

Use econ-edit for wording revision and econ-paper for explaining a source paper. Use the relevant research method for a requested substantive question; a writing or wording task selects how to present the supplied content. Choose the substantive level established by the evidence—a possibility, a result within a specified setting, or a general result—before wording the claim. Express that level through the claim's subject, verb, comparison and quantity, rather than adding restrictive explanations afterward.

Use the supplied template, bibliography and notation. A talk develops its own spoken argument for the audience and time available. Select each slide for what listeners need to understand at that point; the paper’s supporting evidence need not all appear in the opening. Keep the facts and quantities you select accurate, and preserve the canonical research files. For translation, compose natural syntax in each language while preserving substantive meaning. Use the existing Markdown, QMD, LaTeX or Beamer entry point. For Beamer, preserve the user's theme, Japanese LuaLaTeX support and readable presentation.

For a research site, establish canonical URLs and generated-content owners before changing pages or links, and preserve navigation, language counterparts and deep links. Use saved analysis objects for numerical exhibits.

When a compiled or rendered artifact is requested, read its Make target and template, generate it through the existing entry point, and inspect the result.
