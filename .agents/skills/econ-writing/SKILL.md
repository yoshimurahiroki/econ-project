# Writing and artifacts

Use the requested object, audience, depth and format. Use econ-edit as primary for wording revision and econ-paper as primary for a paper explanation. Read another research method only for a substantive dependency needed to establish the draft's claim.

## Economics prose

Identify the central question, answer or result and the information the reader needs to understand it. Give sections and paragraphs distinct roles, then order them by concept dependencies. Use the profile selected under [econ-assertive](../econ-assertive/SKILL.md) for the functions needed by this text.

An introduction connects a concrete economic question to its importance, the comparison or measurement, the actual findings and the additional knowledge they supply. Introduce an institution before the design uses its rule. Explain a mechanism through actors, choices, constraints and the observable prediction. Connect identification to the actual variation, comparison and estimand. Develop results from an interpretable quantity toward the economic interpretation it supports. Organize prior work around the question, mechanism or comparison that establishes the contribution.

Choose the amount of explanation for the use. A research plan links its proposed comparison to necessary inputs and pending decisions. A review links each judgment to the claim and evidence under review. A slide builds one point from readable evidence. A normal answer follows the requested decision and depth. A paper gives the argument the space its reasoning needs. Use these roles when they advance understanding; do not impose a fixed sequence or paragraph template.

For bilingual deliverables, use one substantive outline and compose natural syntax in each language. Keep economic terms aligned across translations. Verify that quantities, comparisons, attribution, notation and causal relations survive the translation.

## Artifacts

Preserve the supplied template, bibliography system and canonical source. Use the existing Markdown, QMD, LaTeX or Beamer entry point for the requested format.

For Beamer, keep the user's theme, Japanese LuaLaTeX support, readable fonts and plain titles, lists, equations and exhibits. Split dense frames while preserving the argument.

Introduce the economic relation an equation expresses and define its symbols beside it. Give an exhibit the sample, comparison, period, unit, denominator and uncertainty needed to read it. Use saved analysis objects for numerical content.

For a requested compiled or rendered artifact, read the relevant Make target and template, generate it through that entry point, and inspect the changed content or layout. For descriptive-model prose, connect observed variation and evidence to the model features and economic insight requested.
