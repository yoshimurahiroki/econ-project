# From research records to a paper

Use this workflow for a complete paper, a paper-wide argument plan or a substantive whole-paper redraft. For a requested specialist section, use the section-composition instructions. Econ-writing owns the integrated manuscript. Apply [econ-assertive's common prose rule](../../econ-assertive/SKILL.md#common-prose-rule) to every section and use original examples from the selected profile. Use specialist methods to answer unresolved research questions.

Keep writing plans and execution records outside the manuscript. Research deliverables must not contain project plans, progress, stages, decision or adoption status or future user decisions in prose, explanations, annotations or paraphrases. Report a request that requires operational content inside a research deliverable as a conflict before drafting.

## Establish the argument from the research

Read the canonical research record and main exhibits before choosing sections. Identify the economic decision and the knowledge the evidence establishes. Connect actors' incentives, institutional variation, comparisons and measurement to that contribution. Trace central claims to supporting records. Identify the magnitude and economic meaning of the main results. Select the headline result through the economic question it answers. State a source-reported result at the level established by that source or saved record. Do not claim to have reproduced a calculation without reproducing it. Keep missing verification steps in the work record; do not replace the main answer with a secondary statistic. Use this account to compose the manuscript rather than print it as a checklist.

Write the question, mechanism, institutional comparison, measurement and descriptive facts from their records. Keep unavailable results and unresolved scientific choices in the existing work record. Do not fill a Results section with expected estimates or present descriptive patterns as completed causal findings.

## Design the paper's argument

Choose sections and their order through the relations among the economic objects, claims and evidence. Define each section's contribution to the central argument and the evidence that establishes it. Explain institutions before a comparison that depends on their rules. Explain measurement before a design that depends on the measured object. Do not organize the manuscript by execution history.

The Introduction explains the economic argument. The body develops the evidence and methods that establish it. Summarize an explanation's role in the Introduction and develop that explanation in its body section. Do not paste independent summaries together or repeat a full explanation in both places. Compose the Introduction around the central argument rather than summarize the writing plan section by section. Place sample-construction rules, specification details and diagnostics in the sections whose claims depend on them. Select opening content through the economic question and contribution. Place literature comparisons where they explain the paper's added knowledge.

## Compose the requested section

An Abstract compresses the question, decisive comparison, principal answer and economic meaning. An Introduction explains why the question arises, how the comparison answers it and how the findings change existing knowledge. Write the Abstract from the paper's argument and findings. Do not impose Abstract compression on an Introduction or explanation.

Use the selected Abstract–Introduction examples for general exposition. For a specialist section, use a relevant source's corresponding section to model organization and economic reasoning. Apply econ-assertive's common prose rule to the new text. Record the source's edition and section in the working source record.

An institutional section explains the rules that change actors' decisions or generate the comparison. A data section connects the economic object to observed records, units and construction. An identification section explains the source of treatment, defines the comparison and estimand, and explains the conditions and implementation that identify the effect. A results section states the answer and magnitude, then explains the mechanism or economic meaning through additional evidence.

Choose section functions for the paper's argument; do not impose them as a fixed table of contents. Keep corresponding-section models local to the writing task. Do not transfer their technical density into a general profile or later paper explanation. Reading an article to verify a fact does not make it a general prose model. For a general explanation, select content from the economic question and opening model rather than shorten a specialist section.

## Connect the prose

Give each paragraph a claim to establish. Develop the claim's meaning, mechanism or evidence in the order the reader needs. Carry the actor, choice, comparison or finding from one sentence to the next. State the relations between those objects explicitly. Explain how an institutional rule changes an actor's costs, how those costs change the actor's choice and how the empirical comparison measures that margin when the study establishes those relations. Do not invent a causal connection to create a transition.

Use consistent terms, precise quantity comparisons and natural verbs. Match sentence structure to the economic relation. Use passive voice to focus on an outcome and direct definitions to introduce objects. Do not substitute synonyms or connectives for a missing logical relation.

## Revise the integrated manuscript

Read the paper as one argument. Resolve missing reasons from the evidence, delete digressions, move explanations to the claims they establish and rewrite paragraphs around their role in the argument. Check that claims and exhibits support one another and that terms and referents remain consistent. Use econ-edit for scoped revision and econ-review for a requested independent assessment. Do not infer whole-paper coherence from a successful local edit.
