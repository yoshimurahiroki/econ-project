# Choose the next research operation

Use this resource when the method, economic comparison, identification argument or ordering of dependencies is unresolved. Inherit [CORE](CORE.md)'s task boundaries. If established inputs and methods already determine the requested operation, execute it directly.

## Turn the question into an operation

Identify whose choice or understanding the requested output must change and the proposition, quantity or action that resolves it. Preserve the requested deliverable. When the desired state is itself unresolved, define it from the user's purpose before choosing a remedy; when the operation is settled, execute it. For a decision problem, identify feasible options, objective, constraints and determining unknowns; for a descriptive or theoretical question, establish the object and conditions needed to answer it. Express the unresolved problem in the general form that selects its operation: choice, identification, aggregation, constraint satisfaction or coordination. Add equations when they determine the comparison, feasible set or next computation. Retain the correspondence between concrete actors, choices, constraints and information and that general problem in the existing context, then return its result to the requested choice and next authorized action. Child tasks reuse this mapping until its determining conditions change.

Locate the central obstacle: the premise whose failure changes the main claim, the constraint preventing the desired outcome, or the operation blocking delivery. Prioritize work that resolves that obstacle, separates consequential explanations or changes action rankings. Consider its attainable decision value, time and cost under Select a feasible action. Difficulty alone does not lower an important issue's priority: reduce it to an observable prediction, tractable calculation or executable step. When the choice set is open, construct a feasible alternative that changes the binding margin and compare its induced responses. A precise answer to a peripheral question does not resolve the parent issue. Reuse settled technical choices and completed calculations until a new determining condition changes them.

For diagnosis, distinguish the observed gap, the mechanism generating it, the condition that needs to change and the operation that changes that condition. Establish each connection through an observation, derivation or competing prediction. Reversing the wording of the problem supplies a desired outcome, not its cause or remedy. Define the resolved condition in observable terms. Compare a proposed intervention's effect on the whole objective, including displaced activity and responses by existing participants; a local target can improve while total value falls.

For an economic mechanism, connect actors, objectives, incentives, constraints and information to choices and outcomes. When the usual explanation fails to predict an observed pattern, examine the omitted choice, binding constraint or interaction that could change the prediction. Include substitution, strategic responses, entry or exit, equilibrium adjustment, heterogeneity and dynamics when they alter the answer; introduce a new concept or model element through the prediction it adds. Use the relevant sections of [design methods](../skills/econ-design/references/designs.md) for construction, derivation, observation maps and counterfactual objects.

Ask whether exact knowledge of a missing quantity would resolve the choice. If the objective or decision rule would still be missing, settle that dependency first. Before collecting more data under a new assumption, determine whether existing information under the same assumption already answers the question.

## Separate explanations with a comparison

Before adopting an explanation, state the consequential alternatives, their predictions under the same inputs and the observation that would overturn the leading explanation. Choose the population, time, choice boundary or observable where those predictions differ. Prefer a comparison that can change the explanation or action over another confirming example. This may be a non-effect, an opposite response across groups, a timing pattern or an intermediate counterfactual separating two simultaneous changes. Distinguish a precisely bounded non-effect from an estimate that cannot distinguish the relevant alternatives. For a numerical comparison, set a tolerance from the difference that matters to the decision. For a fault diagnosis, vary the suspected cause in a small case while holding other inputs fixed.

Select comparison groups, samples and decision criteria for that question before using their results to select the conclusion. If new evidence requires a changed criterion, population or hypothesis, state the changed premise and retain the original comparison in the existing record. Resolve a contradiction at its source, measurement or model implication; update the affected conclusion and action. Stop once the consequential alternatives are separated or the available evidence establishes the relevant bounds. Additional tests need another unresolved distinction.

When a representation or aggregation changes, preserve the target object, observation unit, feasible choices and decision-relevant distinctions. A different mean or population is a different estimand even when it uses the same records. Read [data implementation](../skills/econ-data/references/implementation.md) only for the relevant measurement, sample, join, estimator or numerical operation.

## Select a feasible action

Compare candidates under the same objective, resource constraints, population, period and units. First remove candidates violating binding constraints and those dominated under the same conditions. Rank the remaining options by contribution to the requested objective; do not add unlike objectives into an unexplained score. For equivalent outcomes, choose the option requiring fewer additional resources and connecting to adopted definitions. Prioritize comparisons that change the central proposition, identification or feasibility; method novelty and a larger menu are not adoption criteria. Select the highest-ranked feasible candidate when the ordering is determined and carry it into the next authorized operation.

If the same option wins across the admissible range of an unknown value, execute it. When the ranking changes within that range, state the switching condition. Treat information acquisition as an action. Apply the same comparison to search, questions, checks and waiting. Include its monetary cost, delay, resource use and changes to feasible choices in the resulting outcomes, then evaluate those outcomes with the adopted objective. Deduct monetary costs from the outcome amounts before applying utility; deduct additive costs defined in utility units from expected utility. Rescale utility-denominated costs with the utility scale, and transform amounts, monetary costs and utility arguments together when changing currency units. Compare the best action under current information with the action selected after each attainable information outcome, using supported probabilities and the same objective. Execute acquisition when its resulting objective is higher. Evaluate perfect information with the same outcome, cost and feasibility specification to bound the attainable objective from a partial signal; if that bound does not exceed the current best action, execute the current action. Use supported bounds or ordinal comparisons when they determine the choice. Retrieve within the task authority when the comparison selects it; otherwise execute the current action or return the switching condition and its determining input.

## Source integration and method selection

Use the source versions applicable to the requested institutional question. Identify the input needed for the next operation.

Before adopting a searched conclusion, formulate the target question and its determining conditions from the task inputs. When an analytical implication is needed to answer the question or separate alternatives, derive it and use it to direct the source lookup. For a source-interpretation question, identify the passage or definition needed without imposing a separate decision model.

Search for methods when an unresolved assumption, target mismatch or trade-off changes applicability or selection. Compare methods on the same target, action space, data, information, assumptions, decision value and resource cost. Map the selected method’s assumptions and target to this problem before applying it. Before another lookup, identify the unresolved claim, method condition or action ranking its result can establish or change. For action-selection research, apply the information-benefit and cost comparison in Select a feasible action. Skip a lookup that adds nothing to that answer or the requested source coverage. When source research itself is requested, stop on coverage of the requested sources and questions. Stop method discovery when that choice is resolved; reopen it only for a new determining condition.

## Investigate source claims

Use this procedure for source questions about research, institutions, methods, software or other requested subjects. Start from the requested answer and existing source records. Identify the claims and determining conditions that need evidence, including competing explanations when they change the result. Choose the questions by their contribution to that answer; source counts and a fixed number of claims do not define coverage. Keep established definitions and the economic comparison unchanged.

Use available, authorized search, browser and connector tools. Begin with supplied sources and existing holdings; identify relevant terms and synonyms, named authors or institutions and actual publication venues. Match source identity using the available title, author, year, version and DOI or stable identifier. Resolve ambiguous names before following their works; neither the first name match nor the most-cited result selects the applicable source. Record how a candidate was discovered and which unresolved question it can answer in the existing source record or task context.

Select the next retrieval by the claim it can resolve. Follow backward references and links to original sources, forward citations and responses, an identified author's related works, relevant venue records or newly discovered terms. Use another retrieval route when it can distinguish competing answers or expose missing evidence that affects the result. Independent evidence comes from separate underlying observations or arguments, not merely different search tools or domains. Compare copies, syndication, common datasets and circular citations by their originating evidence; retain their relationships and do not count them as independent corroboration. Delegate a search branch only under TEAM's existing benefit and capacity rules; additional agents and stages are not required.

Read the original body that establishes the claim, its population, period, quantities, conditions and contrary findings. A search snippet, login page, abstract-only view or unread citation is a lead, not evidence for claims beyond the text actually inspected. An organization's statement establishes what it said; assess its product quality, effects, advantages or responsibility through underlying observations, methods and applicable comparisons. Apply this distinction equally to OpenAI and other organizations. Source prestige, user insistence or the model's prior answer does not change the evidence; change the judgment when relevant facts, assumptions or reasoning change. Resolve disagreement at the determining passage and applicable version. Preserve source identity, URL or stable locator, version or retrieval date, exact passage location and the content hash of retained source bytes. Keep original read outputs separate from derived notes and retain supporting passages in the existing context or source record. Apply original-source and acquired-evidence admission through the existing harness when formal validation is explicitly selected. Establish each claim from the inspected text and its meaning; never manufacture a successful source read from unavailable or partial content.

After each useful retrieval, update supported and unresolved claims, source-origin relationships and the remaining leads in the existing context. Distinguish a source found, a query searched without a result, a lead not yet searched and a source not yet read or only partially accessible. Absence from one search does not establish nonexistence. Keep the next query or source read and the claim it would resolve in the existing next_action or continuation record when work remains; create no separate research registry or mandatory map file.

Finish when the requested questions have supporting passages and determining conflicts are resolved. For action-selection research, continue when the attainable improvement over the current best action exceeds acquisition and delay costs under Select a feasible action. For requested collection or source verification, continue to meet the specified questions and coverage within the authorized resources; do not replace that deliverable with an unrequested action recommendation. Stop an exhausted branch when further available leads add no decision-relevant evidence, preserving any unresolved claim rather than treating repetition as confirmation. If access, authority or the user's resource limit prevents coverage, report the exact missing passage or claim and its effect on the answer. Reopen a completed branch only for a new task input or requested scope. Deliver source-grounded direct prose through PROSE and CORE's integrated review. Apply the same content and expression rules to quotations.

## Resolve dependencies and integrate findings

Each partial task must complete a named parent proposition, choice or artifact. Reuse the parent's formulation when the target, constraints and decision criterion stay the same. Resolve any change through the governing request and adopted specification before selecting the subtask's judgment and operation. Return the result at the granularity the parent needs. Sequence prerequisites and reuse numerical outputs whose inputs and definitions match. Apply [TEAM](TEAM.md) when the benefit of independent work exceeds briefing and integration costs.

For multi-stage work, connect the current answer, the propositions supporting it and the executable tasks in the existing draft or task record. Keep established premises and candidate explanations distinct. Map each unresolved proposition to the observation or output that would resolve it, the result that would reject the candidate explanation, and the operation producing that evidence. Assign its owner and prerequisites; estimate time and resource demands where they determine feasibility or ordering. Decompose until the next operation can be executed and its result returned to the parent proposition. Preserve all required outputs and conditions when sequencing the work.

Start the operation that resolves the central dependency. As results arrive, test the corresponding proposition, update its supported conclusion and carry the change through the argument and remaining tasks. Keep completed results with matching inputs. An implemented action is tested by its requested outcome; a completed analysis is tested by the comparison it establishes. If an input, method or premise fails, repair that dependency and its affected work. A required action or fixed specification changes only with its existing authority; evidence revises a delegated judgment through its adopted switching condition. Use the [existing governing context](../harness/state.md#governing-context) when persistent continuation is needed.

When the task requires independent reasoning alongside source findings, share the question, definitions and assigned inputs while keeping developing conclusions separate until integration. Compare the proposed implication with the observed or calculated result. Compare analytical and source results using the same estimand, units, population, timing and assumptions. Reconcile disagreements at their determining passage, equation or input; update the affected premise and descendants. Keep unaffected choices and outputs.

When views conflict, identify the precise difference in the question, definitions, observations, inference, objective or feasible action. Settle a factual disagreement with its determining evidence and a logical disagreement with the missing implication. For different preferences or constraints, compare choices under the applicable objective and decision authority. Agreement on a preferred conclusion does not establish its premises. End a requested discussion with the supported judgment, the issue still requiring a decision and the authorized work it changes.

Return the selected design, comparison, implication or next executable operation. End this research step when that dependency is resolved. Additional formalization, source collection and alternative enumeration require another unresolved dependency within the task.

## Operational plans

For a requested operational plan, specify responsible actions, handoffs, needed cooperation and completion conditions within confirmed actor time and resources. Define each stage's inputs, sources and update triggers. Derive success conditions from the requested target state and supplied acceptance criteria; preserve the distinction between reaching a target level and improving from a baseline.

Use only the requested adoption or release conditions. Preserve required frequencies. Reuse existing records and routines. Include a new record, check, meeting or cadence only when the user requires it or a concrete decision or corrective action needs it. Remove unnecessary operations before drafting and deliver the actionable plan without a selection-process narrative or an added audit checklist.
