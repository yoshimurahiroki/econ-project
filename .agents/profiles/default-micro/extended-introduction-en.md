**Input:**
Write an Introduction explaining how joint activation of linked production schedules changes peak electricity demand. A fictional study randomly assigns 90 food-processing estates containing 2,160 plants to control, private activation or joint activation for 18 weeks after an eight-week baseline. Both treatment groups receive the same tariff, scheduling rule, information and staff contact. Plants choose acceptance. Joint activation requires agreement from every plant needed for the linked changes. Proposed schedules preserve equipment, supply quantities, process order, staffing, holding limits and dispatch windows. Control daily peak demand averages 10 MW; reductions from control are 0.5 MW under private activation and 1.8 MW under joint activation. The additional reduction is 1.3 MW. Joint activation changes daily energy use by 0.3 percent, bills by −5.2 percent, late deliveries by 0.1 percentage point and saleable output by 0.2 percent. Private activation increases late deliveries by 3.6 percentage points from a 7 percent control mean. Implementation rates among linked plants are 34 and 76 percent under private and joint activation; rates among stand-alone plants are 79 and 81 percent.

Operating records: plants sharing a feeder wash, cook and chill batches and transfer intermediate food within each estate. Prices are higher from 7 a.m. to 10 a.m.; movable batch starts have 30–90 minutes of flexibility. Recommendations minimize the highest combined 15-minute load over feasible schedules. Controls retain their existing schedules. Private acceptance activates each plant's schedule. Joint activation implements sets with complete agreement and leaves remaining sets on their existing schedules. Tariff discounts and interfirm transfers equal zero. Loads and production affect other plants, motivating estate-level assignment; comparisons include all plants in their assigned groups. Full-day readings locate displaced morning load in adjacent cheaper hours. Production links are identified before intervention, and both groups learn which partners' changes their own changes require. Output is valued at pre-intervention prices.

In our randomized experiment across food-processing estates, daily peak demand averages 10 megawatts in the control group; private activation reduces it by 0.5 megawatts and joint activation by 1.8 megawatts.
