## Conclusion and comparison

Example ID: default-micro.en.conclusion.

**Input:**
Choose one service to maximize annual net benefit. The shuttle's benefit is £84,000 and its cost is £60,000. The bicycle service's net benefit is £19,000. Each option fits the £65,000 budget. One implementation slot is available.

**Ideal output:**
Choose the shuttle service. Its annual benefit of £84,000 less its cost of £60,000 gives a net benefit of £24,000. The gain over the bicycle service's £19,000 net benefit is £5,000. Both options fit the £65,000 budget, so allocate the implementation slot to the service with the higher net benefit.

**Generalizable operation:**
Lead with the choice, calculate net benefits in the same period and currency, and state the difference determining the allocation among feasible options.

### Paper conclusion from a completed trial

**Input:**
Original fictional study: Cedar Plain has 48 municipalities with 100 eligible households each. A lottery assigned 24 municipalities to send households a benefit-application notice giving the deadline and office address. Application completion within eight weeks was 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in the controls. The saved assignment-effect estimate is 8 percentage points, with a municipality-clustered 95 percent interval of 3 to 13 percentage points. Write the conclusion using this evidence.

**Ideal output:**
Notices giving the application deadline and office address increased completion within eight weeks by 8 percentage points. Random assignment across 48 municipalities identifies this response among 4,800 eligible households. The result establishes that this administrative communication raises timely applications in Cedar Plain.

**Generalizable operation:**
End with the measured behavioral response and the evidence establishing it. Keep the intervention, outcome, horizon and population fixed as the result becomes the conclusion.

## Institution and mechanism

Example ID: default-micro.en.mechanism.

**Input:**
A cooperative introduced morning and afternoon reservations for a shared mill. Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon use those vehicles to carry grain. Hired transport carries a fee. Explain the relation between reservation time and transport cost.

**Ideal output:**
Farmers whose own vehicles are free in the afternoon reserve the afternoon mill slot and carry their grain in those vehicles. Matching the reservation to the vehicle's availability saves the fee for hiring transport for that load.

**Generalizable operation:**
Connect the reservation, available vehicle and transport fee. Explain the farmer's timing choice through the payment that choice changes.

### Theory proposition and economic mechanism

**Input:**
Original model: two firms simultaneously choose quantities q_i >= 0. Inverse demand is p = a - b(q_1 + q_2), marginal cost is c, a > c >= 0 and b > 0. Each firm maximizes (p-c)q_i. Write the equilibrium result and explain the response to c.

**Ideal output:**
Each firm supplies (a-c)/(3b) in the unique Cournot equilibrium, and the market price is (a+2c)/3. The first-order condition equates a-c-bq_j to 2bq_i. Solving the two conditions gives the equilibrium quantities. The derivatives with respect to marginal cost are -1/(3b) for each firm's output and 2/3 for the market price. Lower production costs therefore expand output through both firms' quantity choices.

**Generalizable operation:**
Present the economic object, derive it from the firms' optimal choices and explain the comparative static through the same equations. Preserve the parameter domain and the equilibrium concept in the surrounding model.

## Quantities and relationship

**Input:**
Describe observational data from 160 repair shops in 2024. Shops with a certified-worker share 20 percentage points higher had average revenue per worker-hour €12 higher. Report the between-shop association.

**Ideal output:**
Among 160 repair shops surveyed in 2024, shops with a certified-worker share 20 percentage points higher had average revenue per worker-hour €12 higher.

**Generalizable operation:**

### Results with saved inference

**Input:**
Original fictional trial: 24 of Cedar Plain's 48 municipalities were randomly assigned to send benefit-application notices to their 100 eligible households. The other 24 municipalities formed the control group. Saved completion rates at eight weeks are 42 and 34 percent. The saved assignment effect is 8 percentage points with a municipality-clustered 95 percent interval [3, 13]. The exhibit is Table 2, column 1.

**Ideal output:**
Application notices increased completion within eight weeks by 8 percentage points (Table 2, column 1). Completion reached 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in the control municipalities. The municipality-clustered 95 percent confidence interval runs from 3 to 13 percentage points. These estimates measure the response of the 4,800 eligible households to assignment of the notice program.

**Generalizable operation:**
Connect the reported effect to its group rates, outcome horizon, population and saved inference. Keep assignment at the municipality level and the measured outcome at the household level in direct sentences.

### Model counterfactual and transfer accounting

**Input:**
Original numerical model: one firm faces weekly demand Q(p) = 120-2p and constant marginal resource cost 20 currency units. It maximizes (p-20+s)Q(p), where s is a per-unit production subsidy. The baseline has s=0; the intervention has s=10. Saved solutions give (p,Q)=(40,40) and (35,50). Consumer surplus rises from 400 to 625, producer profit including the subsidy rises from 800 to 1,250, and subsidy payments rise from 0 to 500. Social surplus equals consumer surplus plus producer profit less subsidy payments; public funds have unit resource cost.

**Ideal output:**
A production subsidy of 10 per unit lowers the model's price from 40 to 35 and raises weekly output from 40 to 50. Consumer surplus increases by 225 and producer profit by 450. Public subsidy payments amount to 500 per week. Social surplus therefore rises by 175, from 1,200 to 1,375. The firm's output response creates the gain, while the transfer enters producer income and the public budget with opposite signs.

**Generalizable operation:**
Name the model operation, show the quantity response and carry all transfers through the stated welfare criterion. Use the saved scenario values and preserve their weekly horizon and currency unit.

## Abstract and Introduction

### Abstract

Example ID: default-micro.en.abstract.

**Input:**
A study randomly assigned 800 households in Alder Bay to an envelope group or a control group for a housing survey. The envelope group received a prepaid return envelope. Response rates were 64 percent in that group and 51 percent in the control group. Write the study's Abstract.

**Ideal output:**
Including a prepaid return envelope increased the housing survey response rate by 13 percentage points. We estimated this effect by randomly assigning 800 households in Alder Bay to an envelope group, with a 64 percent response rate, or a control group, with a 51 percent response rate.

**Generalizable operation:**
Lead with the main finding and give the group rates, population and assignment establishing it. Express the difference between the rates in percentage points.

#### Title and abstract from one source card

**Input:**
Original fictional study: 48 municipalities in Cedar Plain contain 4,800 eligible households. Random assignment selected 24 municipalities whose staff sent households the benefit-application deadline and office address. Eight-week application completion rates are 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in the controls. The saved assignment effect is 8 percentage points with a municipality-clustered 95 percent interval [3, 13].

**Title:**
Application notices and timely benefit applications

**Abstract:**
Notices giving the application deadline and office address increased completion within eight weeks by 8 percentage points. We estimate this effect using random assignment of a notice program across 48 municipalities and 4,800 eligible households in Cedar Plain. Completion rates were 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in the controls. The 95 percent confidence interval spans 3 to 13 percentage points. Administrative communication increased timely applications among the eligible households.

**Generalizable operation:**
Build the title and abstract from the body's same intervention, comparison and measured response. Preserve the assignment unit and the actor delivering the notice as the argument is compressed.

### Introduction opening

Example ID: default-micro.en.introduction.

**Input:**
Write the opening of the same study's Introduction. Control households pay the postage to return the survey. Prepaid envelopes transfer that payment to the survey organizer. Random assignment among 800 households produced a 13-percentage-point difference in response rates.

**Ideal output:**
Including a prepaid return envelope increased the housing survey response rate by 13 percentage points. The survey organizer pays the return postage through the prepaid envelope, reducing the household's cost of replying. **Generalizable operation:**

#### An empirical introduction with a connected question and answer

**Input:**
Original fictional Cedar Plain trial: 48 municipalities have 100 eligible households each. Random assignment selected 24 municipalities whose staff sent benefit-application notices giving the deadline and office address. Completion within eight weeks was 42 percent in assigned municipalities and 34 percent in controls. The saved assignment effect is 8 percentage points with a municipality-clustered 95 percent interval [3, 13].

**Ideal output:**
Can a notice giving the application deadline and office address increase timely benefit applications? We study this question by randomly assigning a notice program across 48 municipalities in Cedar Plain. Municipal staff sent the notice to eligible households in the 24 selected municipalities. The program increased completion within eight weeks by 8 percentage points.

The trial follows 4,800 eligible households. Completion reached 42 percent in the municipalities assigned to the program and 34 percent in the control municipalities. The municipality-clustered 95 percent confidence interval for the assignment effect spans 3 to 13 percentage points. The comparison establishes the response of timely applications to this administrative communication.

**Generalizable operation:**
Open with the choice being studied, state the answer, then develop the assignment and measured comparison establishing it. Each additional paragraph contributes evidence to the same economic object.

## Assignment and comparison

Example ID: default-micro.en.assignment.

**Input:**
An oversubscribed vocational training program allocates training opportunities by lottery. Explain a research design comparing subsequent paid employment among lottery winners and losers.

**Ideal output:**
We compare subsequent paid employment among applicants who won and lost the training lottery to estimate the effect of receiving an opportunity to attend vocational training.

**Generalizable operation:**
Connect the assignment mechanism to the outcome comparison and define the effect through the opportunity allocated by the lottery. Give the regression equation when it determines the requested interpretation of the estimate.
