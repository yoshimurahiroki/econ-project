# Select and solve the consequential problem

The lead owns formulation and adoption; specialists supply the needed evidence and calculations. These operations govern internal decisions and evidence; [PROSE](PROSE.md#common-output) governs user-facing claims. Read the section resolving the pending decision.

## Turn the question into an operation

1. **Adopted task → specialist.** Execute the common [economist frame](CORE.md#economist-role) at each substantive task adoption. Pass its frame, ranked question pair, selected margin, rationale and decisive observation/derivation to the next operation; reuse matching adopted formulations and inputs.

## Deepen a consequential research problem

1. **Output: question pair; consumer: formulation comparison.** Use the common task frame and upstream question pair; complete their comparison across objective, incentive, constraint, information and identifying variation against the fixed deliverable and specification. Before: “Estimate the subsidy coefficient.” After: “Estimate how the subsidy changes firms' entry and employment under the adopted policy contrast.”
2. **Output: determining distinction; consumer: design.** Reconsider the pair once more. Name the premise that could invalidate dependent work and the observation, derivation or tractable operation separating it. Retain the initial question when it resolves that margin.
3. **Output: economic mechanism; consumer: selected question.** Derive the determining optimality, best-response or switching condition using the relevant [design method](../skills/econ-design/references/designs.md) and knowledge bodies. Connect objectives, feasible choices and information to behavior, equilibrium, welfare and adjustment. Include substitution, entry, heterogeneity, dynamics or organization when they change the prediction. Diagnosis connects the observed gap to its generating mechanism and observable repair condition; compare whole-objective effects including displacement.
4. **Output: adopted formulation; consumer: decisive operation.** Save the lead's initial/alternative questions, five dimensions, selected question and rationale against the existing specification through the [formulation interface](../harness/research.md#formulation-interface). Match definitions and reuse valid adopted designs. Reopen for a specific distinction changing the choice, rejected mechanism or binding constraint, within authority and resources.

## Separate explanations with a comparison

1. **Output: prediction comparison; consumer: evidence search.** Record consequential alternatives, predictions under common inputs and an observation overturning the leading explanation. Select population, time, choice boundary or counterfactual where predictions differ. Fix groups and criteria before conclusions; retain the original comparison after an authorized premise change.
2. **Output: separating evidence or bounds; consumer: judgment.** Preserve target, observation unit and consequential distinctions through aggregation. Set numerical tolerance from the decision margin; compare subgroup differences directly and separate bounded non-effects from uninformative estimates. Before: “One subgroup is significant.” After: “Estimate the difference between subgroup effects with its covariance and 95% interval.”
3. **Output: resolved comparison; consumer: answer/action.** Resolve contradictions at the determining source, measurement or model. Diagnosis uses a small case holding other inputs fixed. Factual disagreement returns to the passage; logical disagreement to the implication; preference differences to objective and decision right. Requested independent reasoning shares inputs and definitions, then integrates separate judgments against evidence. Finish when alternatives are separated or relevant bounds established.

## Select a feasible action

1. **Output: feasible candidates; consumer: ranking.** Compare actions under the adopted objective, rights, resources, population, period and units. Remove infeasible and dominated choices. Keep distinct objectives explicit; equal outcomes favor fewer additional resources.
2. **Output: selected action or switching condition; consumer: execution.** Select the highest-ranked feasible action using supported quantities or ordinal rankings. Execute a winner spanning the admissible range of a missing input; retain a ranking switch and its determining input internally. Before: “Consider A and B.” After: “Choose A for cost c ∈ [0, 3); choose B for c ∈ [3, 8].” Novelty and menu size supply no ranking evidence.
3. **Output: information choice; consumer: current action.** When acquisition is itself a candidate, compare the current winner with decisions after attainable signals under the same objective and supported probabilities. Count acquisition costs, delay and feasibility changes once. Deduct monetary costs before utility and utility costs from expected utility; transform costs consistently across scales. Perfect-information bounds use the same costs and feasibility. Acquire when the resulting objective exceeds the current action; otherwise execute the winner or retain the determining switching input. CORE governs ordinary working effort.
4. **Output: adopted project allocation; consumer: project execution.** Compare complete feasible allocations, displaced shared capacity, timing and execution costs through the [project allocation interface](../harness/state.md#project-allocation-interface). Information value already includes downstream decision gains. Preserve fixed commitments and amendment authority.

## Source integration and method selection

1. **Output: lookup target; consumer: retrieval.** Define the target, determining conditions and analytical implication directing search or separating methods.
2. **Output: method comparison; consumer: adoption.** Compare methods on the same target, data, information, economic restrictions and resources. Read the selected method's required knowledge. Continue search for a specific unresolved claim, applicability condition, ranking or requested coverage.

## Investigate source claims

1. **Output: retrieval selection; consumer: source reading.** Start with supplied sources and holdings; choose retrievals resolving the requested claim or competing predictions. Match title, author, year, edition and stable ID. Follow determining references, citations, original observations and new terms; copied reports share their originating evidence.
2. **Output: inspected evidence; consumer: claim judgment.** Read original passages, equations and tables with population, period, quantities and conditions. Treat snippets and unread citations as leads. An organization's statement establishes its statement; effects and quality consume underlying observations and comparison. Apply equal standards across organizations and repeated requests.
3. **Output: source record; consumer: analysis/writing.** Retain exact identity, version, location and inspected text through existing evidence owners, with hashes for persistent bindings and derived notes separately. Classify searched, found, inspected and inaccessible material internally; update the pending claim and useful lead.
4. **Output: completed source coverage; consumer: parent question.** Resolve determining conflicts and requested coverage. End exhausted branches or actual access/resource limits with the exact pending passage and completion effect internally. Reopen for new determining input or scope.

## Resolve dependencies and integrate findings

1. **Output: executable assignment; consumer: next owner.** Connect contributions to their parent proposition/artifact, sequence prerequisites and parallelize independent reads or isolated writes under TEAM. Supply executable inputs and a completion condition; use deterministic tools for settled transformations and the economic owner for judgment.
2. **Output: integrated result; consumer: parent deliverable.** Return a consequential mismatch to its producer and rebuild affected consumers. Reuse unaffected definitions/results and continue the original deliverable in existing context.

## Interpret a consequential result

1. **Output: result comparison; consumer: interpretation.** Compare saved quantity, sample, units and specification with the adopted question, predicted mechanism and decision criterion. Before: “The run completed.” After: “Compare the returned employment contrast with the adopted population, units and policy criterion.”
2. **Output: retain/repair/authority judgment; consumer: artifact or producing owner.** Pass an expected result onward. For a mismatch identify its producing formulation, evidence, method, implementation, calculation or interpretation and an observable repair condition. Preserve authority, budgets and convergence rules.
3. **Output: bound interpretation; consumer: resumed selection.** Use the [execution interface](../harness/research.md#interpretation-interface) to bind specification, actual result fields, comparison, criterion, conclusion and outcome to the owner's judgment and artifact. Name the repair owner and next operation; reuse requires matching inputs. Explanations use existing draft/source correspondence.
4. **Output: resolved repair; consumer: next research stage.** Judge returned evidence against the repair condition. Continue for a concrete remaining defect and authorized resolving operation using existing task context; PROSE governs public expression.

## Operational plans

1. **Output: executable plan; consumer: responsible owners.** Specify actions, inputs, handoffs, cooperation, update triggers and completion conditions within adopted time/resources. Derive acceptance from the requested target, retaining level and baseline comparison internally. Preserve required frequencies and routines; add records, checks or meetings for an actual decision, recovery need or user requirement.
